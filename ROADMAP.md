# Public Institutional Roadmap

**Status:** The institutional destination is FROZEN. Phase sequencing and gate details are BASELINE and must evolve through evidence.

The detailed implementation sprints in `docs/roadmap/sprints.md` describe near-term build order. This roadmap describes the longer institutional progression: how product capability, evidence, economics, stewardship, institutional interoperability, and governance mature together.

## Roadmap rules

- Advancement is gate-based, not calendar-based.
- Product utility precedes data-network, research, marketplace, enterprise, or governance expansion.
- Personal rights do not depend on progression, payment, contribution, provider enrollment, enterprise distribution, or secondary-use consent.
- Institutional systems may be important sources, destinations, and partners; they do not become the automatic owner of Chronicle meaning, product authority, permissions, incentives, or governance.
- Standards, profiles, implementation guides, mappings, providers, connectors, and vendors remain versioned and replaceable rather than becoming unquestioned canonical truth.
- Governance power is never unlocked solely by fundraising, enterprise revenue, provider status, token issuance, user count, or contributor volume.
- Every phase must publish its evidence, unresolved risks, responsible stewards, conflicts, dependencies, and rollback conditions.
- A later phase may be paused or reversed when safety, legitimacy, sustainability, provider neutrality, personal utility, or constitutional compliance deteriorates.
- Decentralization transfers authority; it does not remove accountability.

## Phase 0 — Constitutional and open-source foundations

**Purpose:** Make the repository the source of truth and establish the boundaries future implementations cannot silently erase.

**Product and data**

- Frozen product, architecture, gameplay, lore, and incentive foundations
- Synthetic-data-only public contribution workflows
- Explicit separation of open code and private production health data
- Versioned schemas, provenance, consent, receipt, export, and deletion directions
- Explicit consumer-first and provider-independent product center
- Explicit separation between exchange standards, source assertions, versioned mappings, and canonical Living Chronicle meaning
- Future provider, EHR, payer, laboratory, pharmacy, device, exchange, clinical, and enterprise integrations treated as gated adapter and partnership work rather than assumed core dependencies

**Organization**

- Maintainer-led execution
- Public pull requests, decision records, contribution policy, and review requirements
- Named specialist-review domains
- Initial succession and key-person-risk register
- Initial founder-subsidy and economic-dependency register using reviewed public categories without exposing private financial source records
- A public feedback-to-governed-work baseline linking issues, prioritization, implementation, validation, and outcome review
- A funding and sponsorship baseline defining acceptable support, prohibited benefits, conflicts, public reporting, concentration, dependency, correction, termination, and unresolved operating gates before a fundraising surface becomes live
- A proposed consumer-first and institutional-interoperability workstream defining provider, connector, standards, funding, evidence, replacement, and future enterprise-partnership gates before a material relationship becomes operational

**Governance unlock**

- Public visibility into decisions
- Contributor proposal and review rights
- Structured public-safe issue intake and an inspectable work ledger
- Non-binding community signals with maintainer rationale
- Public visibility into material funding, provider, connector, enterprise, and institutional relationships and conflicts without exposing protected source records
- No broad community control over safety-critical or constitutional systems yet

**Exit gate**

- Core foundations are internally consistent
- Repository controls meet minimum viable validation
- Material decisions no longer live only in private notes or chat
- The progressive-decentralization mandate is accepted as an architectural constraint
- The consumer-first and provider-independent boundary is accepted, revised, or explicitly declined rather than left to future procurement or sponsor pressure
- Funding cannot purchase player rights, private-data access, Chronicle schema authority, provider placement, connector rank, product or research authority, roadmap control, governance power, favorable findings, or exemption from ordinary review
- Material funding, sponsor, provider, connector, vendor-credit, related-party, concentration, distribution, source-reach, and dependency relationships have a public institutional record shape and explicit private-source boundary
- Future connector and institutional-partnership work has named evidence requirements, specialist holdpoints, provider-replacement behavior, and rollback conditions
- Any unresolved entity, custody, accounting, tax, compensation, treasury, payment, contracting, procurement, interoperability, clinical, and operational financial gates are named rather than implied to be complete
- Founder-provided labor, accounts, services, guarantees, and critical economic dependencies have an initial public-safe inventory and replacement owner

## Phase 1 — Useful private product

**Purpose:** Prove that the product creates meaningful personal value before asking people to support research, markets, enterprise relationships, or governance.

**Product and data**

- A usable private Living Chronicle
- Manual and assisted capture with player confirmation
- Understandable provenance, source class, correction, conflict, export, and deletion
- First complete gameplay and personal-value loops
- Essential non-AI and accessibility fallbacks
- A complete personal-value path without provider, EHR, payer, employer, researcher, sponsor, or enterprise enrollment
- Usable manual, import, export, correction, and deletion behavior when an institutional connector is unavailable

**Organization**

- A small accountable operating team
- Transparent roadmap ownership
- Documented release, incident, and escalation authority
- At least two people capable of maintaining every critical repository and release function
- Material operating support, provider, connector, and enterprise relationships remain publicly attributable, conflict-reviewed, and replaceable

**Governance unlock**

- Structured player feedback
- Public issue and proposal pathways
- Safe in-product creation or linking of public work items after classification and minimization
- Typed signals for affectedness, reproduction, priority preference, validation, contribution, and domain evidence
- Non-binding player and contributor advisory sessions
- Versioned advisory priority assessments
- Published reasons for material deferrals, declines, and overrides

**Exit gate**

- Meaningful retention and utility evidence
- Export and deletion reliability
- Acceptable consent comprehension
- No unresolved critical safety or security blocker
- No founder-only operational credential or undocumented recovery path
- Public feedback can be routed without exposing protected health, account, security, conduct, provider, interoperability, or third-party information
- Personal utility is demonstrated without making institutional enrollment a prerequisite
- No operating sponsor, grant, provider, connector, or enterprise relationship can silently weaken core rights, product evidence, publication independence, canonical Chronicle meaning, or provider replacement
- The first AS-0012 evidence review records whether consumer-first sequencing remains supported, narrowed, or uncertain

## Phase 2 — Evidence and trust

**Purpose:** Demonstrate that Calypso’s Promise can operate responsibly with real people and increasingly sensitive workflows.

**Product and data**

- Private beta with measured reliability
- Security, privacy, clinical-safety, accessibility, interoperability, and AI-governance controls
- Player-visible permissions and access receipts
- Auditable provider, connector, mapping, and data-flow boundaries
- Versioned source capabilities, implementation guides, terminology, mappings, conformance evidence, semantic loss, conflicts, and correction behavior
- Provider-facing or provider-integrated surfaces independently validated where risk warrants it
- Independent validation where risk warrants it

**Organization**

- Formal maintainer and specialist-review criteria
- Clinical-informatics and interoperability review responsibilities
- Conflict disclosures
- Incident transparency rules
- Public funding and expenditure reporting implementing Decision 0008 when funding is active
- Financial, provider, connector, source-authority, distribution, conflict, concentration, continuity, source-loss, correction, and funded-outcome controls
- Contributor onboarding and removal procedures

**Governance unlock**

- Player advisory body
- Contributor council
- Specialist councils for bounded domains
- Delegated authority over low-risk content, documentation, community, tooling, and selected triage decisions
- Council participation in evidence validation and priority review under published charters
- Appeals to maintainers for delegated decisions

**Exit gate**

- Sustained evidence of safe operation
- Multiple active maintainers with demonstrated judgment
- Representative player participation
- Council charters, term limits, recusals, and removal procedures
- Public financial controls and an independently reviewable treasury record
- Active funding and institutional relationships, if any, reconcile support, purpose, restrictions, approved benefits, work, expenditure where applicable, standards, mappings, findings, incidents, outcomes, conflicts, concentration, correction, exit readiness, and residual obligations
- Provider or standards conformance is not represented as proof of clinical safety, semantic completeness, or personal value
- At least one bounded feedback-prioritization pilot has published participation, concentration, decisions, outcomes, failures, and corrections

## Phase 3 — Community stewardship

**Purpose:** Move from founder-centered execution to a resilient multi-stakeholder operating institution.

**Product and data**

- Stable personal-value loops
- Mature provider-independent connector, mapping, and data-quality systems
- Portable APIs, schemas, source records, mapping history, and exports
- Replaceable infrastructure, AI-provider, EHR, exchange, terminology, and institutional-partner boundaries
- Public capability and limitation reporting

**Organization**

- Distributed release and operational authority
- Documented service-owner and connector-owner rotation
- Maintainer succession pipeline
- Community moderation and dispute process
- Stewardship of brand, domains, signing keys, critical accounts, connector credentials, and institutional relationships under written controls

**Governance unlock**

- Binding delegated authority for defined non-constitutional domains
- A defined percentage of eligible roadmap or grant capacity controlled through a chartered community process
- Explicit maintenance, safety, constitutional, and accountable executive capacity reserves
- Elected or selected council seats under public criteria
- Revocable delegation
- Transparent voting, priority assessments, override records, rationale, and measured outcomes
- A formal petition process for constitutional or roadmap review

**Exit gate**

- No critical function depends on one person
- At least one successful leadership or maintainer transition
- Tested disaster recovery and emergency succession
- Independent review of capture, conflict, provider, enterprise, and representation risks
- Demonstrated ability to replace a major vendor, provider connector, implementation guide, or institutional counterparty without abandoning records, rights, personal utility, or the mission
- At least one material connector or mapping migration has been exercised and reconciled
- Binding roadmap-capacity governance has completed at least one review cycle without weakening player rights or safety controls

## Phase 4 — Research Commons and public-good infrastructure

**Purpose:** Enable collective learning without converting the product into a data-extraction system.

**Product and data**

- Separately authorized research workflows
- Secure analysis environments
- Study-specific purpose, access, retention, and result-return rules
- Participant-readable receipts and withdrawal behavior
- Public-benefit and compensated opportunities clearly distinguished
- Research data models, standards, and institutional systems remain purpose-specific derivatives and destinations rather than canonical Chronicle authority
- A clinical institution, research institution, provider, or funder receives no broader access merely because it supplies another source, connector, infrastructure, distribution, or support

**Organization**

- Research governance charter
- Qualified scientific, privacy, security, interoperability, and participant representation
- Study review, adverse-event, publication, mapping, and conflict policies
- Protected public-good funding accounting

**Governance unlock**

- Participant representation in research-policy decisions
- Research council authority within constitutional limits
- Community challenge and appeal pathways
- Public reporting on approved, rejected, paused, and completed studies

**Exit gate**

- Personal utility remains independent from research participation
- Consent comprehension and withdrawal work in practice
- Research access can be audited and revoked
- Results and public benefit are reported
- Research funding cannot purchase connector, source, provider, or roadmap priority
- No research constituency can override player rights

## Phase 5 — Sustainable economics and shared value

**Purpose:** Sustain the institution and compensate legitimate value creation without making surveillance, enterprise dependency, provider placement, or speculation the business model.

Phase 5 operationalizes and matures Decision 0008. It does not originate or weaken the rights, anti-capture, conflict, public-ledger, provider-neutrality, consumer-first, private-source, correction, termination, or concentration rules accepted in Phase 0.

**Product and economics**

- Transparent hosted-service and donation economics
- Mature implementation of Decision 0008 across funding, expenditure, provider, connector, affiliate, enterprise, compensation, and public-good relationships
- Hosted, implementation, support, B2B, and B2B2C economics compatible with the complete meaningfully free personal product
- Separately authorized compensated opportunities
- Clear fee, subsidy, enterprise-support, and public-good funding policies
- Separate decisions for provider placement, connector ranking, referral, affiliate, procurement, distribution, and enterprise incentives
- Financial reserves and runway targets
- Compensation rules for contributors, reviewers, participants, and operators where appropriate

**Organization**

- Treasury policy
- Budget approval and reporting
- Related-party transaction controls
- Independent financial review
- Protected assets and mission-restricted funds where legally available

**Governance unlock**

- Participatory budget input
- Bounded community allocation pools
- Treasury oversight with expertise, affected-party representation, and conflict controls
- Public rationale for fees, reserves, compensation, enterprise relationships, and public-good allocations

**Exit gate**

- Operations can continue without founder subsidy
- Revenue, donor, provider, enterprise, distribution, source-reach, and critical-integration concentration risks are bounded
- Compensation, investment, procurement, or enterprise revenue does not purchase constitutional, product, clinical, research, or governance authority
- Personal-core utility does not depend on renewal by one institutional counterparty
- Public-good commitments are measurable
- Treasury access and recovery do not depend on one person

## Phase 6 — Constitutional governance

**Purpose:** Ratify a mature weighted-governance system after the institution has enough evidence and diversity to govern consequential decisions responsibly.

**Required design properties**

- Decision classes with distinct authority and quorum rules
- Representation of affected people
- Recognition of sustained contribution and demonstrated competence
- Delegation, revocation, term limits, and rotation
- Capital, donor, provider, enterprise-customer, and market-distribution weight caps
- Anti-Sybil and identity-privacy safeguards
- Conflict disclosure and recusal
- Minority protections
- Appeals and judicial or ombuds function
- Emergency powers with narrow scope, audit, and automatic sunset
- Constitutional amendment thresholds higher than ordinary policy thresholds

**Governance unlock**

- Binding constitutional proposals and votes
- Selection and removal of major stewards
- Ratification of treasury and institutional strategy
- Community authority to replace governance mechanisms that fail

**Exit gate**

- The model survives simulations, tabletop exercises, and adversarial review
- Ordinary participants can understand the process
- No single constituency, including capital, donors, providers, enterprise customers, vendors, researchers, or contributors, can unilaterally capture constitutional control
- Safety-critical expertise cannot be bypassed by popularity alone
- The model has completed at least one limited real-world governance cycle before broader authority transfers

## Phase 7 — Founder-independent institution

**Purpose:** Complete the transfer from founder-dependent project to durable constitutional institution.

**Organization**

- Founder operational authority is optional rather than required
- Release, infrastructure, connector, institutional-relationship, treasury, legal, brand, archive, and emergency powers are distributed under public rules
- All founder-reserved powers have explicit scope, justification, and sunset status
- Removal, replacement, and incapacity procedures apply to every office
- Institutional knowledge is maintained in public or appropriately protected archives

**Governance unlock**

- The community can appoint, supervise, and remove executive stewards
- The founder may participate under the same published rules as comparable stewards
- Constitutional continuity does not depend on personal relationships or informal permission

**Exit gate**

- A founder-absence exercise succeeds
- Critical operations continue through a leadership transition
- The institution can defend the mission against founder, funder, provider, enterprise customer, vendor, council, or majority capture
- Players retain their rights during organizational change
- The project can fork, migrate, merge, or wind down through legitimate procedures

## Phase 8 — Hundred-Year stewardship

**Purpose:** Maintain the mission across generations, technologies, standards, care systems, legal environments, scientific change, and institutional counterparties.

**Long-horizon systems**

- Periodic constitutional review
- Intergenerational leadership development
- Durable archives and provenance
- Technology, standards, mapping, provider, connector, and institutional-partner migration exercises
- Long-term treasury or endowment strategy
- Legacy, delegated, and abandoned-account stewardship
- Sunset, fork, merger, and dissolution procedures
- Periodic proof that the institution still creates net public and personal benefit

**Continuing gate**

This phase never permanently completes. The institution must repeatedly demonstrate that it remains useful, legitimate, solvent, correctable, provider-independent, portable, and aligned with the people it exists to serve.

## Feedback-to-governed-work authority path

Decision 0006 and `docs/governance/feedback-to-governed-work.md` define the operational path from user friction to progressively governed work.

The path is intentionally asymmetric:

- public visibility and contribution begin early
- advisory signals begin before binding votes
- low-risk delegated domains unlock before strategic, economic, provider, enterprise, research, or constitutional authority
- only a bounded share of eligible capacity transfers at first
- maintenance, safety, constitutional, and accountable executive reserves remain explicit
- success in one decision class does not grant authority over every other class
- every mechanism remains subject to outcome review, appeal, sunset, and rollback

This allows ordinary product refinement to teach and test governance without making user support, funding, provider status, data access, or enterprise revenue a pretext for premature control.

The following work is required before Phase 6:

1. Recover and catalogue the original HealthDAO, CureDAO, and Calypso’s Promise governance notes.
2. Model decision classes and affected constituencies.
3. Define the feedback-work-item, typed-signal, evidence, priority-assessment, override, contribution, and outcome contracts.
4. Design and test deterministic priority dimensions, scales, weights, thresholds, tie-breakers, and missing-data behavior against synthetic and bounded real backlogs.
5. Compare one-person-one-vote, contribution-weighted, reputation-weighted, expertise-qualified, delegated, bicameral, quadratic, conviction, and hybrid systems.
6. Design privacy-preserving identity and anti-Sybil options.
7. Define capital, donor, provider, enterprise-customer, and distribution weight caps and protections.
8. Define founder-reserved powers, their justifications, and measurable sunsets.
9. Simulate capture, apathy, plutocracy, contributor oligarchy, provider or enterprise capture, popularity contests, expertise capture, collusion, brigading, AI-generated consensus, and emergency abuse.
10. Establish appeal, ombuds, dispute, and constitutional-review functions.
11. Design treasury, compensation, enterprise-economics, and public-good allocation governance.
12. Pilot bounded decisions and roadmap-capacity allocations before transferring constitutional authority.
