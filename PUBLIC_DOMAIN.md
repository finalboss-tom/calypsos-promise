# Public-Domain Commons Declaration

**Status:** Constitutional direction accepted; legal implementation and contributor migration remain pending qualified review.

Calypso’s Promise is built from a story inherited by everyone and is intended to become an inheritance for everyone.

The project’s original software, specifications, schemas, documentation, lore, educational content, product designs, synthetic fixtures, and governance materials are to be dedicated to the public domain to the fullest extent permitted by law. Where a jurisdiction does not recognize a complete waiver, the selected legal instrument must provide an unconditional fallback permission consistent with public-domain use.

This is not merely a distribution preference. It is an institutional constraint.

> The source story belongs to everyone. The tools built from it should belong to everyone too.

## What the commons includes

Subject to a file-level exception and completion of the legal migration, the public-domain commitment covers original project materials including:

- software written for Calypso’s Promise
- public interfaces, schemas, ontologies, and protocol specifications
- the Ogygia world, original lore, characters, quests, dialogue, and educational structures
- product and interaction designs
- documentation, governance records, policies, and institutional models
- public website copy and campaign materials
- synthetic examples, fixtures, and demonstration data
- research methods and public-good infrastructure created by the project when no separate duty requires another treatment

Reuse may be commercial or noncommercial. Forks may be modified, combined, translated, sold, hosted, or abandoned without asking permission from the canonical project.

## What the commons never includes automatically

### A person’s information

Personal health data, user content, account records, private correspondence, consent records, access receipts, credentials, and production telemetry are not public-domain project assets.

> The project may be public. The person is not a public resource.

A person’s information remains governed by the Product Constitution, the Seven Laws, applicable law, and the permissions that person actually granted.

### Third-party material

The public-domain declaration cannot waive rights the project does not own. Dependencies, fonts, photographs, music, historical editions, film material, research papers, standards, data sets, and contributed assets may have separate terms. Their notices must remain intact.

### Secrets and protected information

Security reports, private keys, credentials, protected disclosures, personnel information, and legally restricted material are not made public merely because the project is.

### Contributions not yet dedicated

A Developer Certificate of Origin establishes the contributor’s right to submit work; it does not by itself dedicate that work to the public domain. Contribution workflows must add an explicit public-domain dedication before external contributions are accepted at scale.

## Source material and new work

Ancient source material that is already free of known copyright restrictions should be identified as such. The project must not claim to have created or dedicated Homeric source material that already belongs to the public.

New Calypso’s Promise material should use a legally operative dedication rather than merely displaying a Public Domain Mark. The Public Domain Mark labels material already in the worldwide public domain; it does not change a new work’s legal status.

## Legal implementation direction

The exact instruments require qualified review, but the implementation should preserve the following outcome:

| Material | Baseline direction | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original software | Public-domain dedication with an unconditional fallback, with the Unlicense and CC0 evaluated alongside patent implications | Preserve unrestricted use while providing a fallback where waiver is ineffective |
| Original documentation, lore, art, schemas, and synthetic fixtures | CC0 1.0 Universal or an equivalent public-domain dedication | One consistent cross-media dedication with a global fallback |
| Ancient source texts and clearly preexisting public-domain works | Accurate source and public-domain-status notice | Do not falsely claim ownership or a new dedication |
| Third-party materials | Their actual license or permission | The project cannot give away another party’s rights |
| Personal or production data | No public-domain dedication | Individual control and privacy are constitutional requirements |

A canonical `LICENSE`, file notices, contributor certification, dependency audit, and migration record must be completed before the repository describes every current file as legally dedicated.

## Canonical does not mean owned

The project may identify a canonical repository, release, website, governance record, or deployment. “Canonical” is a provenance claim: it identifies where a version came from and which review process it passed.

It is not a claim that forks require permission.

Anyone may create a fork or adaptation. No one may truthfully claim that an unreviewed deployment received a security, privacy, clinical, governance, or release approval that it did not receive.

Canonical provenance should be established through:

- signed releases and checksums
- public release manifests
- documented maintainers and authority
- transparent review evidence
- reproducible builds where practical
- clear operator identity
- public incident and recall records

These controls protect truth and safety without enclosing the underlying work.

## No silent enclosure by the institution

The canonical organization must not later convert public-domain project assets into exclusive private property, condition access to the commons on payment, or use governance to revoke permissions already given to the public.

A hosted service may charge for labor, infrastructure, support, convenience, curation, safety operations, or other real services. It may not pretend those charges purchase ownership of public-domain foundations.

## Forks and commercial reuse

Public-domain status permits uses the canonical project may dislike, including proprietary services, incompatible forks, and commercial adaptations. That is a real and accepted tradeoff.

The project’s response to harmful or extractive forks should rely on better stewardship, transparent provenance, public criticism, interoperability, participant rights, community legitimacy, and applicable law—not a retroactive copyright enclosure.

## Relationship to the Odyssey attention cycle

Calypso’s Promise does not derive from, reproduce, or imply affiliation with any modern film adaptation. A contemporary adaptation may renew public attention around the ancient story, but its screenplay, performances, production design, branding, stills, footage, music, and promotional materials remain separate works.

The campaign position is simple:

> Hollywood did not give this story to us. The story survived because no generation could own its final meaning.

## Required migration work

Before the public-domain status is represented as legally complete across the repository:

1. Accept the public-domain commons decision record.
2. Retire the AGPL baseline proposal.
3. Withdraw the broad reserved-trademark proposal.
4. Select and publish the legal dedication instruments.
5. Add file-level notices and machine-readable metadata.
6. Audit all existing files for authorship and third-party material.
7. Add an explicit contributor dedication and update pull-request certification.
8. Document provenance and exceptions.
9. Obtain qualified legal review for copyright, patent, trademark, privacy, and contributor implications.
10. Publish a migration report stating exactly which materials are covered.

## Core rule

> The commons includes the world, tools, knowledge, and institutional design. It never includes a person without that person’s specific and meaningful permission.
