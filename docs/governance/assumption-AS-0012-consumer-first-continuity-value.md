## Statement

A consumer-first, provider-independent Living Chronicle can create enough recurring personal value and longitudinal continuity to justify beginning without clinic, EHR, payer, employer, research, or enterprise adoption as a prerequisite, while versioned adapters and later bounded partnerships can add institutional data and collaboration without transferring canonical product authority.

## Constitutional boundary

Personal value first, the meaningfully free product, individual control, purpose-specific permission, provider replaceability, export, deletion, correction, accessibility, and non-punitive refusal remain binding even if this hypothesis is weakened.

Failure would require improving, narrowing, replacing, or legitimately winding down the consumer-first implementation strategy. It would not justify coercive disclosure, hidden secondary use, enterprise ownership of the Chronicle, purchased provider placement, or surrender of player rights.

## Evidence for

- The person is the only participant who persists across providers, devices, payers, geography, and life stages.
- The repository already defines a provider-independent Chronicle, source provenance, correction, conflict, export, deletion, purpose-specific authority, and personal-value-first gameplay.
- FHIR, US Core, USCDI, and related standards create valuable exchange contracts while explicitly relying on profiles, versions, terminologies, mappings, and implementation guides for particular use cases.
- Government and standards sources continue to describe variation in implementation, cost, workflow, governance, trust, registration, documentation, and contractual access even as technical interoperability improves.
- Google Health’s limited adoption did not become widespread daily use, which supports the need for recurring utility rather than passive aggregation alone.
- Institutional systems naturally cover bounded workflows and encounters rather than the complete lived sequence.

## Evidence against or missing

- Calypso’s Promise has no private product, real player, retention, usefulness, Chronicle-quality, connector, clinical-collaboration, acquisition-cost, willingness-to-pay, or longitudinal-outcome evidence.
- Google Health and Microsoft HealthVault demonstrate that consumer control and portability do not automatically produce adoption.
- People may not want to maintain a longitudinal record even when the product is well designed.
- Clinical organizations may remain the most practical channel for reaching people, validating data, or sustaining operations in some contexts.
- Person-entered information can be incomplete, inaccurate, difficult to normalize, or biased by recall and access.
- A separate internal ontology can become another silo if external mappings, standards participation, exports, and specialist review are weak.
- Enterprise funding or distribution could materially accelerate public benefit without necessarily causing capture.
- The administrative burden of maintaining many adapters may exceed the project’s capacity.

## Competing explanations

- Lack of adoption may result primarily from weak product usefulness rather than market center.
- Institutional integration may fail because of execution, trust, regulation, procurement, or switching cost rather than schema fragmentation.
- Consumer utility and enterprise distribution may be complements rather than competing strategies.
- A standards-native internal model may be sufficient if carefully profiled and extended.
- A narrower disease, device, or care-pathway focus may create more value than a broad longitudinal Chronicle.
- The narrative game may be less important than direct data access, summaries, and care preparation.

## Rights and safety implications

A consumer-first product can strengthen control and portability, but it can also:

- overstate the reliability of person-entered information;
- present incomplete records as clinically complete;
- create unsafe self-interpretation;
- exclude people with limited time, literacy, access, disability support, or technical confidence;
- increase privacy and security risk by aggregating sources;
- create family or third-party inference risk; or
- encourage avoidance of appropriate clinical care.

The product must preserve source identity, uncertainty, direct language, accessible alternatives, clinical boundaries, correction, and explicit statements that it is not a substitute for care.

## Invalidation or weakening conditions

Weaken or invalidate reliance when one or more of these persist after reasonable bounded iterations:

- the complete personal-value loop does not produce meaningful voluntary return or perceived utility;
- useful participation depends primarily on a provider, employer, payer, researcher, or enterprise mandate;
- people cannot understand or maintain source, correction, and uncertainty distinctions;
- Chronicle quality remains too incomplete or unreliable for the claimed personal uses;
- standards-based imports repeatedly fail to create timely personal value;
- maintaining a separate ontology creates greater semantic loss or isolation than a standards-native alternative;
- provider partnerships consistently improve access and outcomes without materially threatening rights, neutrality, or portability, requiring the hypothesis to be narrowed;
- enterprise revenue repeatedly becomes necessary to preserve the complete personal product;
- provider-specific dependencies cannot be replaced at reasonable cost; or
- the strategy systematically excludes people most affected by fragmented records.

No single disappointing metric automatically invalidates the thesis. Review must examine product quality, population, use case, access, source availability, and alternative explanations.

## Containment or fallback

If weakened:

- narrow the initial population, use case, source set, or claimed value;
- improve direct-mode utility, capture, reconciliation, and visit preparation;
- increase clinical and interoperability co-design without transferring canonical authority;
- adopt more standards-native internal structures where evidence supports them;
- use bounded B2B2C distribution or institutional collaboration under Decision 0010;
- fund connector maintenance and migration rather than hiding gaps;
- preserve manual, import, export, correction, and deletion paths;
- prevent new provider or sponsor exclusivity while evidence is unresolved; and
- publish the limitation and revised hypothesis.

If invalidated, replace the market and product sequencing through an explicit decision while preserving the constitutional rights floor and portable records.

## Evidence plan

Before representing the hypothesis as supported, gather evidence on:

- time to first useful Chronicle outcome;
- seven-day and thirty-day meaningful return;
- self-reported utility by use case;
- ability to prepare for and communicate during care;
- import completion and failure rates by source;
- mapping coverage, semantic loss, and unresolved conflict;
- source and uncertainty comprehension;
- correction, export, and deletion reliability;
- accessibility and direct-mode success;
- provider comprehension and usefulness of person-authorized summaries;
- acquisition and support cost without enterprise distribution;
- connector maintenance and replacement cost;
- roadmap attention by funded versus unfunded integrations;
- source, provider, and revenue concentration; and
- differential effects across people with fragmented care, disability, chronic illness, limited access, and low technical confidence.

## Reviewer and independence limitations

The founding steward originated the product thesis and may benefit reputationally, institutionally, or economically from its acceptance. Founding-steward review is not independent validation.

Relevant future review should include people with experience in:

- clinical informatics and terminology;
- patient and caregiver record use;
- EHR and health-system operations;
- interoperability implementation;
- privacy and security;
- accessibility and health literacy;
- consumer product adoption;
- public-interest health technology; and
- economics and institutional partnerships.

A provider, sponsor, funded connector implementer, or enterprise counterparty cannot be the sole evaluator of this hypothesis.
