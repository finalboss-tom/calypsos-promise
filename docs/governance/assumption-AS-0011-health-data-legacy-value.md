# AS-0011 — A provenance-rich Living Chronicle can retain legitimate value after death

## Statement

A high-quality, provenance-rich longitudinal Living Chronicle can retain enough legitimate familial, scientific, historical, memorial, or institutional value after its creator dies to justify an optional person-controlled legacy and succession capability, provided the continuing privacy, legal, security, third-party, preservation, governance, and operational costs can be bounded honestly.

## Constitutional boundary

The hypothesis does not determine the person’s rights.

Even if the claim is strongly supported:

- death does not make a Chronicle public, abandoned, research-ready, inheritable by default, or platform property;
- no family member, executor, steward, researcher, archive, provider, funder, or institution receives blanket authority;
- a person may direct deletion, private preservation, narrow family disclosure, restricted research, embargoed archive, or no post-mortem contribution;
- broader legacy permission may not produce progression, payment, superior service, research eligibility, governance power, or other advantage; and
- purpose-specific permission, third-party protection, specialist review, challenge, correction, restoration, and truthful uncertainty remain required.

If the hypothesis weakens or fails, the project must narrow, suspend, replace, or retire the retention and succession mechanism rather than coercing preservation or secondary use.

## Intended decision use

This assumption may guide:

- Decision 0009 and the health-data legacy architecture;
- prioritization of a future Legacy Directive and succession workstream;
- preservation, export, family-health, research, archive, and deletion design;
- long-term custody, migration, shutdown, and abandoned-account planning;
- research and archival evidence thresholds;
- cost, retention, subsidy, and sustainability decisions; and
- periodic review of whether continued storage creates net personal or public benefit.

It may not be used as proof that any individual Chronicle is scientifically useful, complete, representative, lawfully transferable, safe to disclose, or worth a fixed amount of money.

## Evidence for

- The Living Chronicle architecture preserves source identity, provenance, temporal context, correction, conflict, and derived relationships, which are prerequisites for later interpretation rather than isolated health-data fragments.
- Longitudinal information can reveal sequence, change, recurrence, treatment context, uncertainty, and life-course patterns that a single observation cannot.
- Family-health information can remain relevant to descendants and relatives, particularly where hereditary, environmental, or shared-context questions exist.
- Rare conditions, unusual treatment journeys, long-latency outcomes, and historical care practices may be difficult to study without long-duration records.
- The frozen Vision already treats legacy wishes, durable archives, abandoned-account stewardship, and intergenerational succession as long-horizon design responsibilities.
- Official U.S. health-privacy guidance recognizes that information about a deceased person can remain protected and that legally authorized representatives may retain a role, demonstrating that death does not make health information irrelevant or ungoverned.

## Evidence against or missing

- Calypso’s Promise has no production Living Chronicle, post-mortem directive, family derivative, research contribution, archive, claimant workflow, or longitudinal outcome evidence.
- A Chronicle may be incomplete, inaccurate, biased, unrepresentative, selectively recorded, or shaped by changing variables, devices, clinical practices, social conditions, and missing context.
- Provenance improves interpretability but does not guarantee causal validity, diagnostic truth, research quality, or historical representativeness.
- Family usefulness may be overstated when genetic, environmental, reproductive, social, and clinical factors are uncertain or sensitive.
- The record may expose living relatives, correspondents, caregivers, minors, abuse survivors, or other third parties who did not create the directive.
- Death, incapacity, estate, digital-assets, health-privacy, contract, source-rights, research, and archive law vary by jurisdiction and operating model.
- Long-term encryption, key recovery, migration, format preservation, identity verification, document verification, dispute handling, and deletion are expensive and failure-prone.
- Retaining data for hypothetical future value creates continuing breach, inference, re-identification, family-conflict, institutional-capture, and provider-dependency risks.
- Research and archive demand may create pressure to overstate value, narrow privacy, extend retention, or reinterpret vague preferences.
- The platform may not survive long enough to activate the directive, or a successor institution may be unable or unwilling to preserve the Promise.
- No evidence yet establishes that people want the capability, understand it, will keep instructions current, or trust the institution to operate it.

## Competing explanations

- The apparent value may come primarily from a small subset of unusually complete, rare, or clinically documented records rather than ordinary Chronicles.
- Family value may be satisfied better through a minimized family-history artifact created during life than through post-mortem succession.
- Research value may depend more on standardized cohort design, clinical validation, and population sampling than on broad retention of individual consumer records.
- Historical value may be better served by voluntary archival projects with specialist governance rather than a general product capability.
- People may prefer complete deletion or private export because institutional long-term custody creates more risk than value.
- Future interoperability may make a Calypso-specific succession capability unnecessary if portable directives and personal custody become standard.

## Affected groups

- people creating Living Chronicles;
- people with serious, rare, chronic, stigmatized, genetic, reproductive, psychiatric, substance-use, disability, injury, or abuse-related information;
- family members, genetic relatives, descendants, partners, caregivers, dependents, minors, correspondents, and other third-party subjects;
- named Legacy Stewards and contingent stewards;
- executors, administrators, guardians, conservators, trustees, agents, courts, and other fiduciary or legal actors;
- clinicians, genetic counselors, researchers, research participants, archives, historians, and public-interest institutions;
- operators, security teams, identity and document verifiers, custodians, providers, insurers, and successor institutions;
- people who cannot afford counsel, use one identity method, or independently complete complex forms; and
- communities historically harmed by health-data extraction, surveillance, discrimination, family separation, eugenics, or research abuse.

## Rights and safety implications

Potential benefits include preserving person intent, supporting relatives, enabling carefully governed longitudinal evidence, avoiding abandonment, and creating a legitimate deletion or archive path.

Potential harms include irreversible disclosure, familial inference, identity fraud, coercion, false death activation, contested deletion, public exposure, research exploitation, AI impersonation, indefinite retention, key loss, provider failure, and transfer to a captured successor institution.

Aggregate scientific or historical benefit cannot erase concentrated harm to one person, family, minority, survivor, or vulnerable group.

## Dependencies

- a useful and trustworthy Living Chronicle;
- source, provenance, correction, conflict, export, deletion, and third-party subject contracts;
- direct person control and accessible Legacy Directive execution;
- identity, event, document, role, and authority verification;
- House of Keys purpose-specific permission;
- security, encryption, key management, recovery, audit, incident, and deletion controls;
- estate, probate, fiduciary, incapacity, digital-assets, health-privacy, electronic-signature, contract, research, archive, and cross-border review;
- family-health derivative and genetic-risk governance;
- research and archive recipient governance;
- provider portability and institutional succession;
- cost, subsidy, retention, shutdown, and abandoned-account policy;
- challenge, appeal, containment, correction, restoration, and revalidation; and
- representative participant research before live design claims.

## Invalidation or weakening conditions

Confidence should be weakened, narrowed, or invalidated when:

- prospective users consistently prefer deletion or personal export and reject institutional custody after understanding the tradeoffs;
- retained Chronicles rarely provide reliable familial, research, historical, or memorial value beyond safer alternatives;
- provenance, completeness, standardization, or bias limitations make later use materially misleading;
- third-party and familial exposure cannot be bounded at acceptable cost;
- death, incapacity, identity, document, and authority verification produce unacceptable fraud, exclusion, delay, or false activation;
- legal variation prevents an understandable and governable capability;
- secure long-term custody, migration, deletion, and provider replacement are not sustainable;
- post-mortem research or archive demand repeatedly pressures the project to weaken privacy, specificity, withdrawal, or publication controls;
- the institution cannot preserve directives and rights through founder, provider, entity, or governance transition;
- continuation costs require surveillance, commercialization, paywalls over essential rights, or indefinite founder subsidy; or
- measurable harms and residual risk exceed the demonstrated personal and public benefit.

## Containment and fallback

If the assumption weakens:

- keep the capability non-operational or reduce it to exportable instructions;
- prefer person-controlled external custody or a minimized family-history artifact;
- narrow supported jurisdictions, roles, actions, data categories, recipients, or retention periods;
- prohibit public archives, identifiable research, full exports, or other high-risk pathways;
- pause new directives or activations while preserving revocation, export, deletion, challenge, and existing obligations;
- migrate to a qualified successor only under reviewed authority;
- delete or return data where required and technically possible;
- publish a reviewed institutional limitation rather than inflate the value claim; and
- supersede Decision 0009 or the architecture through a public decision record without weakening the person’s rights floor.

## Evidence plan

Before representing the assumption as supported, gather evidence from:

- interviews and comprehension studies with prospective users, caregivers, executors, privacy advocates, patient communities, researchers, archivists, and people who prefer deletion;
- analysis of which Chronicle attributes create valid longitudinal utility and which create misleading completeness;
- synthetic family-health, research, archive, deletion, and contested-authority scenarios;
- specialist review of familial, genetic, third-party, legal, security, accessibility, research, and archival risks;
- cost and continuity models for protected custody, key recovery, migration, shutdown, and abandoned accounts;
- comparisons among full institutional custody, personal export, external escrow, minimized derivatives, and deletion;
- bounded prototypes that do not activate legal or data-release claims; and
- explicit contradictory evidence, failure cases, and minority protections.
