# Open Marketing and Public-Relations Strategy

**Status:** BASELINE public operating strategy  
**Owner:** Founding steward during the current phase  
**Applies to:** Public communication, audience development, media relations, community growth, partnerships, campaigns, events, sponsorship communication, and paid promotion  
**Related documents:** `campaign-system.md`, `launch-kit.md`, `attention-window-2026-07.md`, `content-calendar-2026-07-27.md`, `founder-origin-essay.md`, `../../PUBLIC_DOMAIN.md`, `../../VISION.md`

## Purpose

Calypso’s Promise will develop its marketing and public-relations strategy in public.

The project already asks people to believe that software, institutional design, provenance, and public-good infrastructure can be built as a commons. Its communication system should operate by the same principle.

This document lays out:

- what the project is trying to achieve through communication
- which audiences it serves
- which stories it will tell
- which channels it will use
- how current events may be used without misrepresentation
- how media, creators, partners, and communities will be approached
- which tactics are prohibited
- what will be measured
- what will be published about results
- how failures, corrections, and strategic changes will remain visible

This is not a promise that every tactic will work. It is a promise that the reasoning, execution, evidence, and changes will be inspectable.

## The open-campaign thesis

Most organizations publish their polished message and conceal the machinery behind it.

Calypso’s Promise will expose the machinery wherever doing so does not violate privacy, safety, security, confidentiality, or another person’s legitimate rights.

The public should be able to follow:

1. what the project believed
2. what it attempted
3. why it selected a tactic
4. what it spent
5. what happened
6. what was learned
7. what changed as a result

The campaign itself becomes part of the project’s institutional memory.

This serves four purposes.

### Trust

People can compare public claims with the actual strategy and results behind them.

### Learning

Other public-interest and open projects can reuse what works and avoid repeated mistakes.

### Accountability

The project cannot quietly rewrite failed experiments or pretend that a later strategy was always the plan.

### Participation

Contributors can improve messaging, research audiences, challenge assumptions, produce assets, translate materials, identify risks, and propose experiments.

## Strategic objective

The marketing system exists to help the right people accurately understand the project and take an appropriate next step.

The order is:

1. earn attention without deception
2. convert attention into understanding
3. convert understanding into justified trust
4. convert trust into low-risk participation
5. convert participation into durable personal and public value

Reach without comprehension is not success.

Conversion without informed choice is not success.

Publicity that creates capabilities the product cannot yet support is not success.

## Primary goals

### 1. Establish accurate public understanding

A person should be able to explain that Calypso’s Promise is:

- an independent public-domain health project inspired by ancient mythology
- a narrative-driven path toward a person-controlled longitudinal health record
- currently an early public project with frozen foundations, schemas, governance, and synthetic examples rather than a completed consumer product
- designed around private personal information and open project infrastructure
- committed to progressive decentralization and credible founder independence

### 2. Establish founder and project provenance

The campaign should make the project’s actual evolution visible:

- ConstitutionDAO as the catalytic example
- HealthDAO as an early thesis
- the public 99designs work as evidence and experimentation
- CureDAO as a consequential collaboration and lesson
- the failure of premature decentralization and unclear authority
- the emergence of Calypso’s Promise and progressive decentralization
- the present public-domain commitment

### 3. Build the founding expedition

Attract people who may eventually become:

- players
- open-source contributors
- designers and storytellers
- health, privacy, security, accessibility, legal, research, governance, and economic reviewers
- public-good supporters
- educators and community organizers
- institutional partners

Joining the founding expedition is an expression of interest, not product enrollment, research participation, data consent, governance membership, investment, or donation.

### 4. Build a durable public record

Every important public campaign should leave behind:

- source material
- claims and supporting evidence
- dates
- versions
- channel adaptations
- outcome measures
- corrections
- lessons
- successor strategies

### 5. Prepare for responsible scale

The system should gradually develop:

- an owned audience
- a credible press record
- a discoverable knowledge base
- repeatable campaign operations
- privacy-respecting measurement
- partner and specialist relationships
- public funding transparency
- communication succession beyond the founder

## Explicit non-goals

The strategy is not designed to:

- make the founder famous as an end in itself
- maximize followers without regard to relevance or comprehension
- exploit controversy for outrage engagement
- imply affiliation with a modern adaptation of the Odyssey
- collect health data through marketing surfaces
- create speculative investment demand
- market planned capabilities as live
- manufacture a grassroots movement
- conceal paid relationships
- shame people into participation
- use fear about illness, mortality, or data exploitation to rush consent
- turn every audience into a customer
- protect the project from legitimate criticism

## Communication principles

### Truth before reach

Every material claim must be supportable. Uncertainty and current status should be stated where relevant.

### Understanding before conversion

The project should prefer a smaller audience that understands the promise over a larger audience attracted by a misleading simplification.

### Provenance before polish

Historical evidence, source artifacts, and decision records should remain accessible even when they are visually imperfect or strategically outdated.

### Personal value before collective extraction

Marketing should lead with the usefulness of the Living Chronicle, not research access, data markets, or institutional value.

### Cultural relevance without borrowed authority

The project may explain why a current cultural moment makes the story newly legible. It may not imply affiliation, endorsement, adaptation rights, or creative dependence on a modern production.

### Public project, private person

Open strategy and open infrastructure do not make a person’s health information, correspondence, identity, or participation history public.

### Invitation without manufactured urgency

A cultural moment can justify paying attention. It cannot justify rushing a health-data decision, donation, account creation, governance choice, or research consent.

### Corrections are assets

A visible correction is evidence of an accountable system, not a communications failure to conceal.

### Failure remains in the archive

Failed campaigns, weak assumptions, poor conversion, and strategic reversals should be documented rather than erased.

## Prohibited practices

Calypso’s Promise must not use:

- fake accounts or sockpuppets
- purchased followers, likes, comments, reviews, signatures, or community members
- undisclosed paid endorsements
- undisclosed employee, contractor, founder, or partner advocacy
- astroturfing
- fabricated testimonials
- fake scarcity or countdowns
- misleading waitlist numbers
- invented press interest
- deceptive headlines that contradict the destination page
- dark patterns in signup or unsubscribe flows
- health-condition targeting based on inferred or purchased profiles
- sensitive-data enrichment
- hidden tracking pixels on health-related campaign content
- session replay without a separately justified and disclosed decision
- scraping private communities for personal targeting
- harassment, brigading, dogpiling, or outrage mobilization
- manufactured conflict with critics or adjacent projects
- impersonation of journalists, researchers, institutions, communities, or film participants
- film stills, actor likenesses, protected adaptation art, copied key art, or confusingly similar promotional assets
- claims that public-domain status makes private health data public
- claims that a public-domain project has no canonical source or accountable stewards

## Message architecture

### Core promise

> Build your Living Chronicle. Improve your health. Keep the key.

### Institutional thesis

> Calypso’s Promise uses one of humanity’s oldest shared stories to build a shared institution for human health—beginning with useful personal tools and progressively transferring stewardship as the evidence and community mature.

### Public-domain thesis

> The story belongs to everyone. The original project materials are being prepared to belong to everyone too. The person’s private information is not part of the commons.

### Founder-story thesis

> The project did not appear because the Odyssey became culturally visible again. The renewed attention created a moment in which a project already years into its own odyssey could be understood.

### Failure thesis

> The failed versions remain visible because the lessons only matter when the evidence remains visible too.

### Product-status thesis

> The vision is expansive. The present implementation is early. The public can inspect both.

## Audience map

The project does not have one undifferentiated audience.

### Future players

**Primary question:** Will this help me understand my life without taking control away from me?  
**Needed evidence:** Plain product explanation, rights, synthetic prologue, current status, privacy model, accessibility, and stopping points.  
**Appropriate action:** Follow development or try a synthetic public experience when available.

### Builders, designers, and storytellers

**Primary question:** Is this coherent, contribution-ready, and worth my effort?  
**Needed evidence:** Repository quality, frozen foundations, contribution paths, decisions, validation, open briefs, and respectful review.  
**Appropriate action:** Inspect an issue, review a document, create an artifact, or contribute a focused change.

### Specialists

**Primary question:** Does this project recognize the limits of its own competence?  
**Needed evidence:** Explicit review gates, bounded claims, current status, unresolved questions, privacy and safety boundaries, and named areas requiring expertise.  
**Appropriate action:** Provide a bounded review rather than implied general endorsement.

### Journalists, analysts, and researchers

**Primary question:** Is there a real story, credible evidence, and honest access?  
**Needed evidence:** Founder genealogy, public records, current status, independent positioning, source links, correction process, and clear availability.  
**Appropriate action:** Review the press room, inspect primary sources, request an interview, or challenge a claim.

### Public-good supporters and institutions

**Primary question:** Can this become useful and sustainable without contradicting its mission?  
**Needed evidence:** Public roadmap, governance, funding boundaries, public-domain commitment, product-first sequence, impact thesis, and transparent use of funds.  
**Appropriate action:** Follow funding readiness, provide expertise, support a bounded public-good workstream, or wait until the relevant structure is established.

### Mythology, literature, film, and cultural communities

**Primary question:** Is this a thoughtful reinterpretation or a promotional imitation?  
**Needed evidence:** Direct engagement with the ancient source, the reinterpretation of Calypso, original visual language, public-domain literacy, and independence from modern adaptations.  
**Appropriate action:** Read, critique, retell, remix, or contribute to the public-domain world.

## Channel strategy

### Owned channels

Owned channels preserve context and institutional memory.

Priority surfaces:

- canonical website
- public repository
- founder essay and project journal
- public email updates
- status and correction pages
- public campaign strategy and log
- original long-form video or audio with transcripts

Owned channels are the source of truth. Social platforms distribute links and adapted explanations; they do not become the only surviving record.

### Shared and community channels

These include:

- GitHub discussions and issues
- relevant open-source communities
- mythology, literature, quantified-self, personal informatics, public-interest technology, privacy, governance, and digital-health communities
- founder social accounts
- contributor and partner accounts

Participation should adapt to each community’s norms. The project should contribute useful context rather than arrive only to promote itself.

### Earned media

Earned media is valuable when independent scrutiny improves public understanding.

Initial story angles include:

- a years-old public-domain health project meeting a newly visible ancient story
- a founder preserving failed designs and strategic mistakes as provenance
- a health-data project rejecting the idea that open infrastructure means open personal data
- progressive decentralization after direct experience with premature DAO design
- giving the project itself to the commons
- designing an institution around eventual founder independence
- using narrative to improve comprehension without concealing consent or evidence

### Partner channels

Potential partners may include:

- open-source foundations and communities
- digital-rights and privacy organizations
- patient and caregiver organizations
- personal-science and quantified-self communities
- universities and research groups
- public-interest technology networks
- mythology, literature, museum, library, and education organizations
- accessibility communities
- public-health and health-data standards communities

A partner’s audience should never be treated as transferable property. Joint communication should state the exact relationship and should not imply broader endorsement than actually exists.

### Paid distribution

Paid promotion is a later amplifier, not the source of legitimacy.

Paid activity should remain gated until:

- the destination page is accurate and useful
- analytics and privacy disclosures are live
- the call to action is low risk
- the project can respond to resulting interest
- claims have been reviewed
- the relevant funding and entity relationship is clear
- the campaign has organic comprehension evidence

Permitted early paid objectives may include:

- distribution of a public educational resource
- recruitment for open-source contribution
- awareness of the public-domain project
- donation campaigns only after the receiving entity and use-of-funds language are ready

Paid targeting must not use inferred health conditions, medical interests, crisis states, or sensitive personal profiles.

## Founder-led communication

The founder is currently the clearest carrier of the origin story, but founder-led does not mean founder-exclusive.

The founder’s initial responsibilities are:

- tell the personal history in first person
- distinguish memory from documented fact
- acknowledge mistakes without self-mythologizing
- explain current authority honestly
- link lessons to repository decisions
- invite scrutiny
- avoid representing future community views as already settled

The system should progressively develop additional voices:

- maintainers
- contributors
- specialist reviewers
- players when real users exist and freely choose to speak
- institutional partners within their actual scope
- future elected or delegated stewards

No testimonial, case study, player story, or contributor profile should be published without informed permission and an opportunity to review how it is represented.

## Public-relations strategy

### The role of PR

Public relations should create informed third-party scrutiny, not merely favorable coverage.

A good outcome may be:

- accurate coverage
- a difficult but fair interview
- a correction to an overbroad project claim
- discovery by a needed specialist
- a new contributor
- a useful public debate
- a durable source that future audiences can inspect

Positive sentiment is not the only valid outcome.

### Media preparation

Before proactive outreach, the project should publish:

- the founder origin essay
- current capability status
- public-domain status and migration language
- the health-data covenant
- the project genealogy
- original approved images
- founder biography
- facts with last-reviewed dates
- a correction contact
- source links for important claims

### Media tiers

#### Tier 1 — Directly relevant specialists

Writers and publications covering:

- digital health
- personal informatics
- health-data rights
- privacy and public-interest technology
- open source
- DAOs and governance
- mythology and literary reinterpretation

These audiences can test the substance of the project.

#### Tier 2 — Broader technology and culture

Writers covering:

- AI-era consumer products
- public-domain creativity
- future institutions
- founder stories
- cultural technology
- internet communities

#### Tier 3 — General-interest media

General-interest outreach becomes appropriate when there is a stronger visual artifact, usable prologue, meaningful community evidence, funding event, or product milestone.

### Pitching standard

Every pitch should:

- identify why that person’s work makes the story relevant
- state the project’s current status early
- include the independent relationship to current cultural events
- link primary sources
- avoid inflated exclusivity or urgency
- make the interview subject and available materials explicit
- disclose material conflicts and relationships
n- provide a correction contact

Mass generic pitching should be avoided. A small number of researched contacts is preferable to indiscriminate outreach.

### Exclusives and embargoes

An exclusive or embargo may be offered only when it improves reporting quality and does not create a false claim of scarcity.

The terms should be explicit:

- what information is being shared
- when it becomes public
- whether another outlet has the same information
- whether the journalist agreed to the terms
- which materials remain on background, off the record, or on the record

The project must not assume a journalist accepted an embargo merely because an email labeled information as embargoed.

### Interviews

Interview preparation should include:

- current facts and status
- known difficult questions
- clear distinctions between vision and implementation
- founder conflicts or prior relationships
- language to avoid
- uncertainty that should be admitted
- links to primary sources

No representative should improvise medical, legal, regulatory, security, or scientific claims outside their competence.

### Corrections

When a publication or creator makes a material error:

1. confirm that the project’s own source material is clear
2. contact the author privately with the specific correction and source
3. avoid public escalation unless necessary
4. record the correction request without publishing private contact details
5. update project material when project ambiguity contributed to the error
6. publish a public correction when the project itself made the mistake

### Media relationship register

The project may publish:

- outlet or public creator name
- date contacted
- general story angle
- status such as planned, contacted, replied, declined, published, or closed
- published result
- disclosed conflicts

It must not publish:

- private email addresses or phone numbers
- unpublished correspondence without permission
- off-the-record information
- personal notes that could enable harassment
- source identities promised confidentiality

## Current-events strategy

The project may enter a current conversation when all of these are true:

1. the event makes an existing project idea easier to understand
2. the project has a legitimate preexisting relationship to the subject
3. the connection can be explained without implying affiliation
4. the destination content has lasting value after the news cycle
5. the campaign can avoid exploiting victims, fear, or sensitive personal circumstances
6. the project can state what is known, inferred, and unknown
7. the connection has a planned sunset

### Raging-river test

Before using a high-attention event, ask:

- Is this our river or merely someone else’s accident?
- Are we adding understanding or merely attaching a name?
- Would the content still be worth publishing without the trend?
- Can a reasonable person mistake us for affiliated?
- Does the topic create pressure around health, money, or consent?
- What date will we stop leading with it?

### Current Odyssey attention

The present Odyssey cycle passes the relevance test because:

- the ancient text and its figures are foundational to the project
- the project’s public history predates the current adaptation
- the cultural attention gives people a shared vocabulary for Ogygia, Calypso, agency, memory, homecoming, and mortality
- the project can use original public-domain interpretation rather than modern film expression

It remains bounded by:

- no film assets
- no affiliation language
- no culture-war participation as a growth tactic
- no dependence on box-office success
- a deliberate transition from the film hook into health, agency, the commons, and governance
- retention of the dated attention brief after the cycle ends

## Editorial system

### Durable content pillars

1. **The Living Chronicle** — personal utility, continuity, provenance, and comprehension
2. **The Promise** — agency, privacy, consent, refusal, return, and evidence
3. **The World of Ogygia** — public-domain lore, characters, zones, and narrative meaning
4. **The Project’s Odyssey** — origin, failures, artifacts, decisions, and evolution
5. **The Open Forge** — architecture, contribution, validation, and public-domain craft
6. **The Institution** — progressive decentralization, incentives, economics, governance, and founder independence
7. **The Commons** — reusable knowledge, public-good infrastructure, research direction, and stewardship
8. **The Build in Public** — campaign results, development status, decisions, corrections, and failures

### Content levels

Every major idea should be available at multiple depths:

- five-second statement
- one-minute explanation
- visual explainer
- short post or thread
- full article or essay
- source document or decision record

The short version must remain true to the long version.

### Editorial status

Content should be labeled where useful as:

- draft
- approved
- scheduled
- published
- corrected
- historical
- retired

Publication dates and meaningful corrections should remain visible.

## Community strategy

### Enter as a participant

The project should learn the norms of a community and contribute something useful before asking for attention.

### Invite bounded participation

Good early invitations include:

- critique this covenant
- inspect this decision
- identify a contradiction
- test this synthetic example
- review accessibility
- annotate a historical artifact
- propose an original public-domain visual direction
- improve this explanation

Vague invitations to “join the movement” should not replace concrete contribution paths.

### Do not mobilize against people

The project will not direct its audience to confront a critic, journalist, contributor, competitor, partner, or public figure.

Substantive criticism should be answered with evidence, correction, or silence—not audience pressure.

### Moderation

Moderation protects participation without sanitizing disagreement.

Remove or restrict:

- personal health information posted in unsafe public contexts
- threats and harassment
- doxxing
- targeted abuse
- impersonation
- spam and fraud
- dangerous medical misinformation presented as project guidance
- content that violates published community rules

Do not remove good-faith criticism merely because it is harsh, skeptical, embarrassing, or strategically inconvenient.

## Creator and influencer relations

Creators may help translate the project to communities the founder cannot reach directly.

Every relationship must disclose:

- whether it is paid
- whether expenses or access were provided
- whether the creator received editorial control
- whether the creator has another material relationship with the project

Creators should be chosen for audience fit, credibility, explanatory ability, and willingness to state limitations—not raw follower count alone.

The project should not supply scripted personal opinions or require favorable coverage. Factual briefing, access, demonstrations, and review copies may be provided with transparent terms.

## Search and discovery

Search strategy should build a truthful canonical record.

Priority topics include:

- Calypso’s Promise
- public-domain health project
- Living Chronicle
- person-controlled longitudinal health record
- narrative health application
- open-source personal health record
- health-data covenant
- progressive decentralization in public-interest technology
- founder-independent institution
- Ogygia public-domain world

Search pages should answer real questions and link to primary materials. They should not imitate film-title pages, generate thin trend pages, or create false comparison content solely to capture traffic.

Historical pages should use dates, artifact labels, and links to current policy so old language is discoverable with context.

## Email strategy

Email is the initial durable relationship channel.

The founding-expedition list should:

- collect only necessary contact and interest information
- use explicit update consent
- state expected frequency
- make unsubscribe easy
- never require health information
- avoid silently enrolling people into product, research, governance, donation, or partner lists
- separate operational notices from promotional communication when relevant

Suggested initial cadence:

- one substantial project letter every two weeks
- additional messages only for a meaningful launch, correction, security or privacy matter, or clearly selected interest path

Every email should link to current status when discussing future capability.

## Partnerships and institutional outreach

A partnership should answer:

- what each party contributes
- who benefits
- which claims each party may make
- whether money or in-kind value is exchanged
- what data, if any, is involved
- what public disclosure is required
- what happens when the relationship ends
- whether the partner gains governance, brand, data, or exclusivity rights

The project should reject partnerships that require:

- hidden data access
- misleading endorsement
- mission-incompatible exclusivity
- unnecessary private information
- suppression of criticism or failures
- capital-linked control that violates the constitution
- presentation of planned capabilities as live

## Funding communication

Funding messages must distinguish:

- donations
- sponsorship
- grants
- service revenue
- investment, if ever applicable
- compensated work
- in-kind support

Before requesting funds, publish:

- the receiving entity or individual
- whether a contribution is tax deductible
- intended use
- restrictions, if any
- fees or related-party flows
- the relationship between Calypso’s Promise, Optimitron, and any nonprofit or commercial entity
- reporting cadence
- refund or revocation limits
- what influence funding does and does not purchase

No donor, sponsor, or investor may purchase private-data access, guaranteed governance power, specialist approval, research conclusions, or immunity from criticism.

## Measurement system

### North-star communication outcome

**Informed participation:** the number and quality of people who accurately understand the project and take a low-risk, relevant action.

### Primary measures

- comprehension of the one-sentence project explanation
- correct understanding of current status
- correct understanding of public project versus private data
- founding-expedition signup completion
- unsubscribe and complaint rates
- repository visits and contribution actions
- specialist-review interest
- origin essay completion
- provenance and current-status page visits
- direct versus social referral mix
- earned-media accuracy
- corrections requested and resolved
- contributor and audience diversity where measurable without unnecessary personal data

### Secondary measures

- qualified reach
- returning visitors
- email opens and link actions, where privacy-respecting measurement is available
- organic search discovery
- media inquiries
- partner inquiries
- community discussion quality
- content reuse and forks

### Vanity metrics

Follower count, raw impressions, likes, and total mentions may be recorded but must not be treated as proof of understanding, trust, product value, or institutional legitimacy.

### Guardrails

Track:

- reports of misleading claims
- accidental affiliation confusion
- health information submitted through public channels
- harassment or brigading linked to campaign activity
- privacy complaints
- spam complaints
- accessibility failures
- disproportionate support burden
- unsupported medical or financial interpretations
- creator or partner disclosure failures

## Experimentation

Marketing experiments are proposals, not excuses to lower ethical standards.

Every material experiment should define:

- hypothesis
- audience
- variable being tested
- success measure
- guardrail
- duration
- approximate cost
- stopping rule
- privacy and accessibility review
- result
- interpretation

The project must not experiment with:

- confusing consent
- hidden data collection
- health fear
- shame
- false scarcity
- deceptive affiliation
- undisclosed sponsorship
- manipulative defaults
- vulnerable populations without appropriate safeguards

Negative and inconclusive results should be published alongside positive results.

## Reputation, controversy, and crisis response

### Types of incidents

- factual error
- misleading project claim
- privacy or data concern
- health or safety misinformation
- modern-adaptation affiliation confusion
- public-domain misunderstanding
- contributor or community conduct issue
- partner or creator disclosure problem
- security incident
- governance or funding conflict
- founder conduct or credibility challenge
- coordinated harassment or misinformation

### Response sequence

1. protect people and systems
2. determine what is known
3. pause scheduled communication when necessary
4. identify the accountable steward
5. correct the project’s own record first
6. communicate scope, uncertainty, and next update
7. preserve evidence
8. avoid speculation and retaliatory audience mobilization
9. publish a post-incident record when safe
10. change the relevant process, not only the wording

### Founder controversy

The project must be able to distinguish criticism of the founder from threats to the project’s constitutional purpose.

Founder reputation may not be protected through:

- deleting accurate project history
- pressuring contributors to defend the founder
- using project accounts for personal retaliation
- withholding corrections
- treating disagreement as disloyalty

Founder independence ultimately requires communication systems, source records, and public trust that do not collapse around one person’s continued availability or reputation.

## Transparency boundaries

Default to publication, but do not publish:

- personal health information
- private signup records
- private email addresses or phone numbers
- confidential sources
- unpublished security details that create material risk
- private correspondence without permission or a valid public-interest basis
- personnel or conduct details that would violate due process or privacy
- partner information covered by a legitimate temporary confidentiality obligation
- tactical moderation or anti-abuse details that would enable evasion
- small-cell analytics that could identify individuals

When information is withheld, publish the category and reason when doing so is safe.

## Decision authority

During the current phase:

- the founding steward approves high-level positioning, founder-story publication, major media participation, and material campaigns
- maintainers review claims that touch frozen foundations, current status, repository policy, governance, and technical facts
- specialist reviewers approve claims within their named safety, health, privacy, accessibility, legal, research, or scientific scope
- no communications approval may silently change a product right or capability status

As governance matures, communication authority should be divided among:

- editorial operations
- factual and status review
- specialist claim review
- incident communication
- community moderation
- institutional and funding communication
- constitutional oversight

- assess channel dependence

- publish major failures and strategic changes

## The public pledge

Calypso’s Promise will not ask the world to trust a polished story while hiding how that story was constructed.

The project will publish the strategy, preserve the failed versions, disclose material relationships, distinguish ambition from reality, report meaningful results, correct errors, and show how communication decisions change.

The campaign is not outside the commons.

It is one of the first things being built inside it.
