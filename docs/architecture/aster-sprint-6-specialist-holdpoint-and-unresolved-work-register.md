- **Status:** OPEN AFTER SPRINT 6 DESIGN COMPLETION — no production or specialist holdpoint is closed by this revision
- **Scope:** production, pilot, specialist, institutional, implementation, operational, and public-claim gates after the Sprint 6 contract baseline
- **Information boundary:** public-safe descriptions and synthetic evidence only

## Purpose

Sprint completion does not authorize a capability whose safety, legitimacy, ownership, provider, or operational evidence remains unresolved.

This register gives each major gate:

- a stable identity;
- the capability or claim it blocks;
- inherited Sprint 5 holdpoints;
- the current evidence state;
- an accountable future role;
- the minimum closure evidence;
- a revalidation trigger; and
- an explicit disposition.

It also records concrete unresolved implementation work so later sprints do not confuse a missing runtime with a missing design decision or silently invent provider, storage, identity, workflow, clinical, or governance authority.

A holdpoint or unresolved item may be narrowed, closed, superseded, or retired only through a versioned record preserving prior scope, evidence, decision authority, remaining uncertainty, downstream consequences, and revalidation conditions.

## Status vocabulary

- **OPEN — PRODUCTION-BLOCKING:** no real protected information or consequential production action may use the capability.
- **OPEN — PILOT-BLOCKING:** no real-person, real-recipient, or representative pilot may use the capability.
- **OPEN — SPECIALIST HOLDPOINT:** qualified bounded review is required before the claim or capability advances.
- **OPEN — INSTITUTIONAL GATE:** ownership, continuity, governance, funding, or administrative evidence is required.
- **OPEN — IMPLEMENTATION GATE:** contracts exist, but the runtime, operating process, or environment-specific evidence does not.
- **OPEN — MEASUREMENT GATE:** representative performance, reliability, quality, accessibility, safety, or operational evidence is required.
- **CURRENT PUBLIC OR SYNTHETIC FLOW:** only the named public or synthetic flow may continue under explicit limitations.
- **CLOSED:** all named closure evidence and residual-risk disposition are recorded; no `HLD-S6-*` holdpoint is closed in this revision.

## Public and synthetic work that may proceed after acceptance

After explicit Sprint 6 acceptance and merge, these bounded activities may proceed without closing production holdpoints:

- Sprint 7 Forge MCP work over public documentation and synthetic data;
- additional public synthetic fixtures and deterministic validators;
- public documentation search, validation, draft mapping, and schema tools;
- public provider-neutral evaluation design without private data or provider endorsement;
- non-AI repository contribution and validation flows;
- public architecture, threat, assumption, decision, and challenge records; and
- design prototypes that do not collect real health data, create accounts, invoke production providers, or make clinical claims.

These activities remain subject to ordinary issue, pull-request, review, testing, information-handling, funding-conflict, and publication rules.

## Cross-cutting closure rules

A holdpoint or unresolved item is not closed because:

- a contract compiles;
- a validator passes;
- a synthetic fixture is deterministic;
- CI and DCO are green;
- a provider offers credits or advertises a feature;
- a sponsor funds the work;
- a model appears capable in an informal demonstration;
- a founding steward approves Sprint 6;
- no incident has been observed; or
- a public document describes a comprehensive future process.

Closure requires the exact named evidence, accountable authority, residual-risk disposition, conflict record, and revalidation trigger appropriate to the capability or claim.

## Relationship to Sprint 6 acceptance

Sprint 6 may be accepted and merged while every `HLD-S6-*` holdpoint remains open because the accepted sprint scope is contract, governance, deterministic validation, public synthetic fixture, compatibility, migration, and documentation evidence.

Acceptance does not waive or weaken any holdpoint. It establishes the baseline that later implementations and reviews must satisfy.
