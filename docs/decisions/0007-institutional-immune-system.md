# Decision 0007 — Institutional Immune System as the corrigibility layer

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-24
- **Owners:** Founding steward
- **Affected frozen components:** None; implements frozen player-right, progressive-decentralization, and founder-independence constraints
- **Related issues or pull requests:** Added by the institutional-immune-system baseline pull request

## Context

Calypso’s Promise is intentionally designed as a long-lived system of connected product, health, incentive, contribution, governance, research, economic, and institutional loops. Those loops can compound value, but any coherent loop can also become highly efficient at preserving a flawed premise, metric, incentive, interpretation, or authority structure.

Existing repository foundations already require evidence, correction, appeal, rollback, specialist review, minority protection, emergency limits, public decision records, and founder replacement. Decision 0006 also establishes a feedback-to-governed-work loop with reopening, outcome review, override records, and capture controls. These protections are currently distributed across product, architecture, governance, policy, roadmap, and contributor documents.

Without an explicit cross-cutting category, correction mechanisms may be implemented inconsistently, omitted from future modules, controlled by the same authority whose behavior they review, or treated as optional process rather than architecture.

The founding steward has directed that these mechanisms be organized under one durable category: the **Institutional Immune System**.

## Decision

Calypso’s Promise accepts the **Institutional Immune System** as a baseline cross-cutting product and institutional architecture.

The following statements are binding at the baseline level:

1. Every mechanism powerful enough to improve the system must expose a proportionate way to challenge its premise, observe its outcomes, constrain its harm, reverse or replace it, and preserve what was learned.
2. The immune system applies across epistemic, technical, product, health-safety, incentive, governance, economic, and institutional domains. It is not a single service, council, dashboard, or emergency switch.
3. The frozen Promise defines the protected rights and purpose floor. Empirical claims about how best to fulfill that Promise belong in doctrine or implementation and must remain versioned, challengeable, and replaceable.
4. Material policies, incentives, models, authority transfers, experiments, and irreversible operations must declare intended outcomes, guardrails, assumptions, uncertainty, evidence, affected groups, responsible authority, review timing, and failure or rollback conditions appropriate to their risk.
5. The project must maintain an Assumption Registry for material causal, behavioral, technical, economic, and governance beliefs. Rights and constitutional commitments must not be misclassified as ordinary hypotheses merely to make them easier to weaken.
6. Criticism, disconfirmation, reproduction of failure, adversarial review, correction, appeal, rollback, restoration, and proof that an accepted mechanism is ineffective are legitimate contributions. Credit depends on validated value rather than challenge volume or controversy.
7. Challenges must be evaluated by evidence, affectedness, risk, rights implications, uncertainty, and falsifiability rather than popularity or the status of the challenger. A challenger does not need to possess the complete replacement solution to demonstrate a material defect.
8. Proposal, execution, monitoring, review, appeal, and emergency authority should not remain permanently concentrated in one person or body. Independence requirements must increase with consequence and institutional maturity.
9. Reversibility is a default design requirement. Irreversible changes require higher evidence, narrower authority, stronger review, explicit residual-risk acceptance, and a restoration plan for harms that cannot be fully rolled back.
10. Uncertainty must remain visible in records and behavior. Unknown, disputed, inferred, provisional, superseded, and invalidated are legitimate states; uncertainty must not be hidden behind confident language, a computed score, an AI output, or a majority decision.
11. Majority preference, contributor volume, capital, expertise, urgency, or executive authority cannot vote away the protected player-right floor. Affected-party consent, specialist duties, due process, and minority safeguards remain decision-class constraints.
12. Emergency powers may pause, isolate, rate-limit, revoke, or contain a defined threat. They must be narrow, logged, independently reviewable, automatically expiring, and unable to permanently redesign the system or amend the constitution through emergency action alone.
13. Material failures must support restoration as well as correction: affected records, access, incentives, decisions, credit, communications, or user state should be repaired where possible, and residual harm must be documented rather than silently treated as resolved.
14. Every ordinary improvement loop must connect to the immune-system lifecycle: observe, detect, attribute, challenge, contain when necessary, test, decide, restore, learn, and revalidate.
15. Immune-system controls must be proportional to consequence. They must not create blanket paralysis, infinite review, unbounded vetoes, performative compliance, or a new unaccountable authority.
16. The system must preserve voice, meaningful exit, portability, and credible forkability as final defenses against institutional capture. A nominal right to fork without usable specifications, data portability, provenance, buildability, or governance history is insufficient.
17. The architecture begins as public documentation, issue intake, registries, decision records, tests, simulations, reviews, and manual gates. Automation or binding authority unlocks only after the relevant contracts, safeguards, evidence, and phase gates exist.

The canonical baseline is `docs/governance/institutional-immune-system.md`.

## Consequences

### Benefits

- Distributed correction mechanisms become one inspectable architecture rather than unrelated safeguards.
- Future modules receive a consistent requirement to expose assumptions, outcomes, guardrails, challenge, appeal, rollback, restoration, and revalidation.
- The feedback-to-governed-work loop gains an explicit outer loop capable of questioning its own definitions of priority and success.
- The institution can reward discovery of flawed premises without treating criticism as disloyalty.
- Founder independence and progressive decentralization gain stronger defenses against majority, expert, maintainer, donor, executive, AI, and governance-mechanism capture.
- Long-term adaptation can replace doctrine and implementation while protecting the project’s rights and purpose floor.

### Costs and tradeoffs

- Maintaining assumptions, challenge records, reviews, failure evidence, and restoration history creates continuing institutional work.
- Reversibility, observability, and independent review may increase implementation cost and slow high-consequence changes.
- Public challenge can create reputational discomfort and expose disagreement.
- Independence is difficult during Phase 0 while the contributor and specialist community is small.
- Some harms, disclosures, migrations, communications, and external consequences cannot be completely reversed.

### Risks

- The immune-system metaphor could be abused to label people or dissent as threats rather than treating harmful mechanisms and unexamined premises as the target.
- Challenge processes could be brigaded, weaponized for delay, or dominated by high-status participants.
- Emergency brakes could become shadow governance.
- Assumption records could become stale compliance artifacts.
- Confidence labels or risk scores could create false precision.
- A single review body could capture the correction layer.
- Excessive process could make ordinary low-risk contribution inaccessible.

These risks require explicit person-protection language, proportional scrutiny, public authority maps, appeal, rotation, conflict controls, automatic expiry, outcome measurement, and periodic review of the immune system itself.

## Alternatives considered

### Keep correction requirements distributed

Rejected as the durable model because future contributors and modules would have no canonical corrigibility interface, and shared failure patterns would remain difficult to inspect.

### Treat ordinary issue tracking as the complete immune system

Rejected because product issues alone do not cover assumptions, incentives, authority, constitutional review, emergency containment, restoration, independent audit, or institutional exit and forkability.

### Create a permanent safety council immediately

Rejected because the present project lacks the mature constituency, specialist network, identity model, removal rules, and evidence required to grant a new body broad authority. Manual responsibilities remain with current accountable roles under existing decision classes.

### Give the founding steward a universal emergency override

Rejected as the permanent design because it would reinforce the key-person dependency the institutional mandate requires the project to eliminate. Current Phase 0 authority remains bounded and transitional.

### Make every decision fully reversible

Rejected as impossible. Publication, disclosure, external communication, user harm, migration, financial commitments, and downstream data use may leave irreversible effects. The architecture instead requires explicit irreversibility analysis, prevention, containment, restoration, and residual-risk records.

## Validation or review required

Before the baseline is represented as operationally mature:

- publish and seed the Assumption Registry
- provide a public-safe structured challenge path
- define challenge, evidence, containment, decision, restoration, and revalidation record shapes
- map current authorities and separation-of-duty limitations
- define proportional scrutiny bands
- add synthetic fixtures for flawed metrics, minority harms, unsafe incentives, stale assumptions, captured review, bad-faith challenge, emergency overreach, failed rollback, and incomplete restoration
- connect material project, governance, incentive, and architecture proposals to immune-system fields
- define private escalation for protected security, privacy, conduct, account, and person-specific health evidence
- test whether ordinary contributors can understand and use the process

Before independent immune-system authority is delegated:

- establish a charter, scope, constituency, qualification rules, conflicts, terms, removal, appeal, transparency, confidentiality, emergency limits, sunset, and rollback
- prevent the same constituency from permanently controlling proposal, execution, audit, appeal, and emergency functions
- complete adversarial simulations and a bounded real-world pilot
- publish participation, concentration, decisions, false positives, false negatives, delays, outcomes, failures, and corrections

## Migration and rollback

Migration begins with:

1. this decision and the canonical architecture
2. a seeded Assumption Registry
3. a structured public-safe challenge issue form
4. cross-links from repository orientation and later from governance, roadmap, validation, incentive, architecture, and proposal surfaces
5. manual use on selected material assumptions and decisions
6. typed contracts, validators, fixtures, and dashboards only after the manual protocol produces evidence

The category may be revised when it creates systematic paralysis, capture, incomprehension, performative compliance, unsafe disclosure, or poor outcomes. Revisions must preserve meaningful challenge, correction, appeal, containment, restoration, and institutional learning.

## Freeze impact

This decision establishes a new **BASELINE** cross-cutting architecture under existing frozen constraints.

It does not change:

- the player promise or protected player rights
- private-by-default operation
- personal utility before secondary use
- meaningful refusal and non-punitive return
- open code and private production health data
- deterministic incentive boundaries
- AI and domain-authority boundaries
- specialist review and safety precedence
- progressive-decentralization phase gates
- the deferral of weighted-governance, identity, token, blockchain, NFT, treasury, compensation, ownership, and on-chain mechanisms

Removing meaningful corrigibility from a consequential product or institutional mechanism, or granting permanent unreviewable emergency or correction authority, requires a later material governance decision record.
