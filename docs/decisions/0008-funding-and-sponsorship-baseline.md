# Decision 0008 — Funding and Sponsorship Baseline

- **Status:** ACCEPTED BASELINE
- **Date:** 2026-07-26
- **Decision class:** Strategic and economic; governance and institutional resilience
- **Authority:** Founding steward under the Phase 0 governance baseline
- **Tracking issue:** [#37](https://github.com/finalboss-tom/calypsos-promise/issues/37)
- **Entry baseline:** Sprint 5 squash commit `4d09e8fc5b81f354c4568f97794fd9533ec68048`
- **Independent review:** Not yet obtained; legal, accounting, tax, nonprofit, financial-control, and governance review remain pending

## Context

Calypso’s Promise is intended to become a durable public-benefit institution and community-funded public good. The frozen Vision requires transparent contribution, review, funding, and stewardship systems and an economically self-sustaining institution that does not depend on exploitation, surveillance, mission drift, or permanent founder subsidy.

The repository already establishes that capital cannot purchase player rights, private-data access, constitutional dominance, governance immunity, or safety exceptions. It also anticipates public-good support, sponsorship, a donation-transparency website surface, public funding and expenditure reporting, and later sustainable economics.

Before this decision, those boundaries were distributed across the Product Constitution, Vision, Governance Baseline, Public Institutional Roadmap, publication policy, website architecture, incentive model, and Institutional Immune System. The project did not yet have one operating doctrine defining:

- what counts as a donation, grant, sponsorship, underwriting arrangement, in-kind support, affiliate arrangement, investment, compensation, or research funding;
- which relationships may be accepted in Phase 0;
- what recognition or consideration may be offered;
- which benefits are prohibited;
- how conflicts, recusals, concentration, dependency, correction, suspension, termination, and public reporting work; or
- how private financial source records remain separate from public institutional accountability records.

A public support or sponsorship surface without those rules would allow implementation convenience, sponsor pressure, or private negotiation to define the institution’s economics by accident.

## Decision

Calypso’s Promise accepts a provider-independent **Funding and Sponsorship Baseline** as a Phase 0 institutional constraint.

The institution may receive mission-aligned donations, grants, public-good underwriting, sponsorships, services, infrastructure credits, and other approved support only when the relationship:

1. preserves the complete meaningfully free personal product;
2. does not purchase product, data, research, safety, roadmap, governance, or constitutional authority;
3. does not reward broader permission, unnecessary disclosure, research participation, payment, wealth, or donation size;
4. subjects funded work to ordinary issue, pull-request, review, validation, correction, and outcome requirements;
5. publishes a reviewed, minimized institutional record of material relationships, conflicts, restrictions, benefits, dependencies, work, outcomes, and corrections;
6. keeps raw donor, payment, banking, tax, contract, negotiation, correspondence, and credential records outside public repository systems;
7. remains replaceable and contains vendor, sponsor, founder, and donor dependency; and
8. exposes a proportionate challenge, containment, suspension, termination, restoration, and revalidation path.

This decision creates doctrine and public record contracts. It does not create operational financial capability.

## Constitutional funding boundary

Funding must never purchase or condition:

- the right to create, maintain, inspect, correct, export, or delete a useful Living Chronicle;
- essential privacy, permission, receipt, safety, accessibility, or non-AI fallback behavior;
- personal or aggregate private health information;
- signup, donor, participant, contributor, partner, or contact lists;
- research access, recruitment, participation, or findings;
- House of Keys grants, decisions, receipt treatment, or placement inside permission flows;
- Chronicle truth, source truth, clinical claims, health guidance, or player-facing recommendations;
- roadmap priority, guaranteed implementation, issue closure, acceptance criteria, release approval, or favorable review;
- governance seats, votes, vetoes, constitutional authority, emergency powers, or immunity from oversight;
- Fellowship, Renown, Laurels, player progression, contribution credit, or permanent status;
- favorable benchmark, security, accessibility, scientific, clinical, policy, or outcome results;
- suppression, delay, alteration, or pre-approval of unfavorable findings, incidents, corrections, or public reports;
- exclusivity that blocks interoperability, meaningful choice, provider replacement, a fork, or mission continuity; or
- rights in public-domain project foundations beyond those already available to everyone.

## Funding taxonomy decision

Relationships are classified by substance rather than label:

- **individual donation** — support without negotiated commercial benefit;
- **organizational donation** — institutional support without material promotional consideration;
- **restricted grant** — support limited to a defined purpose, deliverable, population, or period;
- **public-good underwriting** — support for a named public artifact, review, fellowship, capability, or institutional outcome;
- **corporate sponsorship** — support accompanied by approved public recognition or other bounded consideration;
- **in-kind support** — infrastructure, services, credits, equipment, professional work, or labor provided without ordinary cash payment;
- **matching support** — funding released against qualifying contributions without manipulative urgency or artificial scarcity;
- **pro bono specialist review** — qualified review provided without ordinary payment, with independence limitations disclosed;
- **affiliate or referral revenue** — compensation based on registration, purchase, usage, or another attributable conversion;
- **research funding** — support tied to a study, recruitment, participant access, analysis, or research use;
- **investment or ownership capital** — equity, debt, repayment, ownership, or control-bearing capital; and
- **individual compensation** — payment or economic benefit provided to a person for work or service.

Affiliate and referral revenue, research funding, investment or ownership capital, treasury custody, compensation frameworks, and operational fundraising rails remain deferred until separately accepted.

## Recognition boundary

Permitted recognition may include:

- public name and optional rights-cleared logo;
- a link from a canonical public funding record;
- neutral acknowledgment on the funded artifact, report, release, or fellowship cohort;
- inclusion in aggregate or relationship-level funding reports;
- a mutually fact-checked announcement;
- a description of the support actually provided; and
- public evidence of delivered work and measured outcomes.

Recognition must not imply product endorsement, medical or scientific approval, safety certification, preferred provider status, institutional authority, or exclusive relationship.

Sponsor names, logos, trademarks, and supplied promotional assets remain third-party materials under their actual rights and do not enter the public-domain commons merely by appearing in project records.

## Public and private record boundary

The public repository stores reviewed institutional derivatives, not financial source systems.

A material public funding record identifies the relationship, classification, status, public value or amount band, purpose, restrictions, approved benefits, conflicts, authority, linked work, dependency, exit behavior, outcomes, variance, corrections, and review timing.

The public repository must not store raw payment records, bank or tax details, personal donor contact information, contracts containing protected terms, private negotiation, sanctions or identity-verification records, credentials, private compensation details, or confidential legal and accounting advice.

Individual donors are aggregated by default. A person is named only with documented permission or a reviewed institutional requirement. Organizational, restricted, related-party, vendor-linked, and otherwise material relationships require a public record unless a lawful and safety-reviewed withholding condition applies; the public record must still disclose the existence, category, material effect, responsible authority, and review date when possible.

## Acceptance authority and conflicts

During Phase 0:

- the founding steward retains accountable acceptance authority;
- ordinary small unrestricted donations may later operate under a standing policy only after a legitimate payment rail, custodian, accounting owner, privacy notice, refund rule, and legal treatment are documented;
- organizational sponsorships, restricted grants, named underwriting, related-party support, vendor-linked support, affiliate arrangements, high-risk-sector relationships, and critical infrastructure support require a material relationship review;
- the direct financial beneficiary, sponsor, funded implementer, or sponsor-funded evaluator cannot be the only reviewer of the relationship or its claimed outcome;
- conflicts and recusals are published through a reviewed derivative;
- an unavailable independent reviewer leaves independence claims pending rather than satisfied; and
- exceptions require a decision record, narrow scope, responsible authority, expiration, challenge path, and rollback or termination behavior.

## Concentration and dependency doctrine

Funding concentration cannot be reduced to one permanent numeric rule. The institution uses both proportion and critical-function dependency.

Provisional review triggers are:

- 10% of trailing-twelve-month support from one source — concentration watch;
- 20% — written mitigation, diversification, replacement, and continuity plan;
- 33% — presumptive pause on additional dependency without independent review and a time-bounded exception; and
- any support controlling a critical function — material dependency regardless of percentage.

These thresholds are baseline doctrine subject to revalidation. They are not proof of safety below a threshold or automatic misconduct above one.

Critical functions include domains, release and signing authority, production credentials, security monitoring, primary storage, backups, authentication, major AI access, fundraising accounts, legal or fiscal custody, and canonical public communication channels.

## Work and outcome doctrine

A funded project record must distinguish:

- offer, pledge, receipt, restriction, and actual available value;
- activity, deliverable, acceptance, release, use, and public benefit;
- expected and actual timing;
- planned and actual spending or in-kind consumption;
- completion, partial completion, deferral, cancellation, failure, and residual obligation; and
- output from outcome.

A merged pull request, published report, spent grant, or completed fellowship is not automatically proof of public benefit.

## Alternatives considered

### Accept support case by case without a baseline

Rejected. Private negotiation would become de facto economic governance and future public reporting would be inconsistent.

### Use conventional Gold, Silver, and Bronze sponsor tiers

Rejected as the primary model. Generic payment tiers optimize visibility rather than public benefit and create pressure to sell prominence. Recognition may vary by material support, but the canonical offer is public-good underwriting linked to work and outcomes.

### Copy a provider marketplace and affiliate model immediately

Rejected for Phase 0. Conversion-linked placement can align incentives in some developer tools, but health, permission, research, and AI-provider contexts create stronger conflicts. Affiliate and referral revenue require a separate reviewed decision.

### Refuse all institutional funding until a final legal entity exists

Rejected. Doctrine, transparent offers, pro bono review, and in-kind support can be designed before final entity selection. Actual receipt must still use a legitimate approved custodian and legal treatment.

### Treat every donation as public and named

Rejected. Transparency about institutional power does not require exposing individual donors or private source records. Organizational and material relationships require stronger public disclosure than ordinary individual giving.

## Consequences

### Positive

- funding rules exist before sponsor pressure or donation implementation;
- public support can be tied to concrete public-good work and outcomes;
- provider, infrastructure, health-content, research, and governance conflicts become reviewable;
- the project can reduce founder subsidy without silently replacing it with donor capture;
- Sprint 8 and Sprint 19 receive a canonical doctrine for donation and funding-transparency surfaces; and
- later treasury and sustainable-economics work begins from explicit rights and information boundaries.

### Costs and risks

- review and public reporting add administrative work;
- some sponsors will reject the limits or disclosure requirements;
- public amount bands may reduce precision;
- concentration thresholds can become false comfort if treated mechanically;
- a founder-led Phase 0 process lacks full independent legitimacy;
- entity, tax, accounting, payment, custody, contract, sanctions, and jurisdiction questions remain unresolved; and
- even mission-aligned funding can create reputational, operational, or agenda pressure.

## Reversibility and replacement

Implementation details, thresholds, record schemas, recognition formats, and operating procedures may be superseded through a later decision and migration record.

The constitutional boundaries—meaningfully free personal value, no purchased private-data access, no purchased rights or constitutional control, no reward for broader permission or unnecessary disclosure, and no bypass of safety and specialist duties—cannot be weakened through ordinary sponsorship policy.

A relationship may be declined, suspended, narrowed, corrected, terminated, refunded where appropriate, allowed to expire, or replaced. Public history must preserve the prior relationship, reason, authority, material outcome, and residual obligation without publishing protected source detail.

## Unresolved gates

Before accepting material funding or activating a public support destination, the project still requires appropriate decisions or evidence for:

- legal entity or fiscal-sponsor authority;
- authorized payment and custody rails;
- tax, charitable, and deductibility representations;
- accounting ownership, controls, reporting, reconciliation, and retention;
- sanctions, fraud, refund, chargeback, and identity-verification duties;
- contract authority and signature controls;
- related-party transaction treatment;
- individual compensation and reimbursement;
- insurance and liability where applicable;
- privacy notices for donor and partner information;
- treasury, budget, reserve, and runway policy;
- independent financial review; and
- entity succession, incapacity, dissolution, and asset-lock behavior.

## Revalidation triggers

Revalidate this decision:

- before the first material organizational sponsorship, restricted grant, affiliate arrangement, investment, or research-funding relationship;
- before a donation or sponsorship form becomes LIVE;
- before one source reaches a concentration trigger;
- when a sponsor supports or controls a critical function;
- after the first relationship suspension or termination;
- when an entity, fiscal sponsor, treasury, payment processor, or accounting system is selected;
- at every institutional phase-exit review; and
- when evidence shows that recognition, restrictions, concentration, or dependency is distorting product, research, provider, governance, or public-reporting decisions.
