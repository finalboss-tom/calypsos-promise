## Review method

This review compares the implementation against the Product Constitution, World and Lore Canon, Gameplay Foundation, Living Chronicle boundary, House of Keys boundary, Aster boundary, Sprint 8 website foundation, accepted pre-Sprint 9 issue #64, and the pre-9.10 quality review.

The review uses:

- the permanent rendered-browser scenarios introduced by 9.8;
- exact route measurements from the accepted 9.7 candidate; and
- explicit limitations where CI automation cannot substitute for people or specialist review.

## Findings

### Product and authority coherence

The prologue remains an explanation through play rather than a premature private product. It accepts only repository-authored synthetic choices and creates no account, Chronicle record, permission, health claim, reward, rank, provider relationship, or durable progression.

Aster and the direct manual path share one state machine, fixture set, review rule, projection rule, completion rule, and departure boundary. Character framing does not purchase authority or completion advantage.

### Refusal, correction, and exit

Review, prepared correction, refusal, alternate-fixture selection, discard, restart, public exit, future-account inspection, and completion without conversion are explicit. The experience has an honest stopping point and does not use countdowns, streak loss, shame, health warnings, or repeated conversion pressure.

### Chronicle and receipt explanation

The Chronicle-shaped view keeps source, original and corrected values, synthetic time, confirmation, authority, lifecycle, and discard meaning inspectable. The receipt-shaped view explicitly states that no request, evaluation, grant, execution, audit event, or data release exists.

The detailed mappings are placed behind native disclosures so essential comprehension does not require reading every contract field.

### First Lantern and canon

First Lantern is a deterministic explanation completion based on eight visible synthetic conditions. It is not one of the durable Fourteen Lanterns, a canonical unlock, a rank, a reward, or evidence of a health action.

### Accessibility and resilience direction

The implementation has semantic scene headings, one current progress step, visible focus, polite transition announcements, native buttons, native disclosures, keyboard-operable choices, reduced-motion and contrast rules, forced-color rules, reduced-data rules, narrow-screen layouts, and a no-JavaScript direct explanation.

The 9.8 harness checks those behaviors in a rendered browser, including keyboard activation for the two representative journeys. Independent assistive-technology review remains open.

### Security and privacy direction

The route inherits the accepted CSP, permissions policy, security headers, first-party-only runtime, secret scan, and manual release control. The browser harness fails on external requests, `/api/join`, WebSockets, errors, arbitrary prologue inputs, cookies, browser storage, IndexedDB, Cache Storage, or state restoration after exit.

### Performance and duration

The accepted 9.7 static evidence shows `/prologue` within every Sprint 8 ceiling. JavaScript is the closest metric, retaining 4,697 bytes of margin. The workstream therefore rejects a Sprint 9 budget increase and adds a direct regression assertion against the Sprint 8 ceilings.

The human-time model uses visible words plus explicit decision time and is intentionally distinct from automation elapsed time. All three modeled direct journeys must remain under ten minutes before acceptance.
