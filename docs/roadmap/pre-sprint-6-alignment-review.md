## Incentive alignment

### Personal value remains first

The project does not require research participation, commercial sharing, provider enrollment, sponsor selection, payment, public contribution, or broader permission to receive the intended personal-core value.

Sprint 6 must preserve that ordering. Aster may reduce interaction cost and help a person understand or structure information, but it may not turn model availability, provider selection, broader collection, retained conversation, secondary-use permission, or premium compute into a condition of useful capture, permission review, correction, export, deletion, or ordinary play.

### Permission cannot become progression

House of Keys authority remains separate from quest completion, rewards, Fellowship, Renown, Laurels, and narrative progression.

Aster must not:

- reward a person for granting broader permission;
- frame refusal as failure or loss;
- infer permission from engagement, silence, continued use, model interaction, imported content, or provider terms;
- use a conversational acknowledgement as confirmation of a materially different action; or
- convert an `indeterminate` policy result into an allow decision.

### Funding and provider relationships cannot become product authority

Decision 0008 and Decision 0010 apply to model credits, cloud credits, sponsored benchmarks, affiliate incentives, provider access, institutional data sources, evaluation support, free implementation labor, enterprise distribution, and related-party relationships.

Those relationships may not determine:

- Chronicle meaning or source rank;
- which provider becomes a default;
- connector priority or placement;
- egress or retention policy;
- the responsive product path;
- benchmark conclusions or publication;
- the removal of a manual or non-AI path;
- roadmap or governance authority; or
- an exception to security, privacy, permission, correction, export, deletion, replacement, migration, or teardown requirements.

## Architecture alignment

### Domain separation remains sound

The current package boundaries correctly keep:

- Chronicle truth in the Living Chronicle capability;
- permission truth in the House of Keys capability;
- content and lore in content contracts;
- application composition outside reusable domain contracts;
- providers and infrastructure behind inward-facing contracts; and
- AI and MCP as adapters rather than authorities.

Sprint 6 should introduce a bounded Aster contract capability rather than placing Aster behavior, provider policy, conversational memory, prompt handling, or model results inside `packages/health-schema`.

The exact package name is an implementation refinement, but `packages/aster` with a deliberate public export such as `@calypsos-promise/aster` is the clean default unless the Sprint 6 plan documents a stronger boundary. It should not be split into provider, gateway, contracts, safety, memory, and retrieval packages before one current consumer and independent change pressure justify those modules.

### Modular monolith remains the right default

Nothing in Sprint 6 requires a production microservice, queue, workflow engine, scheduler, event store, model gateway, vector database, or remote provider.

The contract package should remain deterministic and provider-independent. A future application may orchestrate provider calls and delayed work through ports, but the contract must remain testable with a local deterministic or synthetic adapter and without credentials, production data, or network access.

### Chronicle extension pressure is a known risk

`packages/health-schema/src/types.ts` and `packages/health-schema/src/validate.ts` already carry substantial Chronicle responsibility. Module-boundary guidance correctly identifies internal decomposition before materially extending those files.

Sprint 6 should reference Chronicle identifiers and proposal shapes through explicit contracts. It should not absorb Aster roles, provider metadata, prompt state, memory retention, retrieval, work scheduling, or conversational history into the Chronicle aggregate merely because Aster acts on Chronicle information.

## Required Sprint 6 execution-plan clarifications

These clarifications fit inside the accepted goal and deliverables. They do not renumber the roadmap or authorize production implementation.

### 1. Bounded capability and public contract

Define one pre-stable Aster contract capability with:

- a named owner and one primary reason to change;
- deliberate public exports;
- an independently versioned serialized contract where externally meaningful;
- stable validation issues, refusal reasons, and failure codes;
- no database, network, model SDK, provider, UI, filesystem, environment, session, clock, or secret dependency in the core contract;
- tests importing only the public package surface; and
- public synthetic fixtures covering diverse interaction and accessibility contexts.

### 2. Role contracts and authority matrix

For Scribe, Librarian, Wayfinder, Interpreter, and Storykeeper, define:

- allowed input and output classes;
- prohibited actions;
- required authority and source evidence;
- confirmation and clarification requirements;
- responsive, deferred, or manual-fallback classification;
- confidence and uncertainty behavior;
- error and refusal behavior;
- retention and egress implications; and
- the deterministic domain capability that owns any resulting authoritative action.

A narrative role name must have a direct, accessible explanation and must not create technical authority.

### 3. Proposal boundary

Define a structured Aster proposal envelope separate from:

- canonical Chronicle records;
- House of Keys grants and decisions;
- quest completion and rewards;
- Aster memory;
- access receipts and protected audit;
- provider request or response logs;
- retrieval indexes; and
- delayed-work operation state.

The envelope should preserve source references, relevant model or rule revision, confidence, uncertainty, clarifications, transformation provenance, intended action, and confirmation state without copying unnecessary source material.

### 4. Confirmation and deterministic validation

Aster output is never self-confirming.

The contracts must distinguish:

- a draft was produced;
- the player reviewed it;
- the player confirmed an exact proposal;
- the relevant permission was valid and fresh;
- the domain validator accepted the proposed record or action;
- authoritative storage succeeded;
- and a later projection, enrichment, or notification completed.

A conversational acknowledgement, transport success, queued request, or provider response is not proof of domain completion.

### 5. Intent, confidence, clarification, and ambiguity

The intent taxonomy should:

- separate capture, recall, explanation, navigation, permission review, correction, export, deletion preparation, support routing, and non-actionable conversation;
- allow unknown, mixed, conflicting, and unsupported intent;
- require clarification when a material action cannot be bound safely;
- avoid false precision in confidence;
- preserve approximate time, source uncertainty, semantic mapping loss, and conflicting records; and
- fail safely rather than choosing a consequential action because it is the most likely interpretation.

### 6. Source, retrieval, and recall

Every health-related recalled statement must be traceable to authoritative Chronicle records or clearly labeled public educational material.

The contracts should preserve:

- exact record and revision references;
- source class and provenance;
- mapping and implementation-guide revision where relevant;
- correction, retraction, conflict, and deletion state;
- retrieval or index freshness;
- explanatory uncertainty; and
- a structured-query fallback when semantic retrieval is missing or stale.

A model, provider, sponsor, newest source, standards profile, or retrieval score cannot become automatic truth.

### 7. Memory classes and lifecycle

Define distinct classes for:

- transient interaction context;
- player-visible retained preference or accessibility context;
- player-visible retained conversation where separately chosen;
- derived Aster memory linked to authoritative records;
- narrative presentation state; and
- provider-side operational metadata that is not product memory.

For every material memory class, define:

- purpose;
- source;
- authority;
- visibility;
- editability;
- export;
- deletion;
- retention and expiry;
- correction and supersession;
- provider egress;
- training or secondary-use prohibition or permission requirement;
- and fallback when memory is missing or refused.

Hidden provider memory must not become canonical product state.

### 8. Prompt injection and untrusted input

Treat documents, images, imported records, web content, retrieved passages, provider responses, tool results, model output, and prior conversation content as untrusted.

The contract and synthetic evidence should show that untrusted input cannot:

- grant itself tool or data authority;
- alter House of Keys facts;
- expand the requested action;
- choose the controlling person or Chronicle subject;
- bypass confirmation;
- convert `indeterminate` into allow;
- suppress source references or uncertainty;
- expose another person’s data;
- invoke arbitrary database or network access; or
- persist itself as memory merely by instructing the system to do so.

### 9. Responsive and deferred behavior

Classify each result as:

- required in the current responsive path;
- optional deferred work; or
- fully replaceable by a manual or deterministic path.

Where delayed work is modeled, define:

- stable operation identity;
- requested input and authority revision;
- pending and completed states;
- timeout;
- cancellation;
- retry safety;
- duplicate handling;
- failure class;
- provider-unavailable behavior;
- supersession;
- stale-result rejection;
- and correction or replay implications.

A delayed Aster result cannot overwrite a newer confirmed record or act under stale permission, intent, source, or policy state.

Sprint 6 remains a contract and governance sprint. It does not select a production queue, scheduler, workflow engine, model gateway, or event store.

### 10. Local synthetic substitute and non-AI fallback

Provide a deterministic local or synthetic Aster adapter that can exercise:

- each role contract;
- successful structured drafting;
- unknown and ambiguous intent;
- clarification;
- low confidence;
- refusal;
- prompt injection;
- provider timeout and unavailability;
- stale or superseded work;
- source-linked recall; and
- complete manual capture and permission review.

The substitute is contract evidence, not proof of production-model quality, safety, latency, conformance, privacy, or reliability.

### 11. Provider governance and egress

The provider-egress policy should state:

- allowed information classes and minimum necessary payload;
- prohibited information and authority-bearing context;
- region, retention, logging, training, human-review, subcontractor, and deletion considerations;
- model and provider revision attribution;
- credentials and environment boundary;
- data-processing, legal, security, privacy, clinical, accessibility, procurement, and financial holdpoints;
- fallback, replacement, migration, and teardown;
- cost and concentration review;
- incident and correction behavior; and
- public claims that may or may not be made.

No production provider should be selected during Sprint 6.

### 12. Evidence, compatibility, and completion record

Sprint 6 should close with:

- versioned contracts;
- deterministic validation;
- public synthetic fixtures;
- Node tests or equivalent focused evidence;
- cross-contract checks against Chronicle, House of Keys, content, incentives, and security boundaries;
- a provider-neutral and funding-conflict review;
- compatibility and migration requirements;
- a control and specialist-holdpoint mapping;
- an unresolved-work register with owners and triggers;
- a completion record distinguishing accepted contract evidence from implementation, deployment, specialist review, or production readiness; and
- full repository validation through `pnpm check` and CI.
