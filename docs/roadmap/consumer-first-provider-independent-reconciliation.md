## Decision summary

The repository is already structurally consumer-first and provider-independent.

The frozen Product Constitution, Vision, Architecture Foundation, Living Chronicle contracts, House of Keys, incentive model, roadmap, governance, security baseline, and Decision 0008 consistently require:

1. useful personal value before research, commerce, or collective benefit;
2. a longitudinal record under the person’s control;
3. source, Chronicle, permission, execution, receipt, and AI-proposal separation;
4. provider-independent identifiers, contracts, exports, and replaceable vendors;
5. meaningful refusal without loss of the complete personal product;
6. no purchased data access, roadmap authority, provider placement, favorable findings, or governance power; and
7. evidence-gated expansion into connectors, research, economics, and institutional authority.

No mission-level contradiction requires replacing the player promise or changing Sprint 6 order.

The material gap was **implicit strategy**: the repository did not yet state in one place why providers, EHRs, payers, laboratories, devices, exchanges, and institutional partners are important but do not become the canonical product center.

## Precision correction

The project should not claim that healthcare has no standards.

FHIR, US Core, USCDI, clinical documents, claims standards, terminology systems, imaging standards, device APIs, public-health guides, research models, and other specifications provide substantial interoperability foundations.

The defensible claim is:

> Healthcare has many important standards and implementation guides, but no single universally implemented, semantically complete, institution-neutral schema serves as the uncontested canonical model of a person’s full health and lived experience across a lifetime.

This precision strengthens rather than weakens the thesis.

## Historical correction

Google Health and Microsoft HealthVault were consumer personal health-record platforms. Their adoption and closure histories are relevant to recurring utility, trust, critical mass, portability, and ecosystem design. They are not evidence that every enterprise healthcare strategy failed.

Microsoft Amalga and the later GE–Microsoft Caradigm joint venture are more relevant to enterprise integration. Their stated aim of integrating data across care-delivery silos demonstrates the legitimate need and organizational complexity of that market. The repository should use this history as a warning about complexity and dependency, not a categorical verdict on all B2B health technology.

## Mission alignment

### Product Constitution

The Product Constitution already places capture, normalization, provenance, personal improvement, control, permission, export, deletion, and meaningful refusal around the person. The consumer-first boundary is a direct implementation consequence.

### Vision

The Vision begins with a useful consumer application, calls for longitudinal records under individual control, and orders personal value before separately authorized collective benefit. It also requires transparent funding, sustainable economics, provider replacement, and founder independence.

Decision 0010 clarifies that institutional interoperability can serve that progression but cannot silently reverse it.

### Incentives

The deterministic incentive model does not reward provider choice, sponsor selection, broader permission, enterprise enrollment, payment, or intimate-data volume.

The new boundary extends that rule:

- no reward for importing from a sponsor rather than another source;
- no reward for selecting a preferred provider or connector;
- no loss of personal-core utility when an institution does not participate; and
- no contribution or governance privilege purchased through data, funding, distribution, or market access.

### Architecture

The frozen architecture already separates connectors and imports from Chronicle truth, treats vendors as unfrozen, requires structured authority, preserves provenance, and makes exports first-class.

The missing architectural statement is now explicit:

- the Living Chronicle is the provider-independent canonical model;
- external standards and institutional systems enter through versioned adapters;
- mappings preserve source, version, uncertainty, conflict, and loss;
- destination exports do not redefine internal truth; and
- provider-specific logic does not enter core domain packages.

### Governance

Strategic and economic decisions already require transparent conflicts and evidence. Decision 0008 already prohibits purchased provider defaults, roadmap control, favorable findings, private access, and authority.

The new boundary makes an institutional integration a joint product, architecture, funding, conflict, and governance relationship when it can affect schema, provider choice, connector priority, access, distribution, or roadmap.

## Funding and sponsorship alignment

The Phase 0 funding documentation is relevant earlier than operational fundraising because provider and EHR relationships may arrive as:

- infrastructure credits;
- free integration support;
- data-access assistance;
- implementation grants;
- distribution or channel partnerships;
- co-marketing;
- pro bono clinical or interoperability review;
- funded connectors;
- preferred-provider proposals;
- affiliate or referral offers;
- enterprise contracts; or
- research-linked support.

Each may create economic influence even when no cash changes hands.

The required rule is:

> Support may fund an integration. It may not purchase the meaning of the Chronicle, the order of the roadmap, preferred provider status, private access, favorable findings, or an enterprise prerequisite for personal value.

The existing provider-neutrality, infrastructure-exit, conflict, concentration, public-ledger, correction, and termination policies are sufficient foundations. They require explicit extension to EHR, clinical, exchange, and enterprise integration relationships.

## Sprint analysis

### No Sprint 6 reorder

Sprint 6 remains the next numbered sprint. Its AI-provider and sponsored-evaluation boundaries should inherit source attribution, provider neutrality, and the rule that Aster cannot convert institutional data or funding into authority.

### Sprint 8 addition

The public website should explain why the product is consumer-first without attacking providers or overstating historical evidence. It should clearly distinguish planned standards support, connectors, partnerships, and production exchange.

### Sprint 14 expansion

Sprint 14 is the correct primary implementation point for:

- standards and implementation-guide registry;
- source capability discovery;
- versioned mappings;
- mapping-loss and conflict behavior;
- provider-specific adapters;
- provenance and raw-source preservation;
- funded-connector conflicts;
- provider-neutral prioritization;
- export, replacement, migration, and teardown; and
- synthetic multi-version and partial-conformance evidence.

This is an execution clarification within the existing connector goal, not a new preceding sprint.

### Sprint 17 addition

Clinical and accessibility governance should include clinical-informatics review, provider-summary boundaries, standards-versus-safety distinctions, and partner conflict treatment.

### Sprint 18 addition

Beta evidence should test AS-0012 rather than treating consumer-first as proven. Required measures include personal utility without enterprise enrollment, import and mapping performance, reconciliation burden, connector replacement cost, provider usefulness, and funded-roadmap distortion.

### Sprint 19 addition

Public launch should publish connector capability and limitation status, active material integration relationships, funding conflicts, and correction routes without presenting sponsors as preferred providers or clinical authorities.

## Phase analysis

### Phase 0

Add the doctrine, assumption, funding inheritance, and future partnership gates. Do not represent a standards or provider runtime as complete.

### Phase 1

Require a useful private personal loop without enterprise enrollment and usable manual or portable fallbacks when connectors are unavailable.

### Phase 2

Require auditable mappings, institutional source and access boundaries, provider and sponsor conflict reporting, and proportionate specialist review.

### Phase 3

Require multiple connector stewards and at least one material connector, provider, or implementation-guide migration exercise.

### Phase 4

Keep research models and institutions purpose-specific derivatives rather than Chronicle authority, even when the same institution supplies clinical data or funding.

### Phase 5

Permit hosted, enterprise, implementation, and support economics only when the meaningfully free personal product remains complete and provider placement, ranking, referral, affiliate, and procurement incentives have separate reviewed rules.

### Phases 6–8

Ensure capital and enterprise counterparties remain bounded constituencies, and preserve the institution’s ability to migrate standards, providers, connectors, and operators across generations.

## New future workstream

A dedicated **Clinical and Enterprise Interoperability Partnerships** workstream should begin before—not after—the first material institutional integration or enterprise contract.

Its entry gates include:

- personal utility evidence;
- connector architecture;
- House of Keys and security implementation;
- clinical, interoperability, privacy, security, accessibility, legal, regulatory, procurement, and economic review;
- provider replacement;
- public relationship records; and
- Decision 0008 and Decision 0010 revalidation.

This workstream is defined in the companion roadmap document and remains future work.

## Recommendation

Merge this documentation only when the founding steward accepts the central boundary:

> The Living Chronicle remains centered on the person. Institutional healthcare is a vital source, destination, and partner connected through standards and adapters, but it does not become the automatic owner of product meaning, roadmap, incentives, permission, or governance.

After merge, proceed to Sprint 6 without renumbering. Carry the documented inheritance into Sprint 8, Sprint 14, Sprint 17, Sprint 18, Sprint 19, institutional phase reviews, and every material funding or partnership decision.
