# Funding Concentration and Continuity Policy

- **Status:** ACCEPTED PHASE 0 BASELINE
- **Revision:** 1
- **Current evidence:** no operating funding history, accepted sponsor portfolio, treasury, or independently reviewed concentration model exists

## Purpose

This policy prevents the project from replacing founder dependence with donor, sponsor, vendor, grant, or infrastructure dependence.

Funding diversity is not a goal by itself. The goal is mission continuity, truthful independence, rights protection, and the ability to refuse or lose support without abandoning the Promise.

## Concentration dimensions

Review concentration across several dimensions rather than one revenue percentage.

### Financial concentration

The share of cash or cash-equivalent support attributable to one source, related group, funding class, sector, geography, or restriction.

### In-kind concentration

The share of actual operational value, avoided cost, capacity, or critical service provided by one source.

### Restriction concentration

The share of available support limited to one purpose, program, population, deliverable, provider, or reporting condition.

### Critical-function concentration

Control or practical dependence over a critical function, regardless of financial amount.

### Decision concentration

The extent to which one source can shape priorities, provider choices, evaluation questions, public communication, timing, or institutional agenda through formal or informal leverage.

### Relationship-network concentration

Several nominally separate funders may be related through ownership, management, investment, contracts, political control, foundation control, or coordinated interest.

### Founder-subsidy concentration

Unpaid founder labor, founder-funded infrastructure, founder credit, founder-owned entities, personal guarantees, domains, accounts, contracts, and private relationships are economic support and key-person dependencies even when no invoice exists.

## Provisional financial triggers

Before meaningful funding history exists, every organizational and material in-kind relationship receives concentration review.

After a trailing-twelve-month funding base exists, use these provisional triggers:

| Share from one source or related group | Baseline response                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 10% or more                            | Publish concentration watch and review restrictions, influence, dependency, and replacement                  |
| 20% or more                            | Require written diversification, mitigation, replacement, and continuity plan                                |
| 33% or more                            | Presumptively pause additional dependency unless independent review accepts a narrow, time-bounded exception |

These are review triggers, not safety guarantees or automatic misconduct findings.

A relationship below 10% may be unacceptable. A relationship above 33% may be temporarily necessary. The record must explain affectedness, rights, reversibility, alternatives, time horizon, and exit behavior.

## Critical-function trigger

Any source that controls or materially enables a critical function is classified as concentrated regardless of financial share.

Critical functions include:

- canonical repository, branch, release, package, or signing control;
- domains, DNS, certificates, official email, or public identity;
- production credentials, administrative recovery, or emergency access;
- authentication and account access;
- primary Chronicle, source, House of Keys, receipt, audit, or backup custody;
- security monitoring, incident response, or recovery;
- primary AI or document-processing capability when no fallback exists;
- funding, fiscal, banking, payment, accounting, tax, payroll, or treasury custody;
- legal entity, fiscal sponsor, insurance, or contract authority;
- canonical public communication or community channels; and
- practical ability to migrate, export, fork, or continue without the source.

## Restrictions and available funding

Concentration calculations must distinguish:

- pledged from received;
- received from available;
- restricted from unrestricted;
- cash from in-kind;
- nominal credit from consumed value;
- recurring from one-time;
- refundable or revocable from committed;
- current period from cumulative history; and
- funder identity from related-group identity.

A project with substantial restricted support and little unrestricted operating capacity may be more dependent than total receipts suggest.

## Founder subsidy register

Before Phase 0 exit, record founder-provided economic dependencies, including:

- direct cash subsidy;
- unpaid labor and specialist work;
- personal subscriptions and service accounts;
- domains and communication channels;
- infrastructure credits;
- equipment;
- travel and event cost;
- legal, accounting, design, development, and marketing cost;
- personal guarantees or liabilities;
- private relationships necessary to operate; and
- opportunity cost where useful without false precision.

The public record may use reviewed categories and amount bands. It must not expose private bank, tax, contract, employment, or personal financial detail.

Founder subsidy does not create permanent ownership or constitutional authority. Reducing it requires legitimate replacement, not fictional accounting.

## Mitigation plan

A concentration mitigation plan should identify:

- source and related group;
- relationship class and value states;
- affected functions and groups;
- restrictions and conflicts;
- current leverage and decision exposure;
- continuity period if support ends;
- replacement candidates and qualification gaps;
- diversification actions;
- scope reduction or graceful degradation;
- reserve or runway need when later authorized;
- migration and teardown work;
- responsible steward;
- milestones and review date;
- conditions requiring containment or refusal of additional support; and
- residual risk.

Diversification must not mean accepting incompatible money to improve a ratio.

## Continuity design

Every material relationship must answer:

- What stops when support ends?
- What continues without degradation?
- Which player rights remain fully available?
- Which work is paused, canceled, narrowed, or migrated?
- Which records, code, data, credentials, assets, and institutional memory remain available?
- Who has authority and practical ability to act?
- How long can the institution operate without the source?
- What communication is required?
- Which refunds, unspent restrictions, reports, or contract duties remain?
- What sponsor recognition changes?
- What residual harm cannot be restored?
- What would justify renewal despite concentration?

## Source loss response

When a material source ends support:

1. confirm the factual scope and timing;
2. preserve relevant evidence and authority records;
3. contain new dependency and nonessential spending or consumption;
4. protect player rights, data, export, deletion, correction, safety, accessibility, and non-AI fallback;
5. activate replacement, migration, graceful degradation, or scope reduction;
6. notify responsible stewards and affected people when appropriate;
7. update public relationship, concentration, capability, and current-status records;
8. reconcile receipts, restrictions, refunds, expired credits, accounts, credentials, and residual obligations;
9. remove or correct active recognition;
10. measure operational and public-benefit impact; and
11. revalidate the funding and continuity model.

Loss of support may not be used as a pretext to pressure people into broader consent, additional disclosure, research, payment, advertising, surveillance, or loss of core rights.

## Donor and sponsor capture indicators

Potential capture signals include:

- roadmap shifts repeatedly aligning with funder interests without evidence;
- providers receiving default or preferred status because of credits or funding;
- negative findings, incidents, or limitations being delayed or softened;
- sponsor representatives gaining informal vetoes or privileged access;
- staff, maintainers, specialists, or councils becoming financially dependent on one source;
- related-party arrangements escaping ordinary review;
- grant restrictions becoming permanent product architecture;
- public reporting omitting conflicts, restrictions, dependency, or failure;
- fundraising metrics displacing personal utility and rights outcomes;
- renewal pressure creating artificial urgency;
- criticism being framed as disloyalty to funders;
- a source controlling critical accounts, keys, domains, custody, or recovery;
- inability to fork, migrate, or replace the source; or
- project survival becoming contingent on conduct the institution would otherwise reject.

No single signal proves capture. Multiple credible signals require challenge and containment review.

## Anti-capture controls

Controls may include:

- public relationship and concentration records;
- benefit limits;
- conflict disclosure and recusal;
- provider-neutral evaluation;
- independent review;
- restriction review;
- decision-class separation;
- capital limits on governance;
- multiple custodians or stewards for critical functions;
- portable contracts and export;
- replacement exercises;
- time-bounded exceptions;
- sponsor and vendor rotation;
- public outcome reporting;
- challenge, appeal, and termination paths;
- reserve and runway policy when later authorized;
- diverse unrestricted support; and
- explicit willingness to narrow scope rather than violate the Promise.

## Sector and correlated risk

A portfolio may appear diverse while remaining concentrated in one economic or political interest.

Review correlated exposure across:

- health and pharmaceutical sectors;
- insurance and employer sectors;
- AI, cloud, identity, advertising, analytics, and data sectors;
- one investor or corporate group;
- one foundation network;
- one geography or jurisdiction;
- one government or political constituency;
- one payment, fiscal, banking, or accounting rail;
- one community or contributor faction; and
- one founder or personal network.

## Reporting

Once operating funding exists, public reports should include:

- total received and available support by class;
- restricted and unrestricted shares;
- cash and in-kind distinctions;
- top-source and related-group concentration;
- sector and critical-function concentration;
- founder subsidy categories;
- dependencies and exit readiness;
- trigger status and mitigation progress;
- source loss, suspension, or termination events;
- funded work and outcome evidence; and
- unresolved risks and next review date.

Report enough for accountability without exposing individual donors or private financial source records.

## Revalidation

Review this policy:

- before the first material relationship;
- when operating funding history permits evidence-based threshold revision;
- whenever a trigger is reached;
- when a critical function becomes sponsor-dependent;
- after source loss, suspension, or termination;
- when a legal entity, fiscal sponsor, treasury, reserve, budget, or compensation system is selected;
- before binding participatory budget authority; and
- at every institutional phase-exit review.
