# Infrastructure Sponsorship and Exit Policy

[Economics home](README.md) · [Operating baseline](funding-and-sponsorship-baseline.md) · [Concentration policy](funding-concentration-and-continuity-policy.md) · [Sprint 5 security architecture](../security/README.md)

- **Status:** ACCEPTED PHASE 0 BASELINE
- **Revision:** 1
- **Operational status:** provider-independent doctrine only; no production provider is selected or authorized

## Purpose

Infrastructure credits, free services, discounted plans, pro bono operations, and sponsored tools can substantially advance a public-good project. They can also create hidden data flows, credential concentration, migration barriers, distorted provider selection, false cost assumptions, and sudden continuity risk.

This policy treats infrastructure sponsorship as a material funding and architecture relationship rather than “free” capacity.

## Scope

This policy applies to support involving:

- cloud compute, hosting, serverless functions, storage, databases, queues, and content delivery;
- identity, authentication, authorization, email, messaging, analytics, observability, search, AI, model, vector, document-processing, connector, and MCP services;
- CI, build, registry, package, artifact, code-signing, security-scanning, dependency, and release infrastructure;
- domain, DNS, certificate, email, communication, support, and incident systems;
- backups, recovery, archival, status, monitoring, and public dataset hosting;
- professional services that create, operate, migrate, or secure infrastructure; and
- credits, discounts, waived fees, reserved capacity, free tiers, equipment, licenses, or service commitments.

## Provider-neutrality rule

> Funding support does not establish technical suitability, trusted status, default placement, preferred-provider rank, or permanent selection.

Provider selection must follow the appropriate architecture, security, privacy, accessibility, procurement, legal, and governance review.

Sponsorship is disclosed as a conflict and economic input. It is not a substitute for evaluation.

## Relationship record

Every material infrastructure relationship must record:

- provider and service;
- support classification;
- nominal value;
- expected replacement cost;
- actual consumed value;
- duration and expiration;
- eligible environments and workloads;
- restrictions and prohibited uses;
- data and metadata processed;
- geographic and subprocesser boundaries when known;
- credentials, service identities, and administrative roles;
- logging, analytics, support, and incident access;
- export, deletion, retention, backup, and restoration behavior;
- portability and standards;
- dependency and critical-function status;
- replacement options;
- migration and teardown owner;
- service-level and termination terms;
- concentration implications;
- sponsor benefits;
- conflicts and reviewers;
- public evidence status; and
- unresolved security, privacy, legal, accessibility, operational, and financial gates.

Protected architecture, credentials, account identifiers, contracts, private pricing, exploit details, and operational secrets remain outside the public record.

## Valuation

Record at least three distinct values where applicable:

1. **Nominal value** — provider list or promotional value.
2. **Expected replacement value** — reasonable cost of comparable capacity the project would actually need.
3. **Consumed value** — capacity or service actually used during the reporting period.

Do not:

- count unused headline credits as cash;
- assume list price equals public benefit;
- count a service the project would not otherwise use as equivalent to avoided cost;
- omit migration, integration, compliance, monitoring, or exit cost; or
- let a large nominal grant conceal a small or negative net value.

## Security and privacy requirements

Infrastructure support must preserve Sprint 5 boundaries.

At minimum:

- private origins, databases, object stores, queues, and administrative services do not require public exposure;
- credentials and operational configuration never enter public repository records;
- environments, keys, secrets, logs, backups, and service identities remain separated;
- public development remains synthetic-only;
- provider access cannot create Chronicle, House of Keys, identity, execution, receipt, audit, or product authority;
- sponsor or provider support access is least-privilege, attributable, revocable, and reviewed;
- data processing, egress, retention, training, analytics, support, and subprocesser behavior are evaluated before protected data use;
- sponsor-provided monitoring or support does not become unrestricted surveillance;
- incident, correction, export, deletion, and restoration duties remain project-controlled; and
- a designed provider configuration is not represented as deployed or independently verified.

## Critical functions

A relationship is critical when loss, compromise, suspension, acquisition, price change, or sponsor pressure could materially impair:

- canonical repository or release control;
- domain, DNS, certificate, or public identity;
- signing keys or release provenance;
- production credentials or administrative recovery;
- authentication or account access;
- primary Chronicle, source, permission, receipt, or audit custody;
- backups or disaster recovery;
- incident detection or response;
- public support and funding custody;
- legal or fiscal authority;
- canonical communication channels; or
- the ability to export, migrate, fork, or continue the mission.

Criticality is independent of price.

## Portability and replacement

Before a sponsored service becomes a critical dependency, document:

- provider-independent domain contracts;
- data export format and completeness;
- configuration export;
- identity and stable-resource mapping;
- key and secret transfer or rotation;
- replacement candidates;
- compatibility gaps;
- migration order;
- read-only, degraded, manual, or non-AI fallback;
- maximum tolerable outage or uncertainty without false precision;
- validation and reconciliation after migration;
- sponsor and provider notification requirements;
- user and public notification requirements; and
- rollback or forward-only behavior.

Open code alone is not proof of operational portability.

## Exit triggers

Begin containment, migration, or replacement review when:

- credits or discounts are nearing expiration;
- service terms, pricing, ownership, data use, retention, training, subprocessers, jurisdiction, security, or support change materially;
- the sponsor requests prohibited recognition, influence, access, exclusivity, or reporting control;
- the provider is acquired, reorganized, sanctioned, insolvent, or materially changes strategy;
- reliability, security, privacy, accessibility, performance, deletion, export, or support evidence becomes unacceptable;
- the relationship reaches a concentration or critical-dependency trigger;
- the sponsor stops support or gives notice;
- migration evidence is stale;
- a credible alternative materially reduces risk or cost; or
- continued use would create mission, participant, or public-trust harm.

## Exit procedure

A material exit plan addresses:

1. decision authority and conflict review;
2. containment of new dependency;
3. export and integrity verification;
4. credential, key, session, webhook, token, and service-identity rotation or revocation;
5. queue, cache, index, backup, log, receipt, and derived-data boundaries;
6. replacement configuration and environment isolation;
7. parallel validation or controlled cutover;
8. public and affected-person communication;
9. provider-side retention and deletion evidence;
10. contract, billing, refund, and unused-credit treatment;
11. sponsor recognition update;
12. residual obligations and unresolved harm;
13. teardown verification; and
14. post-migration review and institutional learning.

A provider deletion confirmation proves only its bounded procedure and evidence. It is not proof that every uncontrolled downstream copy ceased to exist.

## Sponsored provider placement

Provider presets, lists, comparison screens, setup links, API-key links, recommendations, defaults, and rankings create direct conversion value.

Before any sponsored provider placement is permitted, a separate decision must define:

- the product need;
- objective eligibility and comparison criteria;
- official, community, sponsor, and unsupported-provider categories;
- ranking and default rules;
- sponsorship disclosure;
- affiliate tracking and privacy;
- security and reliability evidence;
- user choice and portability;
- conflict review;
- sponsor termination and removal;
- negative finding treatment; and
- prevention of placement inside health, consent, permission, research, or safety decisions.

Phase 0 does not authorize sponsored provider placement or referral links.

## Service-credit table exercise requirements

Before a material credit-supported service becomes operationally necessary, exercise at least:

- support ending with thirty days’ notice;
- immediate provider suspension;
- price increase after credit expiration;
- acquisition and terms change;
- export incomplete or rate-limited;
- sponsor requesting default placement;
- sponsor requesting access to usage or signup data;
- sponsor requesting removal of negative findings;
- credentials or logs exposed through support; and
- migration while maintaining correction, export, deletion, and service continuity.

A founding-steward design tabletop does not prove operational migration readiness. Multi-party exercises, implementation, and evidence remain separate holdpoints.

## Public reporting

Report material infrastructure support with:

- relationship and service;
- public value or band;
- nominal, replacement, and consumed value distinctions;
- purpose;
- data and criticality summary;
- dependency and concentration status;
- sponsor benefits;
- current capability status;
- migration and exit readiness;
- incidents or material limitations; and
- next review trigger.

Do not publish credentials, private endpoints, account identifiers, precise security controls that enable abuse, or protected contract detail.
