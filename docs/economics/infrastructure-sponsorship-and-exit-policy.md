# Infrastructure Sponsorship and Exit Policy

[Economics home](README.md) · [Operating baseline](funding-and-sponsorship-baseline.md) · [Concentration policy](funding-concentration-and-continuity-policy.md) · [Decision 0010](../decisions/0010-consumer-first-provider-independent-boundary.md) · [Sprint 5 security architecture](../security/README.md)

- **Status:** ACCEPTED PHASE 0 BASELINE
- **Revision:** 2 — proposed provider and institutional-integration extension
- **Operational status:** provider-independent doctrine only; no production provider, EHR, connector, clinical partner, or infrastructure provider is selected or authorized

## Purpose

Infrastructure credits, free services, discounted plans, pro bono operations, funded connectors, EHR or exchange integration assistance, distribution support, and sponsored tools can substantially advance a public-good project. They can also create hidden data flows, credential concentration, migration barriers, distorted provider selection, schema capture, connector priority, false cost assumptions, and sudden continuity risk.

This policy treats infrastructure and institutional-integration sponsorship as a material funding, product, architecture, and governance relationship rather than “free” capacity.

## Scope

This policy applies to support involving:

- cloud compute, hosting, serverless functions, storage, databases, queues, and content delivery;
- identity, authentication, authorization, email, messaging, analytics, observability, search, AI, model, vector, document-processing, connector, and MCP services;
- EHRs, health information exchanges or networks, payer APIs, laboratory, pharmacy, device, imaging, claims, clinical-document, terminology, mapping, interface-engine, and interoperability services;
- FHIR, US Core, USCDI, SMART, bulk-data, clinical-document, terminology, proprietary API, and implementation-guide development or conformance support;
- provider, health-system, payer, laboratory, pharmacy, device, research, public-health, employer, or enterprise integration and distribution assistance;
- CI, build, registry, package, artifact, code-signing, security-scanning, dependency, and release infrastructure;
- domain, DNS, certificate, email, communication, support, and incident systems;
- backups, recovery, archival, status, monitoring, and public dataset hosting;
- professional services that create, operate, migrate, validate, secure, or evaluate infrastructure, mappings, or integrations; and
- credits, discounts, waived fees, reserved capacity, free tiers, equipment, licenses, data access, implementation labor, distribution, or service commitments.

## Provider-neutrality rule

> Funding support does not establish technical suitability, clinical suitability, trusted status, source authority, default placement, preferred-provider rank, preferred connector, canonical schema authority, or permanent selection.

Provider, EHR, exchange, implementation-guide, mapping, connector, and infrastructure selection must follow the appropriate product, architecture, security, privacy, accessibility, interoperability, clinical, procurement, legal, economic, and governance review.

Sponsorship is disclosed as a conflict and economic input. It is not a substitute for evaluation.

A sponsored institutional system remains an external source, destination, or partner connected through a versioned adapter. It does not become Chronicle truth, House of Keys authority, product identity, clinical endorsement, or a prerequisite for the complete personal-value loop.

## Relationship record

Every material infrastructure or institutional-integration relationship must record:

- provider, institution, product, and service;
- support classification;
- nominal value;
- expected replacement value;
- actual consumed value;
- duration and expiration;
- eligible environments, populations, sources, destinations, and workloads;
- restrictions and prohibited uses;
- requested and approved product, integration, recognition, distribution, or placement benefits;
- applicable standards, profiles, implementation guides, terminologies, mappings, versions, and conformance claims;
- proprietary extensions, local behavior, unsupported fields, mapping loss, ambiguity, and known conflicts;
- data and metadata processed;
- geographic and subprocesser boundaries when known;
- credentials, service identities, administrative roles, implementation access, and support access;
- logging, analytics, training, model, support, incident, and evaluation access;
- export, deletion, retention, backup, correction, provenance, and restoration behavior;
- source authority and Chronicle mapping boundaries;
- portability and standards;
- dependency and critical-function status;
- replacement options;
- migration and teardown owner;
- service-level and termination terms;
- concentration, distribution, data-access, and roadmap-attention implications;
- sponsor benefits;
- conflicts, recusals, evaluator independence, and reviewers;
- public evidence status; and
- unresolved security, privacy, legal, accessibility, clinical, interoperability, procurement, operational, and financial gates.

Protected architecture, credentials, account identifiers, contracts, private pricing, proprietary mappings, exploit details, patient or provider records, and operational secrets remain outside the public record.

## Valuation

Record at least three distinct values where applicable:

1. **Nominal value** — provider list or promotional value.
2. **Expected replacement value** — reasonable cost of comparable capacity, integration labor, data access, or distribution the project would actually need.
3. **Consumed value** — capacity, service, labor, access, or integration actually used during the reporting period.

Do not:

- count unused headline credits as cash;
- assume list price equals public benefit;
- count a service or integration the project would not otherwise use as equivalent to avoided cost;
- omit migration, mapping, integration, validation, compliance, monitoring, support, or exit cost;
- treat access to a provider, dataset, implementation guide, distribution channel, or enterprise market as costless influence; or
- let a large nominal grant conceal a small or negative net value.

## Security and privacy requirements

Infrastructure and institutional support must preserve Sprint 5 and House of Keys boundaries.

At minimum:

- private origins, databases, object stores, queues, and administrative services do not require public exposure;
- credentials, patient identifiers, provider configuration, private endpoints, mappings, and operational configuration never enter public repository records;
- environments, keys, secrets, logs, backups, service identities, tenants, and institutional integrations remain separated;
- public development remains synthetic-only;
- provider, EHR, exchange, sponsor, or support access cannot create Chronicle, House of Keys, identity, execution, receipt, audit, clinical, research, or product authority;
- sponsor or provider support access is least-privilege, attributable, revocable, time-bounded, and reviewed;
- data processing, egress, retention, training, analytics, support, and subprocesser behavior are evaluated before protected data use;
- sponsor-provided monitoring, patient matching, analytics, or support does not become unrestricted surveillance or secondary use;
- source retrieval, mapping, correction, receipt, incident, export, deletion, and restoration duties remain project-controlled; and
- a designed provider configuration, mapping, conformance result, or data flow is not represented as deployed, clinically safe, semantically complete, or independently verified.

## Critical functions

A relationship is critical when loss, compromise, suspension, acquisition, price change, standards change, endpoint restriction, or sponsor pressure could materially impair:

- canonical repository or release control;
- domain, DNS, certificate, or public identity;
- signing keys or release provenance;
- production credentials or administrative recovery;
- authentication or account access;
- primary Chronicle, source, permission, receipt, or audit custody;
- a material provider, EHR, exchange, payer, laboratory, pharmacy, device, terminology, mapping, patient-matching, or connector path;
- the ability to interpret, import, correct, export, migrate, or reconcile a material class of records;
- backups or disaster recovery;
- incident detection or response;
- public support and funding custody;
- legal or fiscal authority;
- canonical communication channels; or
- the ability to export, migrate, fork, continue the personal product, or preserve the mission.

Criticality is independent of price.

## Portability and replacement

Before a sponsored service or institutional integration becomes a critical dependency, document:

- provider-independent domain contracts;
- source and destination data export format and completeness;
- raw or faithful source preservation;
- standards, profile, implementation-guide, terminology, and mapping versions;
- configuration and mapping export;
- identity, source, subject, recipient, and stable-resource mapping;
- key and secret transfer or rotation;
- replacement candidates;
- compatibility gaps, unsupported fields, and semantic loss;
- migration order;
- read-only, degraded, manual, document, or non-AI fallback;
- maximum tolerable outage or uncertainty without false precision;
- validation and reconciliation after migration;
- sponsor, provider, institution, and affected-person notification requirements;
- user and public notification requirements; and
- rollback or forward-only behavior.

Open code, standards conformance, or source export alone is not proof of operational or semantic portability.

## Exit triggers

Begin containment, migration, or replacement review when:

- credits, discounts, implementation assistance, data access, distribution, or support are nearing expiration;
- service terms, pricing, ownership, data use, retention, training, subprocessers, jurisdiction, security, support, standards, profiles, endpoints, registration, documentation, access fees, or conformance behavior change materially;
- the sponsor or institution requests prohibited recognition, influence, schema authority, connector priority, access, exclusivity, reporting control, provider placement, clinical claims, or favorable findings;
- the provider, vendor, sponsor, or institution is acquired, reorganized, sanctioned, insolvent, leaves a market, or materially changes strategy;
- reliability, security, privacy, accessibility, performance, semantic fidelity, correction, deletion, export, documentation, support, or interoperability evidence becomes unacceptable;
- an endpoint, implementation guide, terminology, or mapping version becomes unsupported;
- the relationship reaches a concentration, distribution, source-reach, roadmap-attention, or critical-dependency trigger;
- the sponsor stops support or gives notice;
- migration, mapping, or conformance evidence is stale;
- a credible alternative materially reduces risk, cost, lock-in, or semantic loss; or
- continued use would create mission, participant, clinical, provider-neutrality, or public-trust harm.

## Exit procedure

A material exit plan addresses:

1. decision authority and conflict review;
2. containment of new dependency and new synchronization;
3. source, export, mapping, and integrity verification;
4. credential, key, session, webhook, token, endpoint, certificate, and service-identity rotation or revocation;
5. queue, cache, index, backup, log, receipt, mapping, terminology, and derived-data boundaries;
6. replacement configuration, adapter, and environment isolation;
7. parallel validation or controlled cutover;
8. correction, provenance, conflict, and unsupported-field reconciliation;
9. public and affected-person communication;
10. provider-side retention, revocation, access, and deletion evidence;
11. contract, billing, refund, unused-credit, restricted-value, and residual-support treatment;
12. sponsor recognition and provider-status update;
13. residual obligations, downstream copies, unsupported records, and unresolved harm;
14. teardown verification; and
15. post-migration review and institutional learning.

A provider deletion confirmation or successful standard export proves only its bounded procedure and evidence. It is not proof that every uncontrolled downstream copy ceased to exist or that every semantic distinction survived migration.

## Sponsored provider and connector placement

Provider presets, lists, comparison screens, setup links, API-key links, endpoint directories, implementation guides, recommendations, defaults, rankings, certification marks, and connector order create direct conversion and market value.

Before any sponsored provider or connector placement is permitted, a separate decision must define:

- the person-facing product need;
- objective eligibility and comparison criteria;
- official, community, sponsor, experimental, unsupported, and retired categories;
- ranking, ordering, recommendation, and default rules;
- sponsorship disclosure;
- affiliate tracking and privacy;
- standards, mapping, security, reliability, accessibility, support, and semantic-fidelity evidence;
- user choice and portability;
- conflict review;
- sponsor termination and removal;
- negative finding treatment;
- correction and appeal;
- prevention of placement inside health, consent, permission, research, safety, provider-selection, or clinical decisions; and
- proof that the complete personal product remains usable without the placed provider.

Phase 0 does not authorize sponsored provider placement, preferred connectors, referral links, implementation-guide certification, or enterprise recommendations.

## Service-credit and integration table exercise requirements

Before a material credit-supported service or institutional integration becomes operationally necessary, exercise at least:

- support or data access ending with thirty days’ notice;
- immediate provider, endpoint, or account suspension;
- price increase after credit expiration;
- acquisition and terms change;
- export incomplete, semantically lossy, or rate-limited;
- implementation guide or terminology version change;
- local EHR behavior diverging from claimed conformance;
- sponsor requesting default placement, preferred-provider status, or connector priority;
- sponsor requesting access to usage, signup, patient, provider, or mapping data;
- sponsor requesting removal of negative findings, incidents, or limitations;
- credentials, logs, mappings, or records exposed through implementation support;
- endpoint registration or contract terms becoming information-blocking concerns;
- the enterprise relationship ending while personal users still depend on imports or exports; and
- migration while maintaining source provenance, correction, permission, receipts, export, deletion, and service continuity.

A founding-steward design tabletop does not prove operational migration readiness, clinical safety, interoperability, or semantic fidelity. Multi-party exercises, implementation, and evidence remain separate holdpoints.

## Public reporting

Report material infrastructure and institutional support with:

- relationship, institution, product, and service;
- public value or band;
- nominal, replacement, and consumed value distinctions;
- purpose;
- standards, mapping, data, and criticality summary;
- personal-value and affected-group rationale;
- dependency, distribution, source-reach, and concentration status;
- sponsor benefits and explicitly denied benefits;
- provider, connector, source-authority, and placement status;
- current capability and review status;
- mapping, migration, and exit readiness;
- incidents, negative findings, semantic limitations, or material gaps; and
- next review trigger.

Do not publish credentials, private endpoints, account identifiers, patient or provider records, proprietary mappings, precise security controls that enable abuse, protected contract detail, or nonpublic interoperability findings.
