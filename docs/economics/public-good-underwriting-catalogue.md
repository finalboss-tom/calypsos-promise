## Purpose

This catalogue defines the preferred way for institutions and individuals to support Calypso’s Promise: fund a bounded public-good need with inspectable work, ordinary project review, truthful outcome reporting, and no purchased authority.

The catalogue replaces generic prominence-first sponsor tiers as the canonical model.

## Underwriting principles

Every opportunity must:

- identify a real public-good need rather than invent work to attract funding;
- state the intended outcome and affected groups;
- preserve the Product Constitution and public-domain commitment;
- identify work, evidence, review, and acceptance boundaries;
- state what support does and does not purchase;
- permit partial support only when partial value is real and reportable;
- disclose conflicts, dependencies, and specialist holdpoints;
- identify expiration, replacement, correction, and failure behavior;
- link to a public issue or roadmap record where appropriate; and
- report delivery and measured outcome separately.

## Opportunity classes

### Security and privacy underwriting

May support:

- independent threat-model review;
- penetration testing or secure-design review;
- encryption and key-management review;
- privacy engineering and data-flow review;
- deletion, backup, restore, and incident-response exercises;
- secure build and supply-chain review;
- responsible-disclosure infrastructure;
- public synthetic abuse-case development; and
- remediation of accepted public findings.

Requirements:

- evaluator independence and conflicts are explicit;
- the sponsor cannot control findings or publication;
- no private vulnerability details enter the public ledger;
- no security approval is claimed merely because work was funded; and
- remediation and retest obligations remain distinct from the initial review.

### Accessibility underwriting

May support:

- screen-reader, keyboard, reduced-motion, high-contrast, cognitive-accessibility, and low-bandwidth review;
- assistive-technology testing;
- captioning, transcripts, plain-language summaries, and alternative formats;
- accessibility research with appropriate participant protections;
- remediation and regression testing; and
- accessible contributor and documentation paths.

Requirements:

- disabled people and relevant specialists are represented where appropriate;
- a sponsor cannot define accessibility success through a checklist alone;
- findings and unresolved limitations remain publishable; and
- accessibility features remain part of the meaningfully free product.

### Synthetic Data Commons underwriting

May support:

- synthetic Living Chronicle and House of Keys fixtures;
- inclusive interaction and accessibility contexts;
- public benchmark datasets;
- schema and validator development;
- dataset cards, provenance, limitations, and versioning;
- secure generation procedures that do not derive fixtures from real private records;
- hosting and distribution; and
- multilingual, temporal, unit, provenance, conflict, correction, export, deletion, and consent scenarios.

Requirements:

- all records are fictional or explicitly public and rights-cleared;
- dataset quality, representativeness, and limitation claims remain challengeable;
- sponsor branding does not become data authority; and
- the public-domain or actual third-party rights treatment is explicit.

### Contributor Fellowship underwriting

May support bounded work by contributors, maintainers, translators, designers, documentarians, reviewers, or specialists.

Potential scopes:

- documentation and onboarding;
- issue reproduction and validation;
- accessibility implementation;
- synthetic data and testing;
- localization;
- maintenance and dependency work;
- governance and institutional archives;
- public website and prologue work;
- security remediation; and
- maintainership development.

Requirements:

- compensation is separately authorized before payment;
- the sponsor does not unilaterally select recipients;
- selection criteria, conflicts, scope, review, and outcomes are public;
- Fellowship progress or contribution credit is not purchased by the sponsor or recipient;
- participation does not create universal governance authority; and
- funded work remains under ordinary repository review.

### Infrastructure and operations underwriting

May support:

- hosting;
- CI and build minutes;
- artifact storage;
- observability;
- code signing;
- domain, DNS, and email operations;
- backups and recovery testing;
- accessibility-testing infrastructure;
- security scanning;
- documentation hosting; and
- public synthetic dataset hosting.

Requirements:

- the relationship follows the infrastructure sponsorship and exit policy;
- nominal credits are distinguished from consumed value;
- data processing and credentials are documented privately and summarized publicly;
- provider selection remains separate from sponsorship; and
- replacement, export, migration, and teardown are explicit.

### Legal and commons stewardship underwriting

May support:

- public-domain dedication review and migration;
- contributor dedication and provenance review;
- privacy, contract, nonprofit, fiscal sponsorship, entity, governance, trademark, patent, and public-benefit legal analysis;
- public policy and decision drafting; and
- independent review of institutional claims.

Requirements:

- legal advice and privileged source material remain private;
- the public record describes the question, relationship, material decision, conflicts, and outcome without publishing protected advice;
- no legal approval or tax status is claimed beyond the actual work; and
- a funder cannot control the legal conclusion.

### Governance and institutional-resilience underwriting

May support:

- governance simulations and tabletop exercises;
- succession and founder-absence exercises;
- conflict, recusal, removal, appeal, and ombuds design;
- authority and key-person registers;
- community-participation pilots;
- institutional archives;
- independent governance review; and
- public funding and concentration review.

Requirements:

- funding does not buy governance authority;
- the sponsor is represented only as a stakeholder when legitimately affected, not because of capital alone;
- simulations publish failure and capture findings; and
- constitutional, safety, and affected-party protections remain superior to sponsor preference.

### Clinical and safety review underwriting

May support future qualified review of:

- claim taxonomy;
- emergency and crisis routing;
- high-risk content boundaries;
- medication, pregnancy, injury, eating-disorder, and other sensitive domains;
- adverse-event processes; and
- content and interaction review.

Requirements:

- no sponsor product receives recommendation or privileged placement;
- commercial health, pharmaceutical, insurance, employer, device, or clinical conflicts receive heightened review;
- the funder cannot control findings or public limitations;
- the work does not authorize clinical product behavior before its roadmap gate; and
- qualified reviewers and affected-party safeguards remain required.

### Research Commons underwriting

This category is visible for long-horizon planning but **not open during Phase 0**.

Future support may fund secure analysis environments, participant governance, methodology, study review, public reporting, or shared research infrastructure only after Research Commons gates are satisfied.

Research funding cannot be accepted under another category to bypass study-specific purpose, permission, withdrawal, participant representation, publication, conflict, adverse-event, and benefit requirements.

### Public communication and education underwriting

May support:

- plain-language explainers;
- white papers;
- public-domain history and provenance;
- translations;
- accessible media;
- public status and trust-center reporting; and
- public educational content about health-data ownership, consent, provenance, and individual control.

Requirements:

- funding and editorial conflicts are visible;
- sponsor claims are not repeated without evidence;
- educational content is not disguised advertising;
- modern adaptation affiliation is not implied; and
- health claims remain within accepted evidence and review boundaries.

## Opportunity record lifecycle

An opportunity may be:

- **draft** — being prepared and not open;
- **reviewable** — public for comment but not open to funds;
- **open** — approved support path exists;
- **partially supported** — support received but full target remains open;
- **funded** — accepted support meets the defined funding need;
- **in delivery** — governed work is active;
- **completed** — bounded deliverables are accepted;
- **outcome review** — delivery is complete and outcome evidence is being assessed;
- **expired** — the opportunity ended without ordinary completion;
- **paused** — contained pending review;
- **canceled** — no longer pursued;
- **superseded** — replaced by another opportunity; or
- **closed** — obligations and reporting are complete.

## Opportunity design checklist

Before publishing an opportunity, confirm:

1. The work is already justified by mission, roadmap, risk, or evidence.
2. The support request does not create unnecessary scope.
3. The amount or in-kind need is estimated honestly.
4. Partial funding behavior is clear.
5. Restrictions the project can and cannot accept are explicit.
6. The work owner and acceptance authority are identified.
7. Specialist and independent-review gaps are visible.
8. Sponsor benefits are limited to the recognition policy.
9. Provider, related-party, and dependency conflicts are addressed.
10. Delivery and outcome evidence are distinct.
11. Failure, delay, cancellation, unspent value, refund, and replacement behavior are defined.
12. The public/private information boundary is safe.
13. The opportunity expires or revalidates rather than remaining open indefinitely.
14. The project can decline an offer that would distort the work.
