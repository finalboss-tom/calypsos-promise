# Sponsor Benefit and Recognition Policy

- **Status:** ACCEPTED PHASE 0 BASELINE
- **Revision:** 1
- **Operational status:** recognition doctrine only; no sponsor, endorsement, affiliate, or promotion program is active

## Purpose

This policy defines what Calypso’s Promise may offer in return for support and what no supporter may purchase.

Recognition exists to make institutional relationships and public-good support legible. It is not a storefront for authority, product influence, health recommendations, private information, or preferential treatment.

## Recognition principles

Recognition must be:

- truthful about the support actually received;
- proportionate to the relationship and public-information need;
- visually and semantically distinct from product recommendations;
- accessible in narrative and direct modes;
- accompanied by relationship status and evidence where material;
- correctable and removable when the relationship ends or the record changes;
- compatible with rights-cleared third-party names, logos, and marks;
- free of hidden tracking, health profiling, or undisclosed referral attribution; and
- subordinate to user comprehension, safety, privacy, performance, and product purpose.

## Permitted benefits

A reviewed relationship may receive one or more explicitly listed benefits.

### Public acknowledgment

- public name;
- optional rights-cleared logo or wordmark;
- relationship classification;
- neutral description of support provided;
- start, end, and review dates;
- support value or approved public amount band;
- link to the sponsor’s ordinary public website; and
- link to the canonical project funding record.

### Artifact and outcome attribution

- acknowledgment on a funded public report, release, dataset, audit, fellowship cohort, documentation area, or public capability;
- link from the funded artifact to the funding record;
- inclusion in funding, expenditure, delivery, and outcome reports;
- factual announcement of acceptance, delivery, completion, renewal, suspension, or termination; and
- publication of measured outcomes, limitations, failures, and corrections.

### Public briefings

A sponsor may attend a public briefing, demonstration, or report presentation available under comparable terms to other supporters or the public.

A briefing does not create confidential product access, early health functionality, private user access, or decision authority.

### Factual review

Before publication, a sponsor may review factual statements specifically about:

- its legal or public name;
- support amount or amount band;
- supplied service or credit;
- dates;
- logo rights;
- agreed restrictions; and
- other objective terms directly describing the sponsor.

Factual review does not include editorial approval, suppression of criticism, control of findings, or a veto over project conclusions.

### Renewal recognition

Long-running support may receive renewal, multiyear, or founding-underwriter attribution when truthful.

A duration label does not create permanent placement, governance seniority, product priority, or a vested right to future recognition.

## Prohibited benefits

No relationship may purchase or condition the following.

### Personal and private information

- personal or aggregate private health information;
- Chronicle, source, permission, receipt, account, signup, contact, donor, participant, contributor, partner, or support records;
- access to private correspondence, interviews, analytics, moderation, conduct, incident, or security evidence;
- identity enrichment, lead generation, audience matching, or health-interest profiling; or
- public or private lists of people who did or did not donate, consent, participate, or use a feature.

### Product and health influence

- placement inside the Living Chronicle, House of Keys, consent, permission, receipt, export, deletion, correction, safety, crisis, accessibility, or health-guidance flows;
- health, clinical, scientific, behavioral, medication, device, insurance, employer, or research recommendations;
- default provider, preferred vendor, featured product, comparative ranking, or hidden preselection;
- sponsor-provided claims presented as project findings;
- weakened evidence, review, uncertainty, provenance, or safety language; or
- artificial scarcity, urgency, matching pressure, or progression linked to health, consent, research, or donation.

### Roadmap and implementation control

- guaranteed roadmap priority;
- guaranteed implementation or release date;
- issue closure, acceptance criteria, merge, review, release, or recall authority;
- private feature requirements outside the governed-work record;
- suppression of alternatives, forks, interoperability, portability, or replacement;
- exclusivity over a zone, character, protocol, schema, public dataset, or public-domain artifact; or
- veto over correction, rollback, deprecation, migration, or termination.

### Governance and institutional authority

- votes, seats, vetoes, delegation, constitutional authority, emergency powers, or removal immunity;
- founder, maintainer, specialist, council, operator, auditor, ombuds, or representative office;
- Fellowship, Renown, Laurels, contribution credit, reputation, credentials, or permanent status;
- authority over treasury, budget, public-good allocation, compensation, research, or provider selection; or
- exemption from conflicts, recusals, conduct, security, incident, correction, appeal, or challenge processes.

### Findings and review

- favorable benchmark, security, accessibility, clinical, scientific, privacy, governance, policy, legal, or outcome findings;
- suppression, delay, alteration, pre-approval, or selective release of negative findings;
- a claim of independent review when the reviewer is the sponsor, direct implementer, direct beneficiary, or commercially dependent party;
- the right to define success only through a metric controlled by the sponsor or funded implementer; or
- immunity from public reporting of limitations, incidents, disputes, termination, or residual obligations.

### Public-domain and intellectual-property control

- ownership of public-domain project foundations;
- exclusive rights to code, schemas, documentation, lore, governance records, synthetic fixtures, or public methods committed to the commons;
- a right to prohibit forks, commercial reuse, migration, or independent hosting; or
- project ownership of the sponsor’s name, logo, trademark, third-party materials, confidential information, or supplied assets beyond the actual permission granted.

## Placement rules

### Website

Sponsor recognition belongs on dedicated Support, Funding, Commons, public report, or artifact-attribution surfaces.

It must not:

- displace the primary GitHub, Promise, current-status, safety, privacy, accessibility, or product-navigation actions;
- appear as native health advice;
- obscure capability status;
- create a pay-to-win visual hierarchy;
- use deceptive countdowns or fake scarcity;
- load tracking pixels, cross-site advertising identifiers, or session replay; or
- reduce performance or accessibility of essential information.

### Repository

Recognition may appear in:

- the canonical funding record;
- a funded artifact;
- release or completion evidence;
- an economics index; or
- a funding transparency section.

The root README must not become an unbounded sponsor wall. A compact link to canonical funding records is preferred when relationships grow.

### Product applications

Sponsor recognition is excluded from private Chronicle, permission, receipt, correction, export, deletion, safety, and core gameplay flows unless a future product decision establishes a bounded public attribution surface that remains optional, non-personalized, non-tracking, and unrelated to health or permission choices.

### Developer tooling

A funded dependency, provider preset, tool, or integration cannot receive ranking, default selection, or trusted status solely because of funding.

Any provider listing must use a separate provider-selection and evaluation policy with sponsorship disclosed as a conflict.

## Labels and disclaimers

Material recognition should use plain language such as:

- “Supported by”;
- “Public-good underwriting provided by”;
- “Infrastructure credits provided by”;
- “Independent review funded by”; or
- “Pro bono review contributed by.”

Avoid:

- “Official health partner”;
- “Approved provider”;
- “Trusted medical partner”;
- “Recommended by Calypso’s Promise”;
- “Exclusive partner”; or
- any wording that implies safety, health, scientific, legal, privacy, or product endorsement beyond actual evidence.

Where useful, include:

> Funding recognition does not grant access to personal information, product authority, research access, governance power, or control over findings.

## Sponsor-supplied copy and assets

Sponsor descriptions and assets are REVIEW material until approved.

Requirements:

- use the minimum factual copy needed;
- independently verify material claims before repeating them;
- do not publish unverified performance, safety, price, market, health, or technical claims;
- do not accept scripts or embeds that create tracking or remote-content dependency;
- store only rights-cleared assets appropriate for public distribution;
- record the asset owner and permitted use;
- preserve the actual trademark and copyright notices; and
- remove or update assets when permission, relationship, branding, or factual status changes.

## Benefit register

Every material funding record must enumerate the exact benefits offered.

A phrase such as “standard sponsor benefits” is insufficient unless the referenced standard revision is linked and every exception is recorded.

Benefits outside this policy require a reviewed exception or new decision before activation.

## Termination and correction

When a relationship pauses, expires, terminates, or is corrected:

- active promotional placement is removed or changed promptly;
- historical funding records remain with truthful status;
- funded artifacts retain accurate historical attribution unless removal is legally or ethically required;
- broken or unsafe links are disabled;
- no sponsor may require erasure of material institutional history as a condition of ending support;
- protected details remain protected; and
- disputes, residual obligations, refunds, replacement, and restoration are recorded through reviewed public derivatives.

## Validation questions

Before activating recognition, reviewers must be able to answer:

1. What support was actually received?
2. What exact benefits are offered?
3. Could an ordinary user mistake this for health, provider, product, or institutional endorsement?
4. Is any tracking, personalization, lead generation, or private-data access involved?
5. Does the placement distort core navigation, safety, permission, or current-status comprehension?
6. Are conflicts, restrictions, duration, and dependency visible?
7. Can the recognition be corrected, removed, or migrated without harming the product?
8. Can the project publish unfavorable findings, incidents, limitations, and termination?
9. Are all sponsor-supplied assets rights-cleared and independently reviewed?
10. Does the relationship remain compatible with the public-domain commons and provider replaceability?
