- **Information class:** PUBLIC synthetic evidence only
- **Participants:** founding steward acting through documented roles
- **Evidence boundary:** these exercises test doctrine and decision paths; they do not prove legal, financial, multi-party, provider, security, accounting, or operational readiness

## Method

Each exercise records:

- synthetic facts;
- decision class and affected groups;
- applicable boundaries;
- actions and authority;
- public and private record treatment;
- containment, correction, restoration, and exit;
- result; and
- remaining implementation or specialist holdpoints.

All organizations, offers, amounts, providers, people, incidents, and outcomes are fictional.

## TTX-FND-001 — AI credits for default placement

### Synthetic facts

An AI provider offers $200,000 in nominal model credits for twelve months. It asks to become the default Aster provider, receive first position in setup documentation, and obtain an attributed registration link. The project has not selected a production AI provider.

### Analysis

- **Decision classes:** strategic and economic; AI, security, privacy, technical, and product.
- **Affected groups:** future players, contributors, operators, alternative providers, and people whose information could leave the system.
- **Conflicts:** the provider being evaluated is offering material value tied to selection and conversion.
- **Authority gap:** Phase 0 does not authorize sponsored provider placement, affiliate revenue, production provider selection, or private-data egress.

### Response

1. Separate the credit offer from technical evaluation.
2. Decline default placement, preferential ranking, and attributed referral behavior.
3. Record the offer privately and publish a material derivative only if the relationship proceeds or the offer materially affects public understanding.
4. Require a provider-neutral AI and security review before any selection.
5. Treat nominal credit, expected replacement cost, and actual consumption separately.
6. Require export, migration, expiration, and provider-loss table exercises.

### Result

The placement request is declined. The credits remain deferred pending a separate provider selection and infrastructure review. No sponsor benefit activates.

### Holdpoints

- Sprint 6 AI-governance contracts;
- production provider selection;
- privacy, security, legal, and data-processing review;
- affiliate and endorsement decision; and
- operational migration evidence.

## TTX-FND-002 — Commercial health-content sponsorship

### Synthetic facts

A pharmaceutical company offers $75,000 to fund a medication-education quest. It asks for product naming, approval of final copy, engagement analytics, and category exclusivity.

### Analysis

The offer would place a commercial funder inside health content, create approval authority, expose behavioral analytics, and block alternatives. Funding cannot purchase health recommendations, content authority, private analytics, or exclusivity.

### Response

1. Decline the proposed consideration.
2. Do not create a public work item shaped around the product.
3. Explain that future clinical or educational work requires independent scope, qualified review, accepted claim taxonomy, and no sponsor product placement.
4. Preserve private negotiation; publish a derivative only if the relationship becomes publicly material.

### Result

The relationship is declined. A future unrestricted grant for independently governed medication-safety infrastructure could be reviewed separately, but no product-specific commitment is offered.

### Holdpoints

- Sprint 17 clinical, safety, and accessibility governance;
- qualified clinical and conflict review;
- health-content funding policy revalidation; and
- no production health recommendation before accepted evidence.

## TTX-FND-003 — Cloud credits end with thirty days’ notice

### Synthetic facts

A cloud sponsor hosts public documentation, CI artifacts, synthetic datasets, and an internal preview. It gives thirty days’ notice that credits will end and ordinary pricing will begin.

### Analysis

The relationship supports public functions and build capacity. It is economically material even without private health data. Continuity depends on export, configuration, DNS, credentials, artifacts, and replacement capacity.

### Response

1. Freeze new sponsor-specific dependencies.
2. Confirm actual consumption, replacement cost, expiration, and billing exposure.
3. Activate migration or approved fallback for documentation and CI.
4. Export data and configurations; rotate credentials and remove sponsor support access.
5. Verify public builds and synthetic artifacts after cutover.
6. Update capability, relationship, concentration, recognition, and current-status records.
7. Record residual gaps and post-migration review.

### Result

Public direct-mode documentation remains available through a reduced approved fallback. Optional previews pause. No player right or private-data function is affected.

### Holdpoints

- implemented migration automation;
- multi-party operational exercise;
- approved replacement provider; and
- budget and reserve authority.

## TTX-FND-004 — Donation for a governance seat

### Synthetic facts

A donor offers $500,000 and requests a permanent council seat plus veto rights over partnerships and public communications.

### Analysis

The request directly purchases strategic and governance authority and creates permanent capital privilege. It conflicts with affectedness, competence, revocability, capital limits, and founder-independent constitutional governance.

### Response

1. Decline the governance consideration.
2. State that funding does not create a seat, vote, veto, emergency power, or immunity.
3. Offer no informal observer status that recreates the same influence privately.
4. Consider an unrestricted donation only if the donor accepts ordinary recognition and no authority.

### Result

The conditional offer is declined. Any revised unconditional offer receives ordinary material funding review.

### Holdpoints

None for the decline. Future council participation follows published governance criteria independent of donation.

## TTX-FND-005 — Sponsor requests signup emails

### Synthetic facts

A sponsor underwriting public outreach asks for the email addresses of people joining the Founding Expedition, claiming it needs attribution and co-marketing evidence.

### Analysis

Signup records are private, purpose-limited source data. Sponsorship does not authorize access, lead generation, audience matching, or repurposing.

### Response

1. Decline the request immediately.
2. Confirm that no data was shared.
3. Review whether the request violates accepted terms or indicates incompatible expectations.
4. Pause new promotional benefits while reviewing the relationship.
5. Publish a safe derivative if suspension or termination becomes material.
6. Audit access, forms, analytics, logs, and outbound links for unintended disclosure.

### Result

No data is shared. The relationship is conditionally paused pending written confirmation that private-data access and lead generation are excluded. Repetition triggers termination.

### Holdpoints

- operational signup retention and deletion evidence;
- partner-access audit; and
- incident response if any disclosure occurred.

## TTX-FND-006 — Delay of negative findings

### Synthetic facts

A foundation funds a public synthetic benchmark. Results materially underperform the target. The funder requests a three-month delay until a more favorable rerun is available.

### Analysis

The funder may fact-check its own description but cannot suppress or selectively delay findings to protect reputation. The project must distinguish methodological correction from outcome manipulation.

### Response

1. Preserve the original evidence and methods.
2. Determine whether a genuine methodological defect warrants correction.
3. Publish the current result, limitations, and planned rerun on the evidence-appropriate schedule.
4. Pause unspent restricted funds while reviewing publication rights.
5. Escalate unresolved interference through conflict and termination review.
6. Record unspent value, refund or completion options, and concentration effects.

### Result

Publication independence is preserved. The funder’s request and project response receive a reviewed public derivative if material. Continued interference terminates the relationship.

### Holdpoints

- independent methods review;
- exact grant terms held privately; and
- accounting treatment of unspent funds.

## TTX-FND-007 — Restricted grant cannot complete

### Synthetic facts

A $50,000 restricted grant funds a synthetic dataset and accessibility benchmark. After $28,000 is consumed, an essential external standard changes and the original deliverable is no longer useful.

### Analysis

Spending the remainder to satisfy the original label would create waste and misleading completion. The project lacks authority to redirect restricted funds unilaterally.

### Response

1. Pause spending and delivery claims.
2. Record consumed, available, committed, and unspent value separately.
3. Publish a variance and public-benefit assessment.
4. Propose return, amendment, or a narrower replacement outcome.
5. Review conflicts and whether the new scope was already justified independently.
6. Preserve all accepted work and explain what remains usable.

### Result

The project chooses a reviewed grant amendment only if the revised outcome is independently justified and the funder accepts continued publication independence. Otherwise, unspent funds are returned.

### Holdpoints

- authorized custodian and accounting process;
- contract amendment authority; and
- public outcome review after closure.

## TTX-FND-008 — Exclusivity demand

### Synthetic facts

An infrastructure sponsor offers multiyear support but requires exclusive use of its service category and prohibits public comparison with competitors.

### Analysis

The restriction impairs provider replacement, evidence, portability, public criticism, and fork continuity. It creates capture beyond recognition.

### Response

1. Decline exclusivity and comparison restrictions.
2. Offer a nonexclusive relationship under provider-neutral evaluation.
3. Require public disclosure of any narrow compatibility or term limitation.
4. Do not accept credits before exit and alternative-provider review.

### Result

The original offer is declined. A revised nonexclusive offer may be reviewed.

### Holdpoints

A narrow technical exclusivity exception would require a separate decision, duration, alternatives, public rationale, and migration plan; none is accepted here.

## TTX-FND-009 — Founder-related service company

### Synthetic facts

A company controlled by the founder offers development and hosting services at below-market price. It would receive payment from a restricted grant.

### Analysis

The arrangement may be useful but is a direct related-party transaction. Founder authority, grant custody, vendor selection, work acceptance, and payment cannot remain concentrated without disclosure and independent review.

### Response

1. Publish the related-party category and material public effect.
2. Obtain comparable alternatives and pricing evidence.
3. Separate specification, selection, implementation, acceptance, payment approval, and outcome review where possible.
4. Require founder recusal from sole vendor selection and payment approval.
5. Record public amount or band, scope, conflicts, alternatives, and termination behavior.
6. Avoid transferring public-domain ownership or permanent provider control.

### Result

The arrangement remains deferred until a legal recipient, compensation/procurement policy, unconflicted reviewer, pricing basis, and contract authority exist.

### Holdpoints

- legal entity and related-party controls;
- tax and accounting review;
- compensation or procurement policy; and
- independent acceptance authority.

## TTX-FND-010 — Material anonymous institutional donation

### Synthetic facts

An institution offers $250,000 but requests that its identity remain confidential for one year. The funds are unrestricted and request no explicit benefit.

### Analysis

Ordinary donor privacy does not automatically justify hidden institutional influence. The project must assess law, safety, conflict, related parties, source legitimacy, concentration, and public trust.

### Response

1. Conduct private source and authority review through an approved system.
2. Determine whether temporary withholding is lawful, necessary, and compatible with accountability.
3. Publish a reviewed record with source category, amount or band, purpose, conflicts, concentration effect, authority, withholding reason, review date, and planned identity release when possible.
4. Decline if source legitimacy, conflict, sanctions, related party, or public-interest review cannot be satisfied.

### Result

The offer remains deferred. Anonymity is not accepted merely as a preference at this scale.

### Holdpoints

- entity and custody authority;
- sanctions, fraud, tax, and legal review;
- independently reviewable source verification; and
- public withholding policy implementation.

## TTX-FND-011 — Sponsor-funded self-audit

### Synthetic facts

A security vendor offers to sponsor an audit performed by its own consulting team on infrastructure it also wants the project to adopt.

### Analysis

The team may provide useful external specialist review, but the relationship combines sponsor, vendor, implementer, evaluator, and potential provider roles. It cannot be described as independent certification.

### Response

1. Disclose all roles and commercial interests.
2. Define project-controlled scope and publication rights.
3. Classify the work as sponsor-funded external review, not independent certification.
4. Require separate validation for material findings or provider selection.
5. Do not condition adoption on audit sponsorship or audit payment on favorable findings.

### Result

The review may proceed on public or synthetic material under accurate independence labels. Provider selection remains separate.

### Holdpoints

- separate independent reviewer;
- remediation and retest;
- production evidence; and
- procurement and contract authority.

## TTX-FND-012 — Sponsor conduct crisis

### Synthetic facts

A major sponsor is credibly reported to have engaged in conduct conflicting with participant rights and the project’s mission. Facts are incomplete and disputed.

### Analysis

Immediate permanent judgment without review may be unfair, while continued promotion may create harm and implied endorsement. Containment is not a verdict.

### Response

1. Pause new recognition, renewal, and discretionary expansion.
2. Preserve historical records and private source evidence.
3. Assess credible public facts, affected groups, relationship terms, dependency, continuity, and legal limits.
4. Remove active promotion when necessary to avoid ongoing harm or confusion.
5. Provide the sponsor a factual correction channel without editorial veto.
6. Decide renewal, narrowing, termination, or restoration through attributable authority.
7. Publish a safe outcome and residual obligations.

### Result

The relationship is paused pending review. Critical services activate continuity planning to prevent sponsor leverage over the decision.

### Holdpoints

- conduct and due-process policy where applicable;
- legal review;
- independent governance review; and
- replacement capacity.

## TTX-FND-013 — Infrastructure sponsor acquisition

### Synthetic facts

A sponsored provider is acquired. New terms permit broader analytics and model-training use, support contacts change, and the acquiring company competes in health data.

### Analysis

Acquisition changes the counterparty, data-use risk, conflict, public trust, and exit assumptions. Prior acceptance does not silently transfer.

### Response

1. Freeze new protected-data expansion.
2. Revalidate terms, subprocessers, retention, training, access, support, jurisdiction, and incident duties.
3. Rotate or restrict credentials where appropriate.
4. Activate migration if new terms conflict or uncertainty is unacceptable.
5. Update public counterparty, conflict, dependency, capability, and recognition records.

### Result

Public and synthetic workloads may continue temporarily under a time-bounded review if safe. Protected-data use remains blocked. Migration proceeds if acceptable terms are not restored.

### Holdpoints

- production data-processing review;
- legal and security review;
- operational export and migration evidence; and
- affected-person communication.

## TTX-FND-014 — Health-product referral commission

### Synthetic facts

A wellness marketplace offers 15% commission on purchases made through project links. It proposes personalized recommendations based on player goals and Chronicle variables.

### Analysis

The offer combines affiliate revenue, health-product recommendation, private-data use, behavioral targeting, and direct financial incentive to influence users.

### Response

1. Decline under current authority.
2. Do not add tracking links, product placements, or recommendation logic.
3. Do not share goals, Chronicle variables, or user identity.
4. Record the class as affiliate and health-commercial rather than ordinary sponsorship.

### Result

The relationship is declined. Future affiliate economics would require a separate decision and still could not override health, privacy, permission, and provider-neutrality boundaries.

### Holdpoints

- affiliate and endorsement decision;
- health-product and clinical governance;
- privacy and tracking review;
- user-choice and conflict evidence; and
- entity, tax, accounting, and consumer-protection review.

## TTX-FND-015 — Concentration threshold crossed

### Synthetic facts

One foundation renews a grant that would bring its share of trailing-twelve-month support to 36%. The grant funds maintainers, documentation, security review, and public infrastructure. No replacement source is ready.

### Analysis

The relationship crosses the presumptive 33% pause and spans multiple critical functions. Rejecting immediately may cause harm; accepting without controls increases capture.

### Response

1. Publish concentration and functional dependency.
2. Separate current obligations from new dependency.
3. Produce a time-bounded exception proposal with independent review.
4. Narrow restrictions and preserve publication and provider independence.
5. Establish diversification, scope-reduction, migration, and continuity milestones.
6. Prevent funder control over hiring, reviewers, roadmap, findings, and renewal narratives.
7. Revalidate at defined intervals and on missed milestones.

### Result

Additional dependency is paused. A narrow renewal may be accepted only to protect continuity while reducing concentration, with explicit expiration and no governance benefit.

### Holdpoints

- independent financial and governance review;
- operating budget and runway evidence;
- multiple maintainers and custodians; and
- replacement funding and infrastructure.

## TTX-FND-016 — Delivered feature without public benefit

### Synthetic facts

A sponsor funds a contribution dashboard. The feature ships on time and within budget, but contributors find it confusing, it increases performative activity, and it does not improve validated work quality.

### Analysis

Delivery metrics are positive while the intended public outcome and incentive guardrails fail. Funding success cannot be defined by spending, merge, release, or sponsor satisfaction.

### Response

1. Publish delivery and outcome separately.
2. Record usability, behavior, equity, and incentive evidence.
3. Pause or narrow the dashboard.
4. Remove metrics that reward volume or status performance.
5. Restore simpler contribution paths.
6. Determine whether remaining funds support correction, return, or an independently justified replacement.
7. Update the underlying economic and product assumptions.

### Result

The feature is contained and redesigned. The sponsor receives truthful attribution for funding the experiment, not a claim of successful public benefit.

### Holdpoints

- contributor and affected-party evidence;
- incentive review;
- outcome remeasurement; and
- sponsor-independent acceptance of the replacement.

## Cross-scenario findings

The exercises support the following baseline findings:

1. Funding classification must occur before negotiation defines benefits.
2. Provider credits and in-kind support are material even without cash.
3. Financial share alone misses critical-function capture.
4. Sponsor recognition and provider placement must remain separate.
5. Health, consent, permission, research, and safety surfaces require stronger exclusion of commercial influence.
6. Public reporting must preserve private source records while exposing institutional power and effect.
7. Founding-steward acceptance cannot be represented as independent review.
8. A sponsor may fund an evaluator without becoming the evaluator or controlling findings.
9. Related-party support requires explicit separation of roles and authority.
10. A pledge, nominal credit, accepted grant, completed artifact, and measured public benefit are distinct facts.
11. Termination must preserve history, migration, correction, and residual obligations.
12. The institution must be willing to narrow scope rather than violate the Promise to retain funding.

## Open exercise obligations

Before actual material relationships, the project still needs:

- legal and accounting review of public/private record design;
- operational payment, refund, reconciliation, fraud, sanctions, tax, and custody exercises;
- multi-party conflict and recusal exercises;
- real provider export and migration tests before critical dependency;
- sponsor-loss continuity exercises with implemented infrastructure;
- related-party procurement and compensation procedures;
- public website rendering and privacy review;
- incident procedures for donor, contract, payment, and accounting information;
- independently reviewed concentration thresholds after real history exists; and
- actual outcome reports after bounded relationships operate.
