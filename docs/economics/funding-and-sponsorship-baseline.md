# Funding and Sponsorship Operating Baseline

[Economics home](README.md) · [Decision 0008](../decisions/0008-funding-and-sponsorship-baseline.md) · [Public ledger policy](public-funding-ledger-policy.md) · [Tracking issue #37](https://github.com/finalboss-tom/calypsos-promise/issues/37)

- **Status:** ACCEPTED PHASE 0 BASELINE
- **Revision:** 1
- **Information class:** PUBLIC doctrine and synthetic-only operating structures
- **Operational status:** no payment, treasury, grant, sponsorship, affiliate, investment, compensation, or research-funding runtime is authorized or represented as deployed

## Purpose

This baseline turns Decision 0008 into a reviewable operating model.

It exists so Calypso’s Promise can receive mission-aligned support in the future without allowing financial relationships to become hidden product governance, data access, health influence, provider capture, research access, public-reporting control, or a substitute for evidence.

## Funding thesis

The preferred model is not a generic logo marketplace.

The preferred loop is:

> public-good need → bounded funding opportunity → reviewed relationship → ordinary governed work → inspectable delivery → measured outcome → correction, renewal, or termination

The project may recognize support visibly. Recognition remains subordinate to the public-good outcome and the rights of players, contributors, affected people, and future stewards.

## Relationship classification

Classification follows substance, consideration, restriction, dependency, and expected return.

### Individual donation

Voluntary support from a person without negotiated commercial consideration.

Baseline treatment:

- aggregate publicly by default;
- do not publish identity without documented permission or a reviewed requirement;
- do not mint contribution credit, governance weight, player progression, or priority;
- use only an approved payment and custody route; and
- publish aggregate receipts and use after privacy and re-identification review.

### Organizational donation

Support from a company, nonprofit, foundation, university, association, government body, or other institution without material promotional consideration.

Baseline treatment:

- create a public relationship record;
- disclose purpose, value or amount band, restrictions, conflicts, authority, and work linkage;
- evaluate reputation, legal restrictions, dependency, and mission compatibility; and
- do not describe support as project endorsement of the donor.

### Restricted grant

Support whose use is limited by purpose, deliverable, population, geography, timing, reporting, or another condition.

Baseline treatment:

- accept only restrictions compatible with frozen rights and the accepted roadmap;
- record what happens when the work cannot be completed as proposed;
- preserve independent findings and correction rights;
- identify unspent, redirected, returned, or expired funds; and
- do not allow grant reporting to overstate benefit.

### Public-good underwriting

Support for a named public artifact, review, fellowship, dataset, capability, exercise, or institutional outcome.

Baseline treatment:

- preferred Phase 0 sponsorship pattern;
- publish the opportunity before or alongside the relationship when safe;
- link support to issues, pull requests, reports, releases, or independently held deliverables;
- preserve ordinary review and acceptance authority; and
- distinguish delivery from measured outcome.

### Corporate sponsorship

Support accompanied by approved recognition or other bounded consideration.

Baseline treatment:

- require a material relationship review and public record;
- enumerate every approved benefit;
- refuse hidden consideration and implied endorsement;
- revalidate long-running relationships; and
- apply concentration and conflict controls.

### In-kind support

Infrastructure, software, professional services, equipment, credits, data access, labor, or another non-cash benefit.

Baseline treatment:

- treat as funding rather than “free” support;
- document valuation method and uncertainty;
- disclose duration, restrictions, data processing, credentials, dependency, and exit behavior;
- preserve provider neutrality and replacement; and
- record actual consumption separately from headline credit value.

### Matching support

Funding released when qualifying contributions or actions occur.

Baseline treatment:

- do not manufacture health, consent, research, donation, or governance urgency;
- publish the matching condition and cap;
- do not expose individual donor identity to the matcher without appropriate authority; and
- do not convert matching activity into product or governance pressure.

### Pro bono specialist review

Qualified review supplied without ordinary payment.

Baseline treatment:

- disclose the provider and material conflict or commercial interest;
- distinguish contributor review from independent review;
- do not claim independence when the reviewer funds, sells, or implements the subject system; and
- preserve the ability to publish limitations and disagreement.

### Affiliate or referral revenue

Compensation based on attributable registration, purchase, subscription, usage, lead, or conversion.

Baseline treatment:

- deferred by default in Phase 0;
- requires a separate decision, endorsement policy, tracking and privacy review, comparison rules, conflict reporting, and evidence that user recommendations remain independent;
- never permitted inside health, consent, permission, receipt, research-eligibility, or safety decisions; and
- cannot create default provider placement without a provider-selection decision.

### Research funding

Support tied to study design, recruitment, participant access, data access, analysis, publication, or research use.

Baseline treatment:

- deferred to Research Commons gates;
- cannot be relabeled as ordinary sponsorship to bypass research governance;
- cannot purchase access to players or private information; and
- requires participant, scientific, privacy, security, publication, and conflict controls.

### Investment or ownership capital

Equity, debt, repayment, convertible, ownership, control-bearing, or return-seeking capital.

Baseline treatment:

- outside this baseline;
- requires a separate strategic, economic, legal, and governance decision;
- cannot encumber public-domain foundations or purchase constitutional authority; and
- must address entity structure, fiduciary duties, mission protection, conflicts, ownership, dissolution, and founder independence.

### Individual compensation

Payment or material benefit to a person for work, service, review, participation, or reimbursement.

Baseline treatment:

- not authorized by this baseline;
- requires compensation, tax, employment, contractor, related-party, conflict, and reporting rules; and
- remains separate from Fellowship, Laurels, player rewards, and governance authority.

## Materiality

A relationship is material when any of the following applies:

- it is organizational, restricted, sponsored, affiliate, research-linked, investment-linked, or related-party;
- it provides public recognition or endorsement-like consideration;
- it funds or evaluates safety, privacy, clinical, security, accessibility, legal, governance, research, or canonical work;
- it supports a provider being selected, evaluated, recommended, or integrated;
- it creates or could create critical operational dependency;
- it reaches a concentration trigger;
- it involves a regulated or high-conflict sector;
- it requests exclusivity, access, priority, influence, confidentiality, or unusual reporting control;
- it is likely to affect public trust even when the cash value is small; or
- a reasonable affected person would want the relationship disclosed when evaluating the project’s independence.

Materiality is not only a dollar threshold.

## High-conflict and prohibited sources

A source is not accepted merely because its funds are useful or lawful.

The project must decline or escalate support when the relationship would:

- require violation of the Product Constitution, law, policy, or a specialist duty;
- purchase private information, recruitment, research access, health influence, permission treatment, or governance control;
- require hidden promotion, deceptive attribution, false endorsement, or suppressed disclosure;
- require exclusivity that prevents meaningful choice, interoperability, replacement, or fork continuity;
- materially depend on unlawful, fraudulent, coercive, exploitative, discriminatory, deceptive, or rights-violating conduct;
- obstruct publication of incidents, limitations, negative results, conflicts, corrections, or outcomes;
- expose protected donor, player, participant, contributor, or partner information;
- create an unbounded critical dependency without a credible exit path;
- conflict with public-domain commitments; or
- make acceptance reasonably likely to damage the mission more than the support advances it.

Support from health, pharmaceutical, biotechnology, insurance, employer, advertising, data-broker, analytics, identity, AI, cloud, security, financial, gambling, political, defense, law-enforcement, or research actors is not automatically prohibited. It receives heightened conflict, affected-party, rights, dependency, endorsement, and public-trust review appropriate to the relationship.

## Funding lifecycle

### 1. Need identified

A public-good opportunity states:

- intended outcome;
- affected groups;
- constitutional and policy boundaries;
- work or evidence expected;
- responsible steward;
- requested support type or public amount band;
- whether partial funding is useful;
- conflicts and dependencies;
- what remains unfunded or unresolved;
- how delivery and outcome will be assessed; and
- expiration or revalidation date.

### 2. Offer received privately

Raw outreach, due diligence, contacts, negotiations, contracts, payment information, and legal or accounting records remain outside public GitHub systems.

### 3. Classification and materiality

The responsible steward classifies the relationship, identifies consideration and restrictions, and decides whether the standing policy applies or material review is required.

### 4. Compatibility review

Review at least:

- mission and rights compatibility;
- requested benefits;
- financial and in-kind value;
- restrictions and reporting terms;
- conflicts and related parties;
- affected groups;
- sponsor and project conduct risk;
- data, privacy, security, accessibility, clinical, research, and legal implications;
- provider influence and vendor dependency;
- concentration and continuity;
- exit, refund, unspent value, and termination behavior;
- public disclosure; and
- reviewer independence.

### 5. Decision

The relationship is:

- accepted;
- conditionally accepted;
- declined;
- deferred pending evidence or authority;
- escalated through a decision record; or
- redirected to a different governance path.

### 6. Public record

Before promotional activation, publish the reviewed material relationship and approved benefits when safe and lawful.

A confidential negotiation is not a confidential sponsorship after acceptance.

### 7. Receipt and custody

Distinguish:

- proposed offer;
- nonbinding indication;
- pledge;
- signed agreement;
- received cash;
- available cash;
- granted credit;
- consumed credit;
- delivered service; and
- independently valued in-kind support.

Do not count a headline credit, unsigned promise, restricted balance, or unconsumed service as unrestricted realized funding.

### 8. Governed work

Funded work enters the ordinary governed-work loop. The sponsor cannot privately redefine scope, acceptance, completion, safety, or outcome.

### 9. Progress and variance

Record material delay, scope change, budget or value variance, unavailable reviewer, failed dependency, unspent value, extension, cancellation, or changed restriction.

### 10. Outcome review

Record:

- what was delivered;
- what was accepted;
- what was used;
- what outcome was observed;
- what evidence is missing;
- what harm or conflict occurred;
- what remains owed or unresolved; and
- whether continuation, correction, restoration, replacement, or termination is warranted.

### 11. Closure or renewal

A relationship may complete, expire, renew, narrow, suspend, terminate, refund, redirect, supersede, or remain open with residual obligations.

### 12. Revalidation

Long-running relationships are reviewed for conflict drift, sponsor conduct, concentration, dependency, influence, reporting accuracy, public benefit, and mission effects.

## Status vocabulary

Funding records use distinct states:

- **proposed** — public opportunity or relationship idea, not accepted;
- **under review** — private source review is active;
- **conditionally accepted** — acceptance depends on named conditions;
- **accepted** — doctrine and authority permit the relationship;
- **active** — support or funded work is currently operating;
- **paused** — activity is temporarily contained;
- **completed** — bounded obligations are complete;
- **expired** — time-limited authority or support ended;
- **declined** — the project rejected the relationship;
- **withdrawn** — the source withdrew before completion;
- **terminated** — the relationship ended before ordinary completion;
- **refunded** — value was returned in whole or part;
- **superseded** — a successor record governs new activity; and
- **disputed** — a material challenge or unresolved conflict is active.

These states do not imply that funds were received, work was delivered, or public benefit occurred. Those facts are recorded separately.

## Stop conditions

Stop and seek reviewed authority when a relationship would:

- weaken meaningfully free use or player rights;
- create a reward for payment, wealth, broader consent, disclosure, research, or sponsor-preferred behavior;
- create product, health, permission, research, governance, or constitutional authority;
- require undisclosed consideration or conflict;
- make a sponsor the sole evaluator of funded work;
- create critical dependency without replacement;
- introduce affiliate tracking, investment, compensation, research funding, treasury, or legal-entity behavior not already accepted;
- require protected information in public records;
- claim charitable, tax-deductible, nonprofit, independent-review, deployed-control, or public-benefit status without evidence; or
- make safe termination, correction, reporting, or migration impossible.

## Revalidation

Review this baseline at every Phase 0 exit review, before the first material relationship, before a live support surface, after the first suspension or termination, when a concentration threshold is reached, and whenever evidence suggests funding is distorting priorities, provider choices, reporting, or institutional authority.
