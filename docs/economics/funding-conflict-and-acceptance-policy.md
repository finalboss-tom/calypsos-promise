## Purpose

This policy defines how funding relationships are reviewed, accepted, declined, contained, corrected, and terminated during Phase 0.

It prevents private negotiation, founder interest, sponsor preference, vendor convenience, provider access, enterprise distribution, or funding urgency from becoming unrecorded economic, product, schema, connector, clinical, or roadmap authority.

## Duties

Every person reviewing or administering a material relationship owes duties to:

- preserve the Product Constitution and meaningfully free boundary;
- preserve the consumer-first and provider-independent product center;
- disclose material conflicts and related parties;
- distinguish personal and public benefit from sponsor, provider, vendor, or enterprise benefit;
- preserve independent findings and ordinary project review;
- minimize private information and publish reviewed institutional derivatives;
- avoid commitments beyond current authority;
- record uncertainty, restrictions, dependency, mapping loss, and residual risk;
- stop or escalate when authority or specialist review is missing; and
- correct, migrate, or terminate relationships that become incompatible.

## Conflict categories

A conflict exists when a reviewer, steward, implementer, evaluator, office, or related person may receive a material personal, professional, institutional, reputational, governance, ownership, clinical, product, distribution, or future commercial benefit from the decision.

Relevant conflicts include:

- direct payment, employment, equity, debt, token, commission, referral, bonus, gift, reimbursement, or future opportunity;
- family, household, romantic, close personal, business, fiduciary, donor, patient, provider, client, or investor relationships;
- ownership, office, board, advisory, contractor, employment, clinical, research, or volunteer roles with the source;
- funding of the reviewer, team, laboratory, health system, organization, employer, project, or dependent infrastructure;
- responsibility for selecting, integrating, selling, recommending, ranking, auditing, validating, or evaluating the sponsor’s product or service;
- responsibility for defining a schema, mapping, implementation guide, connector, provider summary, clinical workflow, endpoint, or source ranking that benefits the source;
- public advocacy or adversarial involvement that reasonably affects impartial review;
- a sponsor, provider, EHR, payer, laboratory, device, exchange, AI, cloud, or enterprise relationship that materially supports the canonical project, founder, distribution, data access, or critical function;
- career, publication, grant, prestige, patient-access, market-access, standards, or governance incentives tied to acceptance or claimed success; and
- any fact a reasonable affected person would want to know when evaluating the decision’s independence.

A conflict is not automatically misconduct. Hidden conflict, false independence, purchased source authority, or failure to contain a conflict is unacceptable.

## Related parties

A related-party relationship receives heightened review when support, payment, procurement, reimbursement, contract, data access, integration, distribution, or in-kind value involves:

- the founder or a founder-controlled entity;
- a maintainer, operator, specialist, reviewer, clinician, researcher, or governance participant;
- a family, household, practice, laboratory, employer, or controlled entity of those people;
- an employer, client, investor, donor, provider, payer, EHR vendor, sponsor, or organization with material influence over them; or
- a party able to benefit from nonpublic project, health, provider, mapping, security, interoperability, or decision information.

A related-party record must state:

- the relationship category;
- the value or approved amount band;
- the work, data access, distribution, or benefit provided;
- alternatives considered;
- selection and pricing basis;
- source, provider, connector, schema, and roadmap implications;
- conflicts and recusals;
- approving authority;
- review timing;
- termination and replacement behavior; and
- why the arrangement serves affected people and the project rather than merely the related party.

## Phase 0 authority

### Standing-policy eligibility

A future standing policy may cover small unrestricted individual donations only after the project has documented:

- a legitimate recipient or custodian;
- legal authority to receive the funds;
- payment, fraud, sanctions, refund, chargeback, and reconciliation behavior;
- donor privacy notice, retention, correction, and deletion behavior;
- accounting ownership and review;
- public aggregate reporting; and
- an incident and teardown path.

This baseline does not activate that standing policy.

No standing policy may automatically cover provider, EHR, payer, laboratory, device, exchange, research, infrastructure, connector, enterprise, distribution, affiliate, referral, or data-access relationships merely because their cash value is small.

### Material relationship review

The following always require explicit review and a public funding record:

- organizational donations;
- restricted grants;
- public-good underwriting;
- sponsorships with recognition or consideration;
- related-party support;
- affiliate or referral arrangements;
- research-linked funding;
- investment, ownership, debt, or repayment-bearing support;
- compensation or reimbursement outside an accepted standing rule;
- support from a vendor, provider, EHR, payer, laboratory, pharmacy, device company, exchange, research institution, employer, AI company, infrastructure company, or enterprise being selected, evaluated, integrated, recommended, ranked, or mapped;
- funded connectors, mappings, implementation guides, conformance work, provider summaries, endpoint discovery, patient matching, distribution, or clinical collaboration;
- support for a safety, security, privacy, accessibility, clinical, interoperability, legal, financial, governance, canonical, or research review;
- material in-kind support, data access, implementation labor, distribution, or waived integration fees;
- support controlling or replacing a critical function, source class, connector path, or acquisition channel;
- relationships reaching a financial, data-access, distribution, source-reach, roadmap-attention, or critical-dependency concentration trigger;
- high-conflict sectors or reputational circumstances; and
- any exception to the ordinary benefit, disclosure, provider-neutrality, consumer-first, public-domain, or publication rules.

### Current approval

During Phase 0, the founding steward may accept a material relationship only within accepted doctrine and after documenting conflicts, authority, restrictions, benefits, dependency, source and schema implications, provider and connector neutrality, and unresolved specialist gates.

Founding-steward approval is accountable internal acceptance. It is not independent legal, tax, accounting, financial, security, privacy, accessibility, clinical, interoperability, procurement, research, or governance review.

## Independence rules

### Relationship independence

A material relationship requires at least one reviewer who is not:

- the sponsor, donor, provider, vendor, or institutional counterparty;
- the direct recipient of personal compensation;
- the direct commercial, clinical, integration, distribution, or market beneficiary;
- the sole implementer of the funded work;
- employed, controlled, credentialed, or materially funded by the source in the relevant matter;
- responsible for selling, selecting, ranking, or recommending the sponsored product; or
- responsible for the only mapping, conformance test, provider evaluation, or outcome claim.

If such a reviewer is unavailable, the relationship remains internally reviewed with the independence limitation recorded. It may not be described as independently approved.

### Evaluation independence

A sponsor may fund independent review, but it cannot:

- select the only evaluator without project and conflict review;
- control evaluation questions, methods, sources, mappings, evidence, findings, publication, correction, or release timing;
- remove unfavorable findings, unsupported mappings, incidents, or limitations;
- serve as its own independent evaluator;
- make payment contingent on favorable results; or
- claim project, clinical, standards, provider, or product endorsement from funding the review.

When a sponsor funds work performed by a commercial, clinical, standards, or interoperability evaluator, the public record distinguishes:

- who selected the evaluator;
- who contracted and paid;
- the evaluator’s conflicts;
- the sponsor’s access to drafts and evidence;
- publication and correction rights;
- limitations on independence;
- whether the evaluator also sells, implements, certifies, or operates the product; and
- whether a separate reviewer validated the work.

## Review record

A material review must document:

- funding record ID;
- source and relationship classification;
- public value or amount band;
- purpose and restrictions;
- requested and approved benefits;
- affected groups;
- constitutional and policy boundaries;
- direct and indirect conflicts;
- recusals and replacement reviewers;
- alternatives and opportunity cost;
- provider, connector, schema, mapping, distribution, source-authority, and roadmap implications;
- dependency and concentration;
- data, privacy, security, accessibility, clinical, interoperability, research, legal, regulatory, procurement, tax, and accounting implications;
- standards, implementation-guide, terminology, conformance, and proprietary-dependency status where relevant;
- public disclosure and protected source boundary;
- acceptance, conditions, decline, or escalation;
- authority and independence limitations;
- exit, migration, refund, termination, and residual obligations;
- review and expiration dates; and
- challenge or appeal path.

## Decision outcomes

### Accept

The relationship is compatible with current authority and all required conditions are satisfied.

### Conditionally accept

The relationship may proceed only after named conditions are satisfied. Conditions must be objective, attributable, time-bounded where possible, and publicly recorded when material.

### Defer

The relationship is not rejected, but current authority, evidence, reviewer, entity, custody, legal, accounting, privacy, interoperability, clinical, procurement, or operational prerequisites are missing.

### Decline

The relationship is incompatible, excessively risky, inadequately reviewable, or not worth the dependency, conflict, restriction, semantic loss, placement, or administrative burden.

A decline does not require public identification of a private unsolicited offer. A public derivative is required when the offer, negotiation, conflict, or decline is already material to public understanding or institutional accountability.

### Escalate

A new decision is required when the relationship would introduce or materially alter:

- investment, ownership, debt, or repayment;
- affiliate, referral, provider-placement, connector-ranking, or procurement revenue;
- research funding or participant access;
- treasury, reserves, compensation, or budget authority;
- legal entity, fiscal sponsorship, asset lock, or fiduciary duties;
- public endorsement, standards certification, source ranking, provider recommendation, preferred connector, or clinical placement behavior;
- the canonical Chronicle model, source authority, implementation-guide dependency, or product center;
- an enterprise contract that would make the complete personal product dependent on institutional enrollment, funding, distribution, or renewal;
- an exception to frozen rights, consumer-first, provider-independent, or public-domain commitments;
- material exclusivity;
- a major critical-function, data-source, distribution, or connector dependency; or
- a conflict not safely containable by ordinary policy.

## Recusal

A conflicted person must:

- disclose the conflict before participating materially;
- avoid sole approval;
- avoid controlling evidence, alternatives, mappings, source rankings, or the public record;
- leave the decision to an appropriate unconflicted authority where one exists;
- avoid using nonpublic information for personal, clinical, institutional, or related-party benefit; and
- record any unavoidable participation and why it was necessary.

Recusal from approval does not always require exclusion from factual or technical input. The record must distinguish advice, implementation, mapping, evaluation, clinical review, and decision authority.

## Gifts and informal benefits

A gift, travel, hospitality, event access, discount, credit, equipment, data access, endpoint assistance, implementation labor, provider introduction, private briefing, distribution, or personal service can create a conflict even when not called sponsorship.

No reviewer or steward may accept a material personal or institutional benefit intended or reasonably likely to influence a project, provider, connector, schema, clinical, or roadmap decision without disclosure and reviewed authority.

The exact de minimis and gift-administration rules remain deferred until an operating entity and financial controls exist.

## Confidentiality and disclosure

Private negotiation may be temporarily legitimate. Confidentiality may not conceal:

- an accepted paid, in-kind, data-access, provider, connector, distribution, or enterprise relationship;
- material benefits or restrictions;
- related parties;
- conflicts and recusals;
- sponsor, provider, vendor, or enterprise influence;
- institutional spending, source reach, placement, or dependency;
- a material suspension, migration, termination, refund, or correction; or
- an inability to publish findings, mapping limitations, incidents, or negative outcomes.

Publish a reviewed derivative rather than raw contracts, correspondence, invoices, donor lists, patient or provider records, private mappings, tax records, or legal advice.

## Suspension and containment

Pause recognition, new commitments, funded work, provider use, connector synchronization, integration, distribution, or expenditure when:

- a material conflict was omitted or misrepresented;
- the sponsor or institution requests prohibited access, influence, schema authority, source ranking, placement, exclusivity, or findings control;
- legal, clinical, interoperability, procurement, custody, or authority status becomes uncertain;
- public reporting is materially false or incomplete;
- the relationship creates new safety, privacy, security, clinical, semantic, accessibility, public-domain, or governance risk;
- concentration or critical dependency exceeds accepted conditions;
- sponsor, provider, vendor, or institutional conduct creates a credible mission or participant-protection concern;
- the source attempts to suppress findings, incidents, limitations, mapping loss, or correction; or
- termination, migration, or replacement becomes reasonably necessary to prevent harm.

Containment is not a final verdict. It is narrow, attributable, reviewable, and followed by a decision and public derivative when safe.

## Termination

A material relationship must permit termination for:

- constitutional, consumer-first, provider-neutral, or legal incompatibility;
- prohibited benefit, access, schema, placement, or influence request;
- material undisclosed conflict;
- false representation, standards claim, clinical claim, or deceptive endorsement;
- security, privacy, safety, accessibility, clinical, interoperability, research, conduct, or public-domain breach;
- inability to publish required institutional information or negative findings;
- persistent nonperformance, mapping failure, semantic loss, or unsupported conformance;
- material sponsor, provider, vendor, or institutional conduct risk;
- unacceptable concentration, source reach, distribution, or dependency; or
- ordinary expiration, mutual agreement, or strategic replacement.

Termination records distinguish:

- prospective end of benefit, access, synchronization, distribution, and recognition;
- historical attribution;
- refunds or unspent restrictions;
- migration, source preservation, mapping reconciliation, and service continuity;
- data, credential, account, endpoint, and asset teardown;
- residual contractual, provider, reporting, retention, and downstream duties;
- correction and notification; and
- unresolved semantic or participant harm.

## Appeal and challenge

A source may request factual correction or reconsideration. It has no right to override constitutional, consumer-first, provider-neutral, safety, specialist, publication, or conflict duties.

Players, contributors, clinicians, specialists, donors, partners, providers, vendors, institutions, and affected people may challenge a material relationship using public-safe evidence or an authorized private route.

Challenges are evaluated by evidence, affectedness, rights, conflict, dependency, reversibility, semantic fidelity, personal value, and institutional legitimacy—not by donation size, provider status, sponsor status, enterprise revenue, contributor rank, popularity, or public pressure alone.
