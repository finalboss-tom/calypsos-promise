## Purpose

This policy defines how funding relationships are reviewed, accepted, declined, contained, corrected, and terminated during Phase 0.

It prevents private negotiation, founder interest, sponsor preference, vendor convenience, or funding urgency from becoming unrecorded economic authority.

## Duties

Every person reviewing or administering a material relationship owes duties to:

- preserve the Product Constitution and meaningfully free boundary;
- disclose material conflicts and related parties;
- distinguish public benefit from sponsor benefit;
- preserve independent findings and ordinary project review;
- minimize private information and publish reviewed institutional derivatives;
- avoid commitments beyond current authority;
- record uncertainty, restrictions, dependency, and residual risk;
- stop or escalate when authority is missing; and
- correct or terminate relationships that become incompatible.

## Conflict categories

A conflict exists when a reviewer, steward, implementer, evaluator, office, or related person may receive a material personal, professional, institutional, reputational, governance, ownership, or future commercial benefit from the decision.

Relevant conflicts include:

- direct payment, employment, equity, debt, token, commission, referral, bonus, gift, reimbursement, or future opportunity;
- family, household, romantic, close personal, business, fiduciary, donor, client, or investor relationships;
- ownership, office, board, advisory, contractor, employment, or volunteer roles with the source;
- funding of the reviewer, team, laboratory, organization, employer, project, or dependent infrastructure;
- responsibility for selecting, integrating, selling, auditing, or evaluating the sponsor’s product or service;
- public advocacy or adversarial involvement that reasonably affects impartial review;
- a sponsor relationship that materially supports the canonical project, founder, or critical function;
- career, publication, grant, prestige, or governance incentives tied to acceptance or claimed success; and
- any fact a reasonable affected person would want to know when evaluating the decision’s independence.

A conflict is not automatically misconduct. Hidden conflict, false independence, or failure to contain a conflict is unacceptable.

## Related parties

A related-party relationship receives heightened review when support, payment, procurement, reimbursement, contract, or in-kind value involves:

- the founder or a founder-controlled entity;
- a maintainer, operator, specialist, reviewer, or governance participant;
- a family, household, or controlled entity of those people;
- an employer, client, investor, donor, or organization with material influence over them; or
- a party able to benefit from nonpublic project information or decision authority.

A related-party record must state:

- the relationship category;
- the value or approved amount band;
- the work or benefit provided;
- alternatives considered;
- selection and pricing basis;
- conflicts and recusals;
- approving authority;
- review timing;
- termination and replacement behavior; and
- why the arrangement serves the project rather than merely the related party.

## Phase 0 authority

### Standing-policy eligibility

A future standing policy may cover small unrestricted individual donations only after the project has documented:

- a legitimate recipient or custodian;
- legal authority to receive the funds;
- payment, fraud, sanctions, refund, chargeback, and reconciliation behavior;
- donor privacy notice, retention, correction, and deletion behavior;
- accounting ownership and review;
- public aggregate reporting; and
- an incident and teardown path.

This baseline does not activate that standing policy.

### Material relationship review

The following always require explicit review and a public funding record:

- organizational donations;
- restricted grants;
- public-good underwriting;
- sponsorships with recognition or consideration;
- related-party support;
- affiliate or referral arrangements;
- research-linked funding;
- investment, ownership, debt, or repayment-bearing support;
- compensation or reimbursement outside an accepted standing rule;
- support from a vendor being selected, evaluated, integrated, or recommended;
- support for a safety, security, privacy, accessibility, clinical, legal, financial, governance, canonical, or research review;
- material in-kind support;
- support controlling or replacing a critical function;
- relationships reaching a concentration trigger;
- high-conflict sectors or reputational circumstances; and
- any exception to the ordinary benefit, disclosure, provider-neutrality, or public-domain rules.

### Current approval

During Phase 0, the founding steward may accept a material relationship only within the accepted doctrine and after documenting conflicts, authority, restrictions, benefits, dependency, and unresolved specialist gates.

Founding-steward approval is accountable internal acceptance. It is not independent legal, tax, accounting, financial, security, privacy, accessibility, clinical, research, or governance review.

## Independence rules

### Relationship independence

A material relationship requires at least one reviewer who is not:

- the sponsor or donor;
- the direct recipient of personal compensation;
- the direct commercial beneficiary;
- the sole implementer of the funded work;
- employed or controlled by the sponsor in the relevant matter; or
- responsible for selling or selecting the sponsored product.

If such a reviewer is unavailable, the relationship remains internally reviewed with the independence limitation recorded. It may not be described as independently approved.

### Evaluation independence

A sponsor may fund independent review, but it cannot:

- select the only evaluator without project and conflict review;
- control evaluation questions, methods, evidence, findings, publication, correction, or release timing;
- remove unfavorable findings;
- serve as its own independent evaluator;
- make payment contingent on favorable results; or
- claim project endorsement from funding the review.

When a sponsor funds work performed by a commercial evaluator, the public record distinguishes:

- who selected the evaluator;
- who contracted and paid;
- the evaluator’s conflicts;
- the sponsor’s access to drafts;
- publication and correction rights;
- limitations on independence; and
- whether a separate reviewer validated the work.

## Review record

A material review must document:

- funding record ID;
- source and relationship classification;
- public value or amount band;
- purpose and restrictions;
- requested and approved benefits;
- affected groups;
- constitutional and policy boundaries;
- direct and indirect conflicts;
- recusals and replacement reviewers;
- alternatives and opportunity cost;
- dependency and concentration;
- data, privacy, security, accessibility, clinical, research, legal, tax, and accounting implications;
- public disclosure and protected source boundary;
- acceptance, conditions, decline, or escalation;
- authority and independence limitations;
- exit, refund, termination, and residual obligations;
- review and expiration dates; and
- challenge or appeal path.

## Decision outcomes

### Accept

The relationship is compatible with current authority and all required conditions are satisfied.

### Conditionally accept

The relationship may proceed only after named conditions are satisfied. Conditions must be objective, attributable, time-bounded where possible, and publicly recorded when material.

### Defer

The relationship is not rejected, but current authority, evidence, reviewer, entity, custody, legal, accounting, privacy, or operational prerequisites are missing.

### Decline

The relationship is incompatible, excessively risky, inadequately reviewable, or not worth the dependency, conflict, restriction, or administrative burden.

A decline does not require public identification of a private unsolicited offer. A public derivative is required when the offer, negotiation, conflict, or decline is already material to public understanding or institutional accountability.

### Escalate

A new decision is required when the relationship would introduce or materially alter:

- investment, ownership, debt, or repayment;
- affiliate or referral revenue;
- research funding or participant access;
- treasury, reserves, compensation, or budget authority;
- legal entity, fiscal sponsorship, asset lock, or fiduciary duties;
- public endorsement or provider-ranking behavior;
- an exception to frozen rights or public-domain commitments;
- material exclusivity;
- a major critical-function dependency; or
- a conflict not safely containable by ordinary policy.

## Recusal

A conflicted person must:

- disclose the conflict before participating materially;
- avoid sole approval;
- avoid controlling evidence, alternatives, or the public record;
- leave the decision to an appropriate unconflicted authority where one exists;
- avoid using nonpublic information for personal or related-party benefit; and
- record any unavoidable participation and why it was necessary.

Recusal from approval does not always require exclusion from factual or technical input. The record must distinguish advice, implementation, evaluation, and decision authority.

## Gifts and informal benefits

A gift, travel, hospitality, event access, discount, credit, equipment, private briefing, or personal service can create a conflict even when not called sponsorship.

No reviewer or steward may accept a material personal benefit intended or reasonably likely to influence a project decision without disclosure and reviewed authority.

The exact de minimis and gift-administration rules remain deferred until an operating entity and financial controls exist.

## Confidentiality and disclosure

Private negotiation may be temporarily legitimate. Confidentiality may not conceal:

- an accepted paid relationship;
- material benefits or restrictions;
- related parties;
- conflicts and recusals;
- sponsor influence;
- institutional spending or dependency;
- a material suspension, termination, refund, or correction; or
- an inability to publish findings or incidents.

Publish a reviewed derivative rather than raw contracts, correspondence, invoices, donor lists, tax records, or legal advice.

## Suspension and containment

Pause recognition, new commitments, funded work, provider use, or expenditure when:

- a material conflict was omitted or misrepresented;
- the sponsor requests prohibited access or influence;
- legal or custody authority becomes uncertain;
- public reporting is materially false or incomplete;
- the relationship creates new safety, privacy, security, clinical, research, accessibility, public-domain, or governance risk;
- concentration or critical dependency exceeds accepted conditions;
- sponsor conduct creates a credible mission or participant-protection concern;
- the sponsor attempts to suppress findings, incidents, limitations, or correction; or
- termination or replacement becomes reasonably necessary to prevent harm.

Containment is not a final verdict. It is narrow, attributable, reviewable, and followed by a decision and public derivative when safe.

## Termination

A material relationship must permit termination for:

- constitutional or legal incompatibility;
- prohibited benefit or influence request;
- material undisclosed conflict;
- false representation or deceptive endorsement;
- security, privacy, safety, accessibility, clinical, research, conduct, or public-domain breach;
- inability to publish required institutional information;
- persistent nonperformance;
- material sponsor conduct risk;
- unacceptable concentration or dependency; or
- ordinary expiration, mutual agreement, or strategic replacement.

Termination records distinguish:

- prospective end of benefit and recognition;
- historical attribution;
- refunds or unspent restrictions;
- migration and service continuity;
- data, credential, account, and asset teardown;
- residual contractual or reporting duties;
- correction and notification; and
- unresolved harm.

## Appeal and challenge

A source may request factual correction or reconsideration. It has no right to override constitutional, safety, specialist, publication, or conflict duties.

Players, contributors, specialists, donors, partners, and affected people may challenge a material relationship using public-safe evidence or an authorized private route.

Challenges are evaluated by evidence, affectedness, rights, conflict, dependency, reversibility, and institutional legitimacy—not by donation size, sponsor status, contributor rank, popularity, or public pressure alone.
