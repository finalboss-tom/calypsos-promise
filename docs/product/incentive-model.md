# Core Objective and Incentive Model

**Status:** BASELINE implementation contract derived from FROZEN foundations

## Source basis

This document does not introduce a new product objective. It translates the frozen Product Constitution and Gameplay Foundation into a compact implementation and review contract.

Material changes to the player promise, three connected loops, progress dimensions, or incentive prohibitions require a decision record.

## Core objective

Calypso’s Promise helps people build, understand, and control a high-quality longitudinal account of their health and lived experience through brief, rewarding, narrative-driven participation.

The product returns increasing personal value first. Public-good research and compensated opportunities may follow only through separate authorization and without removing individual control.

The core experience is organized around three connected loops:

1. **Build the Living Chronicle** — capture, connect, normalize, confirm, and preserve provenance.
2. **Improve health and understanding** — receive understandable feedback, learn, reflect, conduct safe personal experiments, and complete self-chosen actions.
3. **Control and share in created value** — inspect permissions and choose whether information contributes to separately governed public-good or compensated opportunities.

The first two loops must create a worthwhile product without requiring the third.

## Why the incentives are core architecture

The incentive model is not decorative gamification layered over a health database. It is the mechanism that turns small acts of capture, confirmation, learning, reflection, recovery, sovereignty, and contribution into visible personal and narrative value.

Implementation should therefore preserve incentives as explicit, deterministic domain rules rather than burying them in UI copy, analytics experiments, or AI prompts.

The architecture should make the following easy to see and hard to violate:

- what action the player is being invited to take
- what immediate value the action returns
- what evidence completes it
- what progress dimension changes
- what part of Ogygia responds
- what data or permission is involved
- how the player can replace, defer, decline, or stop

## Progress dimensions

### Vitality

Rewards constructive actions and health learning. Vitality must never represent body size, symptom absence, moral worth, compliance, or a universal health score.

### Chronicle

Rewards continuity, context, provenance, source quality, confirmation, comparable units, and useful completeness of the Living Chronicle.

Chronicle progress must reflect record quality and usefulness, not volume of intimate disclosure.

### Fellowship

Rewards optional public-good, research, open-source, community, stewardship, or mentorship contributions.

Fellowship must remain optional. A player who never participates in research, commerce, public visibility, or community contribution must still receive a complete and valuable core experience.

### Renown

Represents overall narrative progress through Ogygia. Renown connects meaningful chapter transitions, restoration, discoveries, and player-chosen progress.

Renown must not become a proxy for health status, wealth, consent breadth, or human worth.

### Laurels

Non-cash cosmetic, commemorative, and restoration rewards. Laurels have no cash value unless a separate, explicit, legally reviewed program states otherwise.

Real compensation is never disguised as Laurels or any other game currency.

## Deterministic incentive boundary

The following are deterministic and versioned:

- quest eligibility
- permission requirements
- completion evidence
- reward calculation
- progress changes
- unlocks
- safety gates
- story revelation order

AI may personalize presentation or propose drafts, but it may not decide whether a person earned a reward, completed a quest, granted permission, or unlocked a protected capability.

## Quest incentive contract

Every quest must declare:

- stable identity and revision
- public and in-world meaning
- player value
- required action or evidence
- estimated time
- accessibility variants
- data categories touched
- purpose or permission requirement
- safety classification
- deterministic completion rule
- feedback
- reward and progress dimension
- narrative consequence
- replacement, deferral, decline, and stopping behavior
- review state

A quest must provide immediate player value. No quest may exist solely to collect information, increase consent breadth, create engagement metrics, or advance a secondary-use objective.

## Route composition

A normal route should balance:

- one observation or confirmation quest
- one constructive health, recovery, or learning quest
- one story, sovereignty, or progression quest

This composition prevents the experience from becoming an extraction queue or a generic habit tracker. The player may replace, defer, or reject the route without punishment.

## Hard prohibitions

The incentive model must never:

- reward broader consent merely because it is broader
- reward unnecessary intimate disclosure
- make essential privacy controls depend on payment or progression
- imply that a person’s data has a guaranteed fixed value
- disguise compensation as game currency
- use punitive streaks, shame, fake scarcity, or manufactured urgency
- punish refusal, deferral, withdrawal, correction, export, deletion, or return after interruption
- treat health status, symptom absence, body size, or adherence as moral achievement
- require public-good, research, commercial, or community participation for core progress

## Broken Lantern principle

Interruption is not failure.

When a player returns after a missed day or abandoned route, the system should help them resume, reduce the route, change reminders, or reconsider the goal. It rewards return and adaptation rather than perfection.

## Public-good and compensated opportunities

Public-good contribution belongs primarily to Fellowship and must remain visibly optional.

A compensated opportunity is a separate, purpose-specific offer with its own identity, terms, consent, eligibility, compensation, risks, and governance. It is not a quest reward and is never implied by ordinary progression.

The project’s public-good purpose does not justify concealed access, coercive incentives, or weakened validation.

## Product-centered change test

Every material product, content, or architecture change should identify one of the following:

- which connected loop it strengthens
- which progress dimension it enables
- which player right or safety boundary it protects
- which near-term player flow it directly enables

Infrastructure-only work may be necessary, but it should name the player flow it enables and avoid expanding scope without a corresponding core objective.

## Minimum viable validation

Automated validation should begin with rules that are objective and inexpensive:

- quest records include a non-empty player-value statement
- quest records permit decline
- quest rewards use only approved progress or narrative reward types
- scene choices include a refusal, defer, or exit route
- notifications declare shame-free behavior
- retired extraction-oriented terminology is rejected from active content
- reward and completion rules remain structured rather than AI-decided

Human review remains necessary for contextual rules that cannot be safely reduced to string matching, including whether a quest rewards unnecessary disclosure or manipulates consent.

## Success condition

The model is working when the Living Chronicle becomes more useful, the island visibly restores, the player understands what changed, and the person retains control throughout the process.

Engagement without increasing personal utility, understanding, agency, or restoration is not success for Calypso’s Promise.
